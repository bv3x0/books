# The Agony of Eros

## Metadata
- Thesis: The crisis of love today derives not from too many others or unlimited choice but from the erosion of the Other and the narcissification of the Self, as the neoliberal achievement society flattens everything into the consumable Same and eliminates the negativity, atopia, and self-renunciation that eros requires.
- Topics: eros and the Other, narcissism and depression, neoliberal achievement society, positivity and the elimination of negativity, the crisis of theory and fantasy
- Categories: philosophy, social science, literary criticism
- Author: Byung-Chul Han
- Year: 2017
- Collections: Byung-Chul Han
- Mode: quick

## Melancholia

The crisis of love stems from the vanishing of the Other and the mounting narcissification of the Self, so that only the atopic Other—breaking into the 'inferno of the same' in apocalyptic form—can rescue the subject from narcissistic depression, as dramatized in Lars von Trier's film.

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-001"></span>**Against Eva Illouz, the crisis of love derives not from endless freedom of choice and rationalization but from the erosion of the Other and the growing narcissification of the Self, a dramatic and largely unnoticed vanishing of the Other across all spheres of life.**
    - Illouz traces the end of love to the rationalization of love and expanding technologies of choice; Han argues the real corrosive force is the disappearance of the Other, not too many others.
    - Erotic experience presumes the asymmetry and exteriority of the Other; in the 'inferno of the same' that society is becoming, eros cannot exist.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-002"></span>**The Other is atopos—placeless, removed from the language of sameness—and the culture of constant comparison (Ver-Gleichen) flattens everything into the Same, replacing the negativity of atopic otherness with consumable, positive 'heterotopic' differences.**
    - Socrates the lover is called atopos, placeless; 'Being atopic, the Other makes language indecisive: one cannot speak of the Other, about the Other.'
    - The society of the consumer eliminates atopic otherness in favor of positive, consumable difference, because negativity refuses consumption.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-003"></span>**Depression is a narcissistic malady arising from pathologically distorted self-reference: the narcissistic 'achievement-subject' cannot set boundaries against the Other, degrades the Other into a mirror of the One, and suffocates in itself, whereas eros pulls the subject out of itself toward the Other through freely willed self-renunciation.**
    - Eros pulls the subject out of itself toward the Other; depression plunges the subject into itself.
    - Success-driven recognition robs the Other of otherness and ensnares the achievement-subject deeper in the ego, producing success-induced depression; the strength eros brings is 'the gift of the Other,' not the achievement of the One.
* <span class="pull-quote">The crisis of love does not derive from too many others so much as from the erosion of the Other.</span>
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-004"></span>**In Lars von Trier's Melancholia, the death-bringing planet appears as the atopic Other whose apocalyptic arrival heals depression—eros conquers depression—transforming Justine from a depressive into a lover who desires deadly union, echoing Isolde's Liebestod.**
    - Justine writhes nude in the blue light of the approaching planet, awaiting catastrophe as joyous union with her beloved; the prelude from Tristan und Isolde frames the film and recurs in this only erotic scene.
    - The paradox of the name 'Melancholia': the planet heals the melancholic depression by tearing Justine out of the swamp of narcissism, so the atopia of the Other becomes the utopia of eros.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-005"></span>**Von Trier steers the film through classical paintings that stage the proximity of eros and death and the opposition of negativity to sheer positivity, culminating in Justine's gesture of choosing images of love, desire, and death over the abstract void.**
    - Bruegel's Hunters in the Snow induces wintry melancholy; Millais's Ophelia and Caravaggio's David with the Head of Goliath figure the Liebestod; Bruegel's Land of Cockaigne depicts the 'inferno of the same' where even the cactus is made of bread.
    - Justine places Land of Cockaigne beside a Blake illustration of a living slave suspended by the ribs—the invisible violence of positivity against the brutal power of negativity.
    - Von Trier knew Carl Fredrik Hill suffered psychosis and depression; Hill's baying stag stands for Justine's erotic yearning.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-006"></span>**Blanchot's primal childhood scene of the suddenly open, black, empty sky exemplifies the dialectic of the disaster that structures Melancholia: the invasion of the wholly Other unfolds as dispossession (Ent-Eignis) and death, yet fills the subject with 'ravaging joy,' so catastrophic fatality abruptly switches over into salvation.**
    - "the sky, the same sky suddenly open, absolutely black and absolutely empty, revealing… such an absence that all has since always and forevermore been lost." —Maurice Blanchot
    - 'Disaster' literally means 'unlucky star' (desastrum); the disaster is 'withdrawal outside the sidereal abode.'

## Being Able Not to Be Able

The neoliberal achievement society, governed by the modal 'can' rather than 'should,' produces self-exploitation experienced as freedom, and its logic of debt without atonement generates depression; eros, by contrast, is a relationship to the Other beyond ability, requiring the negative capacity of 'being able not to be able.'

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-007"></span>**Achievement society replaces the disciplinary 'should' with the modal 'can,' turning the subject into an entrepreneur of the self who exploits himself of his own free will, so that auto-exploitation—exploitation without domination—proves more efficient and more fatal than allo-exploitation because there is no way to resist oneself.**
    - 'You can' produces massive compulsion that appears as freedom and therefore goes unrecognized; the achievement-subject is perpetrator and victim in one.
    - Foucault views neoliberal Homo oeconomicus positively as enabling civil liberty and 'freedom to be free,' but fails to see the structure of violence underlying the imperative 'Be free.'
    - "I am going to produce what you need to be free. I am going to see to it that you are free to be free." —Michel Foucault
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-008"></span>**Neoliberalism reframes the subject as a self-realizing project rather than a subjugated subject, so whoever fails is personally guilty with no possibility of pardon or atonement; capitalism—contra Benjamin—is not a religion because it works only with debt and default, never relief, making depression a form of 'psychic insolvency.'**
    - Every religion operates with both guilt and pardon, but capitalism offers no atonement to free the debtor from liability, producing both a crisis of debt and a crisis of gratification.
    - 'Insolvency' (from Latin solvere) literally signifies the impossibility of paying off a debt; burnout and depression amount to an unredeemable failure of ability.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-009"></span>**Eros is a relationship to the Other situated beyond achievement, performance, and ability; its negative counterpart is 'being able not to be able' (Nicht-Können-Können), since possessing, grasping, and knowing are synonyms of power that annihilate the Other, and a successful relationship with the Other appears as a kind of failure.**
    - "If one could possess, grasp, and know the other, it would not be other. Possessing, knowing, and grasping are synonyms of power." —Emmanuel Levinas
    - Today love is positivized into sexuality and subjected to a commandment to perform; the Other is sexualized into an object for arousal, fragmented into sexual part-objects, so one can only consume, not love.
* <span class="pull-quote">The exploiter is the exploited.</span>
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-010"></span>**When the Other becomes a sexual object, Buber's 'primal distance' (Urdistanz)—the principle of being-human and condition for alterity—erodes, and the total abolition of remoteness through social media does not create nearness but abolishes it, producing crowding rather than closeness.**
    - Primal distance frees the Other into otherness and makes it possible to address rather than merely call up the Other; a sex object has no 'countenance.'
    - Nearness acts negatively and is inhabited by tension; crowding acts positively and lacks the power to animate.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-011"></span>**The performance principle domesticates love and sexuality into a risk-free formula for enjoyment stripped of the negativity of injury, transgression, and excess, exemplified by Fifty Shades of Grey's contractual 'job offer' with health rules and safewords that eliminate even symbolic dirt.**
    - The 'submissive' must exercise, eat healthy meals, keep herself shaved and waxed, and observe 'hard limits'; even torture is called 'delicious,' with pain made enjoyable rather than the Hegelian negativity of pain.
    - These sadomasochistic diversions lack the negativity of overstepping found in Bataille's erotics of transgression.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-012"></span>**The future is the time of the Other; by positivizing the future into an optimized present and museumizing the past into a repeatable present, society as a 'search engine, a machine for consumption' abolishes the negativity of absence that erotic desire requires.**
    - Levinas reads the caress and pleasure as figures of erotic desire nourished by absence—'a game with something slipping away' reaching for what is vanishing into the future without end.
    - The 'countenance' that at once gives and conceals the Other opposes the 'face' of pornographic nakedness handed over to total visibility; eros is an asymmetrical relationship that interrupts the exchange rate, admitting no bookkeeping.

## Bare Life

Capitalism absolutizes bare life and survival at the expense of the good life, and the modern achievement-subject—like Hegel's slave who clings to bare life and cannot die—is incapable of erotic experience, which requires the negativity of death and self-surrender that Hegel's 'absolute' Spirit and love alone embrace.

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-013"></span>**Erotic communication in antiquity was conceived as injury, disease, and transformation—Ficino calls love 'the most serious disease of all' that changes a man into the nature of another—whereas today's positivized, domesticated love seeks only self-confirmation in the Other.**
    - The boar's 'eroticized teeth' (erotikous odontas) that killed Adonis, and Ficino's 'eroticized eye' (erotikon omma) transmitting blood between the eyes of Lysias and Phaedrus, figure love's deadly, transformative negativity.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-014"></span>**Against Illouz's claim that love is being 'feminized,' Han argues love is being domesticated into a risk-free consumer formula devoid of excess, madness, and transgression, so that the desire for the Other gives way to the dull comfort of the Same and modern love lacks all transcendence.**
    - Illouz cites 'feminine' adjectives (nice, intimate, quiet, peaceful, comfortable, sweet, gentle) for romantic love; Han reframes this as the positivization of all spheres of life eliminating negativity.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-015"></span>**Hegel's master-slave dialectic turns on the 'ability to die': the master risks bare life for freedom and recognition while the slave, fearing death, clings to bare life and becomes a laboring vassal—and today's achievement-subject is the Hegelian slave who exploits himself, being master and slave at once, so the history of freedom is not over.**
    - What proves decisive is not physical superiority but the capacity for death; those without 'freedom unto death' remain 'standing alone within death' rather than following through to the point of death.
    - We are 'enslaved masters or slaves who think themselves masters,' not free human beings; history would only be over if we were neither master nor slave.
* <span class="pull-quote">A survivor is like the undead: too dead to live, and too alive to die.</span>
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-016"></span>**Capitalism absolutizes bare life and survival, directing compulsive accumulation against death as absolute loss and abandoning the teleology of the good life, so its aimless, accelerating growth becomes 'obscene'—a slave's life dominated by the concern to make sure of survival.**
    - "they are preoccupied with living, not with living well." —Aristotle
    - Whatever is merely positive is lifeless; something is alive only insofar as it contains and endures contradiction within itself, so the fitness of bare life lacks the negativity essential to vitality.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-017"></span>**Hegel's 'Absolute' means not violence and totality but love and 'the reconciled return out of another into self'; Spirit is absolute because it looks the negative in the face and tarries with it, reaching a conclusion (Schluss) through death and self-surrender rather than the short-circuit (Kurzschluss) of the restricted spirit.**
    - Schluss means both inference and conclusion/end (from schliessen, to close); life restricted to itself would be a violent short-circuit, while love as absolute end passes through death and returns to itself as the gift of the Other.
    - "The true essence of love consists in giving up the consciousness of oneself, forgetting oneself in another self." —Hegel
    - The depressive-narcissistic subject has no capacity for conclusion; indecision (Unentschlossenheit) is a symptom of depression, and excessive openness has destroyed the capacity to close, conclude, and die.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-018"></span>**For Ficino and Bataille, love means dying in the Other: the power (Macht) of eros implies powerlessness (Ohn-Macht) and self-loss righted by the Other, and eroticism is 'assenting to life up to the point of death,' dissolving the ego's imaginary identity and the symbolic order in a death weightier than the end of bare life.**
    - "Eroticism, it may be said, is assenting to life up to the point of death." —Georges Bataille
    - Erotic desire presupposes a partial dissolution of the discontinuous person and breaks down the regulated social order, opening the way to 'continuity of being.'
    - The power of Eros is distinguished from the violence of Ares; the ruler possesses others through himself, but the lover takes possession of himself through another.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-019"></span>**The Flying Dutchman's ship manned by the undead offers an analogy to burnout society: the Dutchman, unable to live or die, is condemned to perpetual self-exploitation in the inferno of the same and yearns for an apocalypse, while Senta's suicidal Liebestod—an absolute promise and form of conclusion—introduces eternity into time against the additive capitalist economy of production.**
    - "But love, the essence of which is fidelity…, demonstrates how eternity can exist within the time span of life itself. Happiness, in a word!" —Alain Badiou
    - Senta's declaration of love is a decision and conclusion (Schlussform) that transcends the purely additive, accumulative operations of the capitalist economy, bringing forth a 'clearing' in time.

## Porn

Pornography is the antagonist of eros that displays bare life and annihilates even sexuality by eliminating secrecy and distance, and Agamben's 'profanation' thesis—which treats setting-apart as a religious residue to be reopened for use—actually promotes the deritualization and pornographication of the world it fails to recognize.

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-020"></span>**Pornography is 'bare life on display' that annihilates sexuality more effectively than morality, deriving its appeal from the anticipation of dead sex in living sexuality; what is obscene is not an excess of sex but its total absence, and today even real sex is turning into porn.**
    - "Sexuality does not fade into sublimation, repression and morality, but fades much more surely into the more sexual than sex: porn." —Jean Baudrillard
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-021"></span>**Agamben's 'In Praise of Profanation' rests on a secularization thesis—treating museum, tourism, and nudity as secularized forms of temple, pilgrimage, and grace to be reopened for 'new use'—but this blinds him to phenomena that oppose religious practice, since musealization destroys cult value and tourism creates placeless 'non-sites.'**
    - Agamben's cat playing with yarn 'as if it were a mouse' deactivates predatory behavior and opens it for new use, freeing things into 'means without ends.'
    - Pilgrimage is tied to places constituted by history, memory, and identity (Heidegger's dwelling and the 'divine'), whereas tourism's non-sites are places one passes by rather than lingering.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-022"></span>**Contra Agamben, exhibiting nudity as a face loaded with exhibition-value until it bursts does not open a 'new form of erotic communication' but destroys eros, since the erotic is never free of secrecy and a naked face without mystery or expression is obscene and pornographic; capitalism aggravates this by commodifying and displaying everything.**
    - "the farther you go, the closer you come to the lesser secret, the smaller enigma." —Jean Baudrillard
    - Pornographication unfolds as deritualization and desacralization; Baudrillard sets seduction (in the realm of ritual) against love, and Agamben's profanation even promotes deritualization by suspecting ritual spaces of compulsive sequestration.

## Fantasy

Against Illouz's claim that information-rich digital imagination prevents idealization and rationalizes desire, Han argues that fantasy requires the negativity of withdrawal, absence, and undefined space, so that hypervisibility and high-definition information density stifle fantasy and, with the disappearance of thresholds and the Other, produce the agony of eros.

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-023"></span>**Illouz argues that Internet-mediated, 'information-thick' imagination based on an accretion of attributes prevents idealizing the Other, rationalizes desire into conscious selection, and raises expectations, so that disappointment—the 'handmaid of imagination'—increases.**
    - The premodern 'information-thin' imagination overvalued and idealized the love-interest, granting 'surplus value'; the digital subject is made responsible for rationally spelling out desirable criteria in another.
    - Illouz reads Madame Bovary as showing consumerist and romantic desire reinforcing each other, with Emma's fantasy fueling the debts she incurs with the merchant Lheureux.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-024"></span>**Contra Illouz, Emma Bovary's behavior expresses Bataille's shift from restrictive to general economy—unproductive expenditure and the 'principle of loss' negating the bourgeois economy of balanced accounts—so her death is the due consequence of the logic of expenditure, absolute loss being death itself.**
    - Lheureux, a former money changer, stands for the bourgeois economy Emma's wasteful spending negates; the name Lheureux means the petty-bourgeois happiness she renounces.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-025"></span>**Unchecked freedom of choice threatens the end of desire, because desire is always desire for the Other nourished by the negativity of privation and absence, which the positivity of choice destroys; Illouz conflates desire with wanting and needing and fails to grasp its libido-economical particularity.**
    - The Other as object of desire escapes the positivity of choice; today's ego with its 'endless capacity to enunciate and refine criteria in mate selection' does not desire.
* <span class="pull-quote">Only the negativity of withdrawal brings forth the Other in its atopic otherness.</span>
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-026"></span>**Information and fantasy are opposing forces: high-definition imagery eliminates the vagueness and undefined space fantasy inhabits, so it is not information but higher expectations that cause disappointment; the construction of the Other depends not on more or less information but on the negativity of withdrawal, and pornography destroys erotic fantasy by maximizing visual information.**
    - Illouz fails to distinguish fantasy from expectation; new media do not give flight to fantasy but stifle it through high information density.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-027"></span>**Flaubert and Ballard exploit visual privation to rouse erotic fantasy: the most erotic scene in Madame Bovary shows nothing behind the carriage curtains, and Ballard's blinded protagonist destroys his own eyes to withdraw from hypervisibility into the cave of dreams and desire—an anti-Platonic gesture Žižek misreads as a Platonic ascent to the sun.**
    - Flaubert names every square, bridge, and boulevard the aimless carriage passes but depicts none of the lovers' activities; Emma finally scatters scraps of paper like white butterflies.
    - "an eager, unrepentant Oedipus." —J. G. Ballard
    - Žižek claims Maitland stares into the sun to view the scene in its entirety; in truth Maitland retreats from truth and hypervisibility into a twilight space of dreams and desire.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-028"></span>**Hypervisibility, the telos of the transparency society, dismantles thresholds and borders—the zones of mystery where the atopic Other begins—so that fantasies of the Other disappear, producing the contemporary crisis in literature and art, while today's economic walls merely separate rich from poor and generate no Other.**
    - "We photograph things in order to drive them out of our minds. My stories are a way of shutting my eyes." —Kafka
    - Shutting your eyes is negativity and a form of closure incompatible with acceleration society's hyperactivity; contemplation reaches a conclusion, but the rapid succession of hypervisible images leaves no time to close one's eyes.
    - Money makes everything the same and levels essential differences, so capital's new walls exclude rather than constitute thresholds leading somewhere else.

## The Politics of Eros

Eros contains 'something universal' that, guiding the soul and animating thumos, connects love with politics and art through fidelity to the event, so that neoliberalism's replacement of eros with sexuality and pornography depoliticizes society by making communal action and the 'Two scene' of the Other impossible.

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-029"></span>**Plato's doctrine holds that eros contains 'something universal,' rousing the soul toward beauty-in-itself and producing beautiful actions of universal value; eros is not hostile to the senses but stands above desire (epithumia) and spiritedness (thumos), and profaning love into sexuality destroys its universal quality.**
    - Eros governs the soul's three parts—epithumia (pleasure-based desire), thumos (spiritedness/courage), and logos (reason); each has its own mode of enjoying the beautiful.
    - Thumos is rage that breaks with convention and inaugurates a new state of affairs; today it yields to annoyance that lets circumstances persist, and without eros logos degrades into data-driven calculation.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-030"></span>**Thumos is where eros and politics touch, but contemporary neoliberal politics—lacking both thumos and eros and based on epithumia—has degraded into mere work, depoliticizing society by replacing eros with sexuality and pornography and making communal action, a 'we,' impossible among isolated achievement-subjects.**
    - Eros makes thumos bring forth beautiful deeds; in burnout society thumos withers, so political revolt loses its energetic source.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-031"></span>**Following Badiou, love is a 'Two scene' that interrupts the perspective of the One and makes the world arise from the vantage of the Other, marked by the negativity of upheaval and demanding 'fidelity' to the event—a transcendental fidelity that connects love with politics and art as their shared universal quality.**
    - "under the effect of a loving encounter, if I want to be really faithful to it, I must completely rework my ordinary way of 'living' my situation." —Alain Badiou
    - Badiou sees a 'secret resonance' between a life lived under a political idea and the intensity of love, like two different instruments converging in one work.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-032"></span>**Sexuality and pornography lack the negativity of transformation and the Other: the self-identical sexual object never calls me into question, so pornography occupies the 'One scene,' reinforces habituation, and intensifies narcissification through auto-affection, whereas love as event is dehabitualizing and denarcissifying, generating a 'hole' in the order of the Same.**
    - The pornographic image emanates no resistance of the Other or the Real; autoerotic contact protects the ego from being touched by the Other.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-033"></span>**Surrealism reinvents love as an artistic, existential, and political gesture, with Breton ascribing to eroticism a universal power to renew language, existence, and political action—the revolutionary yearning for an entirely different way of loving and another kind of society, faithful to what is yet to come.**
    - "The only art worthy of man and of space, the only one capable of leading him further than the stars… is eroticism." —André Breton

## The End of Theory

Without seduction by the atopic Other, thinking withers into mere data-driven work that only reproduces the Same, so Chris Anderson's claim that big data makes theory obsolete rests on a weak conception of theory and ignores that strong theory, like eros and Socratic logos, is a primary decision founded on the negativity of the incalculable.

* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-034"></span>**Heidegger held that without seduction by the atopic Other, which sparks erotic desire, thinking withers into mere work that reproduces the Same; thinking grows 'more powerful and uncannier' when moved by the beating wings of Eros to venture onto untrodden paths and translate the wordless Other into language.**
    - "The beat of that god's wings moves me every time I take a substantial step in my thinking and venture onto untrodden paths." —Martin Heidegger
    - When thinking is not driven by eros it strays into 'mere sensuality' or 'mere work'; sensuality and work belong to the same spiritless, desireless order.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-035"></span>**Chris Anderson's 'The End of Theory' claims that massively abundant data makes theoretical models superfluous—letting correlation replace causality and 'the numbers speak for themselves'—but this rests on a weak conception of theory that mistakes strong theories like Plato's Ideas or Hegel's phenomenology for replaceable models.**
    - "With enough data, the numbers speak for themselves." —Chris Anderson
    - Strong theory is founded on emphatic thinking; it is a primary, primordial decision that determines what counts, what is or should be, cutting a highly selective narrative clearing through untrodden terrain.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-036"></span>**There is no data-driven thinking, only calculation; thinking is prior and superordinate to the positivity of given data ('things given'), transcending facts through the negativity of the incalculable, so that amid the pullulating mass of information theories are more necessary than ever because they reduce entropy and confer form.**
    - Theories and ceremonies (rituals) share an origin: both confer form on the world and frame things so they do not overflow, while today's mass of information exercises a deformative effect and raises the level of noise.
    - "European literature is threatened. What we're now experiencing in Europe is a crisis of the spirit." —Michel Butor
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-037"></span>**Data-driven positive science produces cognition but not re-cognition or insight: information is additive, cumulative, and inconsequential, whereas insight is a negativity preceded by transformative experience (Erfahrung) capable of shaking up the status quo, so information society, lacking the transforming power of eros, has no access to the wholly Other.**
    - Experiencing (Erlebnis) is additive and cumulative; transformative experience (Erfahrung) often occurs only once—sexuality, as a positive formula for love, is likewise merely additive.
* <span id="the-agony-of-eros-038"></span>**At the very beginning of philosophy, logos and eros entered an intimate relation: Socrates the atopos seduces and intoxicates by words alone like the satyr Marsyas, and because Plato made Poros ('way') the father of Eros, thinking ventures onto uncharted terrain without getting lost—philosophy is the translation of eros into logos.**
    - Alcibiades says Socrates's 'speeches of wisdom' make his heart beat more violently than the corybants' dance and wound him like a snake bite, while Pericles's words leave him unmoved.
    - "Does not the friend reintroduce into thought a vital relationship with the Other that was supposed to have been excluded from pure thought?" —Deleuze and Guattari
    - Eros is called philosophos, 'friend of wisdom'; the lover is a transcendental condition of thought itself, so without eros thinking becomes repetitive and reactive.
