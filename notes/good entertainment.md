# Good Entertainment: A Deconstruction of the Western Passion Narrative

## Metadata
- Thesis: Western culture built a dichotomy between passion (serious, truth-bearing, transcendent art centered on suffering and death) and entertainment (immanent, sensory amusement), but this opposition is untenable: passion and entertainment are secretly kin, and today entertainment has totalized into a new paradigm of world and being.
- Topics: aesthetics of entertainment vs. passion, Western passion narrative and Christianity, Far Eastern affirmation and immanence, music, art, and the theologization of art, totalization of entertainment as new paradigm
- Categories: philosophy, art, music
- Author: Byung-Chul Han
- Year: 2019
- Collections: Byung-Chul Han
- Mode: quick

## Sweet Cross

Through Bach's Saint Matthew's Passion and the Pietist hostility to music, Han argues that the strict divisions between sacred and secular, word and music, passion and pleasure, cannot be maintained, because divine sweetness and sensual sweetness are indistinguishable and Bach's music itself fuses God with lust.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-001"></span>**The 1727 premiere of Bach's Saint Matthew's Passion scandalized Leipzig listeners as operatic and theatrical, revealing a growing hybridization of spiritual and secular music that Pietists and Lutheran Orthodox critics like Christian Gerber tried to resist by subordinating music to the word.**
    - "God help us, children! It is like being at an opera or a comedy!" —a pious widow (per Christian Gerber)
    - Gerber reduces church music to a mere 'ornament' external to worship, a 'seasoning for the senses' comparable to sugar sweetening bitter divine medicine
    - Bach's Thomaskantor contract required music 'not operatic' that 'encourages silent devotion in the listener'
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-002"></span>**The Pietist attempt to safeguard an internal 'word' from external 'music' collapses because both Christ's blood and Cybele's blood are sweet and both inebriate; there is no essential difference between word-drunkenness and music-drunkenness, godly and worldly wine.**
    - Großgebauer condemns the ecstatic Phrygian music of the Cybelian/Dionysian mysteries yet endorses making the spirit 'drunk' like 'sweet wine' through psalms
    - Pietists opposed dancing yet their religious song melodies resembled minuets and dance music
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-003"></span>**Bach's own definitions and title pages trace a movement from music as laudatio Dei toward music 'for the delight of the minds of music-lovers,' placing his work outside the theological context and rendering it manifestly modern; his parody procedure fuses God with pleasure by transforming an aria of satanic lust into a lullaby for the infant Jesus.**
    - Bach omitted Niedt's phrases 'lust' and 'carnal desires' from his thoroughbass definition, suggesting lust is integral to spiritual delight
    - The lust aria from Hercules at the Crossroads is resurrected in the Christmas Oratorio as a lullaby; Saint Matthew's Passion closes not with terror but a sweet lullaby, 'My Jesus, good night!'
    - Plato in Laws forbids mixing hymns and secular music, making law responsible for keeping music pure; Bach violates this mandate
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-004"></span>**Nietzsche's trajectory dramatizes the passion/entertainment divide: the young Nietzsche demands music 'guide our thoughts to things higher,' but the later Nietzsche celebrates 'lightness,' 'gaiety,' and 'African' pop-like music (Offenbach) that requires no redemption, contrasting Wagner's sweating, redemption-obsessed passion.**
    - "What is good is light; whatever is divine moves on tender feet." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Nietzsche contrasts Offenbach's buoyant redemption 'from the incessant demand for redemption' with Wagner's music as a 'sirocco' that makes him sweat

## Butterfly Dreams

Using the nineteenth-century critical war over Rossini, Han argues that the dichotomy of serious art versus entertainment absolutizes a merely relative difference into a moral law, and that a serene, wordless, gratifying music free of passion and truth has its own dignity and depth.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-005"></span>**Critics like Schumann, Hoffmann, and Wagner condemned Rossini as frivolous, popular, effect-seeking entertainment lacking thought, while Schopenhauer and Heine praised him precisely for freeing music from the fetters of words and expression, so his 'own language' needs no words at all.**
    - "the sweet, Rossinian lemonade that art-savorers slurp down without the slightest complaint" —E. T. A. Hoffmann
    - Wagner absolutizes a relative difference into moral law, designating the 'good in art' as a 'moral good' that requires no public, while the bad merely intends 'to please'
    - Crier est tout, chanter n'est rien.
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-006"></span>**Hegel is carried away by Rossini's melodies and declares he lives 'in utopia,' even though his own aesthetics holds that art serving mere pleasure is 'servile' and that free art works toward truth; for Han, this de-passioning of art produces a god of pure entertainment and effect, closely allied with the god of passion and truth.**
    - "I enjoy such beauty, and live in utopia." —G. W. F. Hegel
    - Utopia is 'absolute entertainment'—pure happiness exists only where work comes to rest and meaning is absent, not through knowledge
    - Hegel holds art has become 'a thing of the past,' yielding to philosophy and science as the site of truth and the work of the spirit
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-007"></span>**Nietzsche locates delight in the word's 'beautiful unnaturalness'—the Greek tragic hero who finds eloquent speech at the abyss—but also in its opposite, Rossini's 'la-la-la,' a deviation from natural mute passion; both directions liberate being from work and passion.**
    - "Confronted with the characters in an opera, we are not supposed to take their word for it, but the sound!" —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Art transforms speechless affect into word, abyss into ground, darkness into spiritual brightness—a 'deviation from nature' that is 'the most agreeable repast for human pride'
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-008"></span>**Adorno rigidly divides art (heaven) from popular music (underworld), diagnosing Tchaikovsky's 'ungovernable yearning for joy' as infantile and false because true joy exists 'only in a broken form,' but Han counters that Adorno's colorblind, defiant 'no' is itself an inability to live and that the most beautiful music may ring out in the underworld, as Orpheus's song freed existence from suffering.**
    - "To be entertained means to be in agreement." —Theodor Adorno
    - Orpheus's singing halts all torment—Tantalus, Ixion's wheel, Sisyphus's stone—so that work comes to rest and the air is cleared of passion
    - For Han, Adorno's claim that 'every joy is illusory' is itself an illusion born of brokenness

## On Luxury

Against Wagner's theory that authentic art springs from natural need and that luxury is degenerate un-nature, Han argues that all culture and beauty originate in a 'luxation' or deviation from nature, so that luxury—the superfluous—is not a failure of spirit but an enhancement of its vitality and the very element in which the soul flourishes.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-009"></span>**Wagner turns nature into ideology, condemning Rossini as a 'man of luxury' who abstracts perfume from flowers, but Han argues culture itself is precisely this abstraction and luxation of nature; perfumed silk flowers do not wilt, and luxury postpones death, an event of nature.**
    - Wagner opposes the 'flower of the people' to Rossini's 'artificial growth,' yet the 'natural tree of the people' is an oxymoron since the flower in the field is not yet culture
    - Nietzsche saw in Wagner himself 'a passionate longing for luxury and splendor,' a 'mad, needless need' springing from delusion, which Wagner must thank for his art
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-010"></span>**Genuine human need is not basic or simple like animal hunger but an unnatural 'needless need'; the intensification of pleasure demands denaturing, the soul flourishes only in superfluity, and even Kant classes acumen, wit, and contemplation as a 'luxury of minds' arising not from need but from a luxation of it.**
    - "The penchant for luxury is rooted in man's depths: it reveals as superfluous and immoderate the waters where his soul prefers to swim." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Kant likens wit to nature which 'appears in her flowers to be at play, whereas in her fruits she seems rather to be at work'—knowledge is the fruit of thought in flower, not of need
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-011"></span>**Even Adorno, a man of knowledge, laments the disappearance of luxury under 'rampant technology,' since true joy springs from excess and the luxation of the necessary, bringing pure amusement—free of all restraint—close to art as its complementary extreme.**
    - Adorno mourns the faded splendor of the train bleu's voluptuous travel, lost to the express train's efficiency
    - "Amusement, free of all restraint, would be not only the opposite of art but its complementary extreme." —Theodor Adorno

## Satori

Han argues that the Western dichotomies (mind/senses, transcendence/immanence) grounding the concept of serious passion-art never developed in the Far East, where haiku, ukiyo-e, and Zen are affirmative, immanent, entertaining, and playful—oriented wholly to the here and now rather than to truth, depth, or redemption.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-012"></span>**Western interpreters like Barthes wrongly read haiku as a spiritual attempt to halt language and reach Zen satori, missing that haiku is chatty, ludic, witty, and entertaining—'playful verse' descended from comic communal linked-verse composed to amuse, too brief for any passion narrative.**
    - Barthes sees haiku as 'attaining to that anterior shore of language,' a metaphysics without subject or god, whereas in Japan it is rarely seen as an earnest attempt to end the soul's babble
    - Haikai means 'comic'; haiku is the seventeen-syllable hokku of communal haikai no renga, undertaken for entertainment with spontaneity and the 'cutting word'
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-013"></span>**Far Eastern art is affirmative and immanent rather than negative and transcendent: it embodies no utopian counterworld, no passion for truth, no aesthetic distance, because Far Eastern thinking is built on complementary correspondences rather than dichotomous oppositions.**
    - Ukiyo-e ('images of the floating world') was mass-produced entertainment of the Edo pleasure quarter—actor portraits, erotica, humor—letting surfaces blaze without hermeneutic depth
    - Cézanne conceives art as a 'priesthood' demanding 'men of purity' and says 'I want to die painting'—a characteristically European passion foreign to ukiyo-e
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-014"></span>**Far Eastern religions—Taoism and Zen Buddhism—teach radical affirmation of the transitory here-and-now rather than redemption from it; satori, expressed as loud laughter, is akin to pure amusement that laughs away every form of passion, since there is no other world, no transcendence to escape to.**
    - "When hungry, eat your rice; when tired, close your eyes. Fools may laugh at me, but wise men will know what I mean." —Zen Master Linji
    - Yao-shan laughed so heartily at the moon appearing behind clouds that the laugh echoed ninety li east of Li-yang
    - Enjoyment implies affirmation—a consent different from Adorno's, which merely lets the false world persist—because everydayness itself is redemption's place

## Moral Entertainment

Against Kant's view that morality is duty, pain, and passion (a via doloris opposed to inclination and enjoyment), Han argues—via La Mettrie and the eighteenth-century moral weeklies—that morality can and must be sweet on its own, working through entertaining narrative that habituates norms into inclinations and stabilizes society.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-015"></span>**For Kant morality is necessitation, intellectual constraint, and pain—a via doloris toward saintliness—because reason must dominate the 'impetuous importunity of inclinations,' but Han argues this antagonism is not natural Enlightenment thought but a residuum of Christian morals produced by the sharp cut cleaving reason from the senses.**
    - La Mettrie, in The Highest Good, denies any contradiction between happiness and virtue, holding that morality leads to happiness and 'moral enjoyment is not an oxymoron'
    - Kant keeps 'squinting' toward happiness, calling on God to distribute happiness 'in exact proportion to morality,' thereby speculating with happiness by mortgaging it for bliss
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-016"></span>**The eighteenth-century moral weeklies (Der Patriot, Der Bürger, Der Glückselige) show a more human Enlightenment in which the path of virtue is agreeable, morality produces enduring pleasure, and happiness is a socially mediated, intersubjective phenomenon rather than infantile wish-fulfillment.**
    - "The path of virtue is not so harsh and austere as many believe." —the Patriot (1724–1726)
    - Entertainment value arises from the dialectical tension of infraction and restoration of moral order—transgression followed by punishment—which stabilizes and internalizes norms into inclinations, as with the Struwwelpeter
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-017"></span>**Even Kant's doctrine of method admits moral entertainment as a 'game of judgment' in which people take pleasure in appraising the moral worth of actions, but he confines it to disinterested reflection on concepts and duty, blinding himself to how prereflexive stories, myths, and inclinations more effectively bind norms to everyday self-evidence.**
    - "Entertainment re-impregnates what one already is." —Niklas Luhmann
    - Kant insists moral education work on concepts not feelings and warns against setting noble 'heroes of romance' before children, whereas Hoffmann held that 'absolute truth' and geometric sentences leave a child's soul to atrophy
    - Stories do not argue; they please and rouse through narrative suspense—the essence of myth—making them more effective than moral imperatives or reason

## Healthy Entertainment

Han shows that Kant relegates entertainment and laughter to mere animal-bodily sensation promoting bodily health while denying it any cognitive value, but argues that entertainment is 'healthy' in a deeper sense—it stabilizes social relations by codifying and internalizing norms through its overlooked cognitive and interpretive structure.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-018"></span>**Kant classifies entertainment (games of chance, harmony, wit, laughter) as mere agreeable sensation belonging to 'animal, i.e. bodily sensation,' distinct from the reflective judgment of the beautiful and the transcendent-oriented feeling of the sublime; its gratification arises from the oscillation of the viscera and diaphragm, comparable to Eastern massage.**
    - Laughter is 'strained expectation being suddenly reduced to nothing,' setting bodily organs in oscillation that restores equilibrium and benefits health, not thought
    - The sublime provokes reluctance-and-pleasure that elevates the subject toward the supersensible, whereas entertainment's mere thrill fails to 'expand' the soul and remains animal gratification
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-019"></span>**Against Kant, Han argues laughter is a cognitive event: incited by deviation from norms, it restores and reinforces them, and laughter at others always implies a feeling of superiority and self-confirmation, so entertainment operates on the reflective plane and infiltrates the cognitive layer under the pretext of amusement.**
    - When deviations provoke laughter, norms are reconfirmed; entertainment codifies what is and must be, favoring the internalization of norms and stabilizing social relations
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-020"></span>**Kant subordinates health to the teleology of reason—health is only 'good' when reason directs it to ends—and elevates pain to a life-principle, making work a via doloris and reason itself a 'panacea' that prevents illness, so that reason and entertainment converge at the point of health.**
    - "To feel one's life, to enjoy oneself, is thus nothing more than to feel oneself continuously driven to leave the present state." —Immanuel Kant
    - Pain holds deadly lifelessness at bay and spurs progress toward the better; absolute contentment would be the standstill of all incentives, like the stopping of the heart
    - In The Conflict of the Faculties Kant claims moral-practical philosophy has an immediate physical effect that can overpower a serious attack of gout

## Being as Passion

Han reads Heidegger's Being and Time as a hidden phenomenology of entertainment: the 'they,' idle talk, distraction, and curiosity that unburden Dasein and keep it at home in the present are cast as inauthentic falling-prey, opposed to being as passion toward death—but Heidegger's willful, value-laden language of passion arbitrarily excludes and lacks serenity before the world.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-021"></span>**Heidegger's condemnation of distraction as a 'flight from death' has religious roots (Pascal) and casts entertainment—the 'they,' idle talk, curiosity—as inauthentic falling-prey that unburdens being and holds Dasein at home in the present, whereas authentic existence is passion toward death, individuation, and heroic resoluteness.**
    - Entertainment is an 'unburdening of being' that generates pleasure by reproducing an already-interpreted world; the they is sustained insofar as it is entertained, disburdening judgment and understanding
    - Idle talk (public communicative ground) is opposed to the 'passionate form of talk' that is 'keeping silent'; entertainment is homekeeping while Angst leaves Dasein homeless and drives it to passion
    - "In this inconspicuousness and unascertainability, the they unfolds its true dictatorship." —Martin Heidegger
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-022"></span>**Heidegger grasps work as the basic form of human existence that alone opens access to the real, factical world (the gravity of mountains, hardness of primeval rock), while media—radio, television, film—de-factify the world into weightless, ghostly representations that give 'the illusion of a world that is no world.'**
    - "It is the work alone that opens up space for the reality that is these mountains." —Martin Heidegger
    - Television viewing is passive channel-surfing curiosity—'not-staying'—that ontologically disperses Dasein in inauthentic being-in-the-world and looks into the distance only to consume outward appearance
    - Those who stay home yet are 'chained to radio and television' are more homeless than the driven-out; only 'a god can save us' from technological machinations
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-023"></span>**For Heidegger thinking and work are themselves passion (passio, suffering, passivity in self-surrender to what cannot be thought in advance), and his thinker is a man of pain whose 'rift of pain' opens human thinking to the superhuman—pain is transcendence, pain is god, while entertainment is immanence and godlessness.**
    - "To be an echo is the suffering of thinking. Its passion is quiet sobriety." —Martin Heidegger
    - Heidegger's last things—'crown and cross'—recall the crown of thorns of Homo doloris; his refactification of the world proceeds through rhymes, half-rhymes, and alliteration
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-024"></span>**Kafka and Handke share Heidegger's dread of the de-factifying media world—letters and telegraphs as ghosts that drink up written kisses and make people blind to reality—but Han faults Heidegger's language of passion as willful, achieving its effect through crude selection and exclusion, lacking serenity and friendliness toward a world that also includes mice and Mickey Mouse.**
    - "Written kisses never arrive at their destination; the ghosts drink them up along the way." —Franz Kafka
    - Handke seeks 'repaysement' in the mild resistance and material gravity of a Serbian shop door, yet his manipulated storybook language generates a world just as unreal as the media world
    - Heidegger constructs the authentic world through an arbitrary distinction (book vs. film, jug vs. device); what he lacks is serenity before the world

## A Hunger Artist

Reading Kafka's letters and 'A Hunger Artist,' Han argues that writing-as-passion is a via doloris toward death yet is itself a sweet, self-indulgent pleasure, so that the hunger artist's art of negation and the hedonistic panther that replaces him are not fundamentally different—both are bound in their depths by the imperative to happiness.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-025"></span>**For Kafka writing is passion and suffering—a 'reward for serving the devil,' a life turned toward death in which he only 'lights up his corpse' and must hold to his desk with his teeth to keep madness at bay—yet it is also a sweet reward and a construct of sensuality and self-indulgence, revealing the writer as both Homo doloris and Homo delectionis.**
    - "Writing is a sweet and wonderful reward, but for what? ... it is the reward for serving the devil." —Franz Kafka
    - Max Brod ties happiness and writing to the same thread, whereas Kafka flees happiness to write, swearing off happiness for the higher pleasure of writing
    - The writer is a martyr who takes up mankind's cross, making it 'possible for men to enjoy sin without guilt'—the imperative to sensuality binds writer and humanity
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-026"></span>**The hunger artist's fasting is an art of negativity that says no to all that is, yet this negation is not pure suffering but the basis of his happiness—he fasts because he 'couldn't find the food I liked'—and his passion for hunger, subordinated to the dictates of publicity, dies out in an age with waning interest in any passion whatsoever.**
    - "Because I couldn't find the food I liked. If I had found it, believe me, I should have made no fuss and stuffed myself like you or anyone else." —the hunger artist
    - The forty-day fasting limit is fixed by the impresario for advertising, so publicity takes precedence over passion; the passion for hunger functions as entertainment obeying the dictates of publicity
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-027"></span>**The young panther that replaces the hunger artist embodies happiness without passion—sovereign joy of life streaming from its throat, freedom lurking in its jaws—and Han argues its digestive hedonistic happiness is no more illusory than the freedom of negation, so both circus figures are bound in their depths by the imperative to happiness.**
    - The well-fed panther brings relief to all—'even the most insensitive felt it refreshing'—and amusement-seekers crowd the cage, identifying with its complacent joy of life
    - Both the hedonistic animal and the hunger artist live out their existence in captivity, which does not exclude happiness but perhaps takes it as a given

## Serenity before the World

Contrasting George Steiner's theologization of art-as-transcendence with Robert Rauschenberg's art of immanence, Han argues for a third relationship to the world—serene friendliness that excludes nothing—which escapes both the art of passion and the art of entertainment through a deviation from the ego toward the world.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-028"></span>**George Steiner conceives art as transcendence, metaphysics, and religion—a 'shining through' of otherness with an 'aura of terror'—making the artist Homo doloris whose seriousness derives from heroic being-toward-death, while entertainment is immanent, ephemeral, and doomed; Adorno stands near Steiner yet recognizes that entertainment freed toward passion bends toward art.**
    - "there is a shining through" —George Steiner
    - For Han neither the theological elevation of art nor the theological liberation of entertainment has serenity—pure sense and pure nonsense converge in hysteria
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-029"></span>**Robert Rauschenberg's art of immanence practices serenity before the world: excluding death imagery, nourishing innocence daily, falling in love with an old can, keeping the television and windows open to de-internalize and dispassion the ego by deflecting it toward the world and its things.**
    - "Innocence you have to nourish every day." —Robert Rauschenberg
    - Leaving the television on de-internalizes the ego and deflects it toward the world, an instrument of primordial distraction; less ego means less world, and less fear means greater serenity
    - Rauschenberg rejects the 'passion for truth' of Barnett Newman, who defines art's task as wresting 'truth from the void'
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-030"></span>**Rauschenberg's art of friendliness offers a 'friendly reception' to the world and its things—inclusion not exclusion—taking only part in the world alongside television as another window onto it, thereby opening an everydayness that escapes both the highly selective art of passion and the blindness of entertainment.**
    - "there is a desire to not shut anything out. Everything has to be brought into it—inclusion and not exclusion." —Barbara Rose / Robert Rauschenberg
    - Only death, crept closer after fifteen years, provokes unease: if all the televisions gave out 'that would be almost like death'

## A Metatheory of Entertainment

Han argues that present-day entertainment is not merely an eighteenth-century leisure phenomenon or an episodic game (contra Luhmann) but is totalizing into a new paradigm—a hypersystem coextensive with the world whose binary code entertaining/not-entertaining determines what is real and true.

* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-031"></span>**Attempts to define entertainment historically (as an eighteenth-century product of the work/leisure distinction) or as a leisure activity that kills free time miss the historical particularity of today's phenomenon, since entertainment always existed and now oversteps free time entirely through hybrids like edutainment, labotainment, and theotainment.**
    - Greeks played the lyre and ball games and medieval residences sheltered jesters, so the claim they lacked entertainment for want of a work/leisure split makes little sense
    - The ubiquity of entertainment expresses a totalization that dissolves the work/free-time division; cognitainment opposes knowledge-as-passion by marrying knowledge and entertainment
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-032"></span>**Luhmann's systems theory, which subsumes entertainment under mass media as an episodic game framed off from 'real reality' (the cat jumping onto the chessboard), fails to grasp entertainment's ubiquity, because entertainment is becoming chronic rather than episodic, attaching to every social system and eroding the boundary between fictional and real reality.**
    - "The cat jumps onto the chessboard." —Niklas Luhmann
    - Edutainment transcends mass media and belongs to pedagogy; the binary code information/non-information is too vague since information is constitutive of all communication
    - Now there is no difference between cat and chessboard—even the cat is subordinate to the game, becoming a mere toy
* <span id="good-entertainment-a-deconstruction-of-the-western-033"></span>**Entertainment has been raised to a new paradigm and formula of world and being: to be, to belong to the world, one must be entertaining; a hypersystem coextensive with the world arises whose binary code entertaining/not-entertaining determines what is real, so that reality itself becomes an effect of entertainment.**
    - Only the entertaining is real or true; the distinction between fictional and real reality that Luhmann clings to is no longer relevant
    - Kafka's hunger artist (passion) and his hedonistic beast (entertainment) inhabit the same cage, 'trading places until the end of time'—passion and entertainment are secretly kin
