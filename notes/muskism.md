# Muskism: An Operating System for the Twenty-First Century

## Metadata
- Thesis: Elon Musk is not merely an eccentric billionaire but the avatar of a coherent worldview called 'Muskism,' which offers sovereignty through technology as its central promise—fusing with the state rather than escaping it, delivering autonomy for some through expulsion of others, and accelerating the merger of humans with machines while hardening racial and class hierarchies.
- Topics: techno-sovereignty and state symbiosis, fortress futurism and apartheid South Africa, attention alchemy and meme economics, cybernetic collectives and the woke mind virus, AI, automation, and the far-right cyborg synthesis
- Categories: political science, biography & autobiography, social science

- Year: 2025
- Mode: quick

### Introduction: An Operating System for the Twenty-First Century

Rather than analyzing Musk as an individual, the authors frame him as the symptom and avatar of 'Muskism'—a coherent worldview that offers sovereignty through technology as its central promise, combining state symbiosis with expulsion of outsiders, and positioning itself as a possible operating system for the twenty-first century analogous to Fordism for the twentieth.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-001"></span>**Muskism is better understood as a systemic worldview than as the expression of one man's personality, just as Fordism was more than Henry Ford—it named a total configuration of capitalism built on mass production and mass consumption.**
    - A century ago, Henry Ford's best-selling memoir prompted the coinage of 'Fordism' as shorthand for twentieth-century capitalism; the authors apply the same analytical move to Musk.
    - Unlike Fordism's broad distribution of rising living standards, Muskism concentrates rewards while selling the fantasy of self-reliance through dependence on Musk's own infrastructures.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-002"></span>**Muskism's core promise—techno-sovereignty—is paradoxical: what is sold as self-reliance is actually entry into a walled garden controlled by Musk, making clients dependent on the very infrastructure they believe liberates them.**
    - The Pentagon, NASA, Ukrainian forces, and rural broadband consumers all depend on SpaceX or Starlink, meaning that trying to unplug from Musk reveals he owns the socket.
    - Musk sells techno-sovereignty to both states and individuals, positioning his infrastructure as indispensable for energy independence, battlefield communication, and disaster resilience.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-003"></span>**Muskism has a dual face: techno-sovereignty for some and expulsion for others, with its vision of a purified community defined by cultural and genetic membership in a white European West defended by superior technology.**
    - Empathy for fellow humans is described by Musk as an 'exploit'—a software vulnerability manipulated by bad actors to push the West toward 'civilizational suicide.'
    - "Suicidal empathy is like an autoimmune disease, the body attacks itself." —Elon Musk
* <span class="pull-quote">If Fordism was the operating system of the twentieth century, we contend that Muskism offers a possible operating system for the twenty-first.</span>
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-004"></span>**Musk is widely misread as a libertarian who despises government, but the reality is inverted: he has built his empire through deep fusion with the state, and his apparent provocations—from Mars talk to trolling—are functional instruments rather than personality quirks.**
    - Mars colonization functions not as a serious exit plan but as leverage—a bargaining chip for further techno-sovereigntist pursuits with state partners.
    - Trolling is infrastructure: every joke and poll is a stress test of responsiveness, measuring the elasticity of attention and the bandwidth of belief.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-005"></span>**Muskism's financial architecture is structurally fragile because Musk's personal wealth is almost entirely held in the stock of his own companies, making the perpetual inflation of belief in exponential future growth a survival necessity rather than mere boosterism.**
    - "If Tesla and SpaceX went bankrupt, I would go bankrupt too immediately." —Elon Musk
    - Like Henry Ford, whose wealth was also almost entirely concentrated in Ford stock held privately until after his death, Musk must sustain the bubble to sustain himself.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-006"></span>**Muskism is well suited to the current historical conjuncture—declining trust in institutions, rising far-right movements, deglobalization, and techno-maximalism—meaning it could provide the foundation for a new social settlement if the current institutional breakdown continues.**
    - The intensifying rivalry between the United States and China, the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and the collapse of international law norms all create conditions where Muskism's promise of sovereignty through technology resonates.
    - Muskism's fascination with cyborgs accords with the techno-maximalism of a political and business elite that finds every occasion to digitize our lives more deeply, most recently under the guise of artificial intelligence.

## Part One: Foundation

### Fortress Futurism

Musk's childhood in apartheid South Africa shaped the foundational logic of Muskism—'fortress futurism,' the belief that technology can strengthen self-reliance in a hostile world—by immersing him in a state that combined white-supremacist racial hierarchy with modernizing technocracy, mass surveillance, and a siege mentality that treated the entire society as a launchpad for a privileged few.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-007"></span>**Musk's maternal grandfather Joshua Haldeman was a member of Technocracy Incorporated, a movement that envisioned a continental superstate run by engineers as a self-sufficient fortress against the global financial system—an ideological prototype for Muskism's techno-sovereigntist ambitions.**
    - Technocracy, founded by American engineer Howard Scott in 1933, proposed replacing currency with energy units called 'ergs' and replacing democratic capitalism with the dictatorship of engineers.
    - Haldeman was attracted to apartheid South Africa partly because of its racial separatism, telling a pro-apartheid newspaper that the government's attitude 'encouraged me to come and settle here.'
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-008"></span>**Apartheid South Africa was not merely a racial state but a modernizing technocratic project that used IBM mainframes, nuclear weapons, and imported auto factories to enforce racial hierarchy and pursue economic self-sufficiency—what the authors call 'racial Fordism.'**
    - South Africa developed operational nuclear warheads by 1982 using uranium from gold mining, with expertise from American and Israeli scientists, making it the ultimate 'poisonous shrimp'—indigestible to its enemies.
    - IBM mainframes powered the 'Book of Life' identification system that recorded the racial classification of every citizen, making apartheid what one historian calls a 'biometric state.'
    - Political scientist Stephen Gelb named the South African system 'racial Fordism': mass consumption reserved as a privilege for the white minority while mass production relied on Black labor.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-009"></span>**The personal computer gave the young Musk a portal out of apartheid South Africa's closed, provincial society and introduced him to the cyborg consciousness of total immersion in code—the foundational experience behind his later obsession with merging humans and machines.**
    - Musk acquired a Commodore VIC-20 at a Johannesburg mall and stayed awake for three days learning BASIC, programming his first product—a video game called Blastar—which he sold for $500 to a magazine.
    - He demonstrated a modem to his father, explaining it let him communicate with a computer at Oxford University, an aperture out of a closed society that would define his sense of what technology could do.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-010"></span>**Japanese anime series like Transformers and Robotech, broadcast in South Africa in the 1980s, gave Musk a formative obsession with the 'mech'—the human-machine fusion—that would directly shape his later ventures into humanoid robots and brain-computer interfaces.**
    - Musk named Tesla's humanoid robot 'Optimus' after Optimus Prime, the leader of the Autobots in Transformers, and in 2018 tweeted to his followers 'It is time to create a mecha.'
    - The mech embodied the siege logic of fortress futurism: an advanced technology originally possessed by the enemy that could be turned against the enemy, analogous to apartheid South Africa's nuclear weapons program.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-011"></span>**Musk left South Africa at seventeen to avoid mandatory military service—which he described as 'suppressing Black people' and serving in 'a fascist army'—using a Canadian passport obtained through his mother and financial support from his father, a departure that brought the residue of apartheid thinking with him.**
    - Upper-class white South African life habituated Musk to floating above a 'largely faceless class of laborers,' whose invisibility as 'non-player characters' would furnish one of his favorite later terms of abuse.
    - "As South African journalist Eve Fairbanks notes, apartheid taught a dual lesson: that 'certain classic power hierarchies ought to be maintained and that those maintaining them deserve to be seen as disruptors, even misunderstood victims.'" —Eve Fairbanks

### The Superset

Musk's time in Silicon Valley during the dot-com boom of the 1990s taught him that the real opportunity lay not in escaping the state but in grafting onto it as a source of power and profit, while the internet's transformation into a commercial superset revealed that whoever controlled the code would have dominion over everything it subsumed.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-012"></span>**Silicon Valley's core lesson for Musk was not libertarian exit from the state but 'state symbiosis': the government's guarantees and publicly funded infrastructure serve as scaffolding for private accumulation, a principle Musk first grasped as a college intern examining Brady Bonds.**
    - While interning at Scotiabank in 1991, Musk tried to convince his boss to buy Brady Bonds, explaining that the point was that the securities were 'fucking backed by Uncle Sam'—the government as guarantor, not obstacle.
    - His first company, Zip2, rested entirely on publicly funded infrastructure: GPS data from military satellites and internet access from the newly privatized government network, both acquired at zero cost.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-013"></span>**The dot-com boom was structurally enabled by the federal government's privatization of the internet in April 1995 and by the financialization of tech through venture capital, establishing the model where companies raised billions on imagined future profits rather than current earnings—what the authors call 'financial fabulism.'**
    - The Netscape IPO on August 9, 1995—the company hadn't turned a profit and mostly gave away its software—doubled on day one to a valuation of $2.3 billion, establishing that the internet was an asset class.
    - Musk demonstrated the value of financial fabulism at Zip2 by building a big case around a normal PC to make investors think his software ran on a supercomputer: 'The investors thought that was impressive,' recalled Kimbal.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-014"></span>**X.com, Musk's second internet company, aimed to make the entire financial system—savings, checking, loans, mortgages, insurance—into a single website, but after merging with Peter Thiel's Confinity in 2000, Musk was ousted in a power struggle and the company was rebranded PayPal.**
    - PayPal brought 'frictionless' ease to online transactions, functioning as what one journalist called 'the perfect lubricant' for e-commerce, and its co-founders including Thiel and David Sacks had grown up in apartheid South Africa where neither capital nor information moved freely.
    - The 'PayPal Mafia'—including Thiel, Sacks, and Roelof Botha—would go on to shape Silicon Valley's next decade, with Thiel co-founding Palantir and Sacks later becoming Trump's AI czar.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-015"></span>**Peter Thiel's philosophy—that monopoly is the goal of every business, that 'severely unequal distributions' are the law of the universe, and that innovative companies 'resemble feudal monarchies'—crystallized the Silicon Valley doctrine that Muskism absorbed wholesale, though Musk differed in believing the state should be fused with rather than privatized away.**
    - Competition and capitalism are opposites, Thiel argued, since capitalism is about accumulation of capital and monopoly firms can set their own prices, make long-term plans, and finance ambitious research.
    - Thiel believed technology was creating conditions for escaping the state—describing PayPal as 'a new world currency, free from all government control'; Musk's concept of the internet superset was instead a colonizing metaphor, not a secessionist one.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-016"></span>**Musk's 1998 prediction that the internet would become 'the superset of all media'—absorbing older forms while adding interactivity—was a theory of power disguised as technological description: whoever owned the superset would have dominion over its subsets, a colonizing logic that would guide Muskism's subsequent expansion.**
    - In software, a superset contains the features of an earlier language but adds new capabilities; Musk believed the internet would assimilate older media while introducing interactivity as a new capacity for control.
    - Marc Andreessen's later famous declaration that 'software is eating the world' was an elaboration of what Musk had already formulated in 1998 as the internet superset.

### Sovereignty as a Service

SpaceX transformed Muskism's logic of state symbiosis into a new paradigm—sovereignty-as-a-service—in which a private company absorbs powers formerly reserved for sovereign entities, becoming so dominant in the launch market that states must purchase their own sovereignty back in increments, a process enabled by the post-9/11 militarization and privatization of the security state.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-017"></span>**SpaceX was born at the intersection of two convergent forces: Musk's intuition that space was the next publicly funded business opportunity (analogous to DARPA's role in creating the internet), and the post-9/11 Rumsfeld Doctrine that sought to modernize the military by outsourcing functions to private entrepreneurs.**
    - At Stanford in 2003, Musk stated explicitly that just as DARPA underwrote the internet's costs initially, NASA had done the same for space technologies, and bringing in commercial free enterprise would produce the same dramatic acceleration.
    - The TacSat program, launched in May 2003 by Arthur Cebrowski's Office of Force Transformation, sought small affordable satellites deployable on battlefield timelines—and its first launch contract went to SpaceX, only a year old.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-018"></span>**Musk imported Silicon Valley's 'Agile' software methodology into aerospace manufacturing, combining fail-fast iteration with radical vertical integration to create a hybrid he practiced as 'lean Fordism'—centralizing production to accelerate it, in direct opposition to the outsourcing consensus of the early 2000s.**
    - "Air Force Brigadier General Pete Worden was struck by SpaceX engineers working like 'a bunch of kids in Silicon Valley would do software—they would stay up all night and try this and try that.'" —Pete Worden
    - Frustrated with high prices from aerospace suppliers, Musk brought production in-house so that 'if we didn't make stuff ourselves, we would be beholden to those legacy costs,' eventually aiming to crank out rockets on an assembly line.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-019"></span>**Musk's 2004 legal challenge to a no-bid NASA contract for competitor Kistler Aerospace—which he won through the Government Accountability Office—directly pushed NASA to create the COTS fixed-price contracting program, which provided a $278 million lifeline to SpaceX and restructured government aerospace contracting in ways that systematically favored Musk.**
    - COTS used a 'fixed-price' model rather than the cost-plus arrangement that had dominated aerospace since World War Two, incentivizing the relentless cost reduction that Musk implemented in his factories.
    - "'Absent that, you could debate whether SpaceX would have survived or not,' remarked James Maser, SpaceX's president at the time, about the $278 million COTS award." —James Maser
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-020"></span>**Starlink—which by 2025 comprised over 8,000 satellites accounting for two-thirds of all active satellites—transformed SpaceX from a launch provider into a global platform for national projects, but the Russian invasion of Ukraine revealed the geopolitical risks of private infrastructure ownership when Musk reportedly disabled service to prevent a Ukrainian attack on the Russian Navy.**
    - Starlink became indispensable for Ukrainian battlefield coordination after 2022 and spawned Starshield, a $1.8 billion military version providing encrypted communications, reconnaissance, and missile detection—the fulfillment of the old Star Wars dream.
    - When the Polish foreign minister warned his country might seek other suppliers if Starlink was unreliable, Musk replied 'Be quiet, small man'—when a single individual owns the infrastructure, he doesn't need to run a government to shape geopolitics.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-021"></span>**SpaceX's conquest of 95 percent of U.S. orbital launches and more than half of global launches produced a new kind of monopoly power—sovereignty-as-a-service—in which states cannot exercise their authority without purchasing access from a private provider, reversing the Cold War model where contractors built to government specifications under government supervision.**
    - Spain called its SpaceX satellite launch a step toward 'strategic sovereignty,' illustrating how Muskism reframes dependency as empowerment—sovereignty is real but conditional on the terms of service.
    - The wager of Muskism is that sovereignty, going forward, will be infrastructural before it is territorial—defined by access to bandwidth, compute, launch cadence, and orbital real estate as much as by borders and bureaucracies.

### Electric Autonomy

Tesla's success was enabled by three convergent forces—Obama-era green capitalism, Silicon Valley's financial dominance, and China's rise—and Musk leveraged all three to build an ecosystem that sells electric autonomy as national energy independence and personal resilience, adapting the fortress futurism of apartheid South Africa into an industrial form perfectly suited to a deglobalizing world.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-022"></span>**Tesla's survival depended on an $465 million federal loan from the Department of Energy in 2009, regulatory credits from California's zero-emission vehicle program (which at peak supplied roughly 40 percent of Tesla's net income), and a $100,000 tax credit for EV buyers—making it a textbook case of the 'entrepreneurial state' enabling private innovation.**
    - Tesla's chief lobbyist Diarmuid O'Connell, a former State Department official who read casualty reports from Iraq and Afghanistan every morning, came to Tesla because dependence on foreign oil seemed to him to be the root cause of the wars.
    - The New York Times wondered whether the federal loan should be called 'the 2008 Bailout of Very, Very High-Net-Worth Individuals Who Invested in Tesla Motors Act,' but Tesla's defenders argued that starting with a luxury model was a deliberate strategy to shed the stigma of the EV as a golf cart.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-023"></span>**The lithium-ion battery was Tesla's central technological challenge: by 2013, the company's chief technology officer calculated that running the Fremont factory at full capacity would require the entire global output of lithium-ion batteries, forcing Musk to build his own supply through the Gigafactory rather than rely on global markets.**
    - Tesla's early battery sourcing—buying cells from Japan, assembling packs in Thailand, shipping to Palo Alto—was standard operating procedure for early-2000s American manufacturers following the outsourcing consensus.
    - The Nevada Gigafactory, which won the site competition over other states partly because Nevada has no corporate income tax, is a right-to-work state, and has looser labor and environmental regulations than California, opened in 2016 producing lithium-ion batteries at scale.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-024"></span>**Musk built a geoeconomic strategy that served both the United States and China by manufacturing Teslas inside China (jumping tariff walls via a Shanghai Gigafactory financed with $1.6 billion in Chinese bank loans) and inside the EU (via a Berlin Gigafactory)—positioning himself to win regardless of which superpower gained the upper hand in the deglobalizing world order.**
    - China's 'big green bang'—dominated by battery firm CATL, which outpaced Tesla's Nevada output within years of the Gigafactory's opening—was tied to a broader push for strategic self-reliance through Made in China 2025 and the Belt and Road Initiative.
    - "French finance minister Bruno Le Maire told the Financial Times that 'Mobility is a matter of sovereignty' in reference to European battery efforts—illustrating how Musk's electric autonomy pitch resonated with national projects across the political spectrum." —Bruno Le Maire
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-025"></span>**Tesla's shift from clean-energy optimism to fortress survivalism—embodied in the Cybertruck, the Powerwall, and Musk's 'prepper' pitch—tracked a real change in the climate discourse, as scientists moved from talking about avoiding climate change to mitigation and adaptation in a grimmer future.**
    - "Musk told Tesla shareholders in 2021 that in a snowstorm that knocked out power, solar panels, a Powerwall, and Starlink would provide 'all the things you need for a prepper'—'If Doomsday comes, it could be helpful.'" —Elon Musk
    - The Cybertruck—hulking, unpainted stainless steel, more armored shell than sports car—echoed the Hummer's strange journey from battlefield to boulevard but with an even more overt embrace of survivalist aesthetics, and South African researchers saw echoes of the armored vehicle used to put down township insurrections.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-026"></span>**Musk's ultimate vision for Tesla is a 'dark factory'—a fully automated 'alien dreadnought' with no human workers, where self-driving trucks haul raw materials to robots that transform them into batteries that power more robots, creating a sovereign factory state in which the factory is not just the means of production but the world itself.**
    - The Guggenheim exhibition on the Tahoe Reno Industrial Center by architect Rem Koolhaas observed that the buildings 'are not for humans but for things and machines' and that 'there are no users, only robots'—a post-human architecture.
    - In the most extreme version of Muskism, the human is no longer the subject of history, but its discarded scaffolding—the product is not a car or a robot but a self-regulating infrastructure in which the world runs on its own.

## Part Two: Cyborg

### Attention Alchemy

Musk's immersion in social media from 2015 onward produced a new financial technique—attention alchemy—in which virality is converted into asset price inflation through meme coins and meme stocks, turning his Twitter followers into his real capital, while the platform's feedback loops simultaneously pushed him toward a cyborg worldview in which the boundary between humans and machines was dissolving.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-027"></span>**Web 2.0's platform economy—built on surveillance capitalism's formula of maximizing user engagement to generate data monetized through targeted ads—was enabled not just by clever technology but by the Federal Reserve's near-zero interest rates after 2007–8, which flooded Silicon Valley with cheap capital.**
    - U.S. newspaper advertising revenue fell by nearly 63 percent from 2006 to 2016, while online advertising revenue grew by more than 300 percent over the same period, most captured by Google and Facebook—fulfilling Musk's 1998 prediction that the internet would become the superset of all media.
    - Facebook was founded in 2004, YouTube in 2005, Twitter in 2006—a new generation of 'social' startups powered by monetizing the architecture of participation that Tim O'Reilly named 'Web 2.0.'
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-028"></span>**The political hashtag of the 2010s—from #BlackLivesMatter to #MeToo to #JeSuisCharlie—appeared to fulfill the techno-utopian promise of social media as a democratizing force, but was always shadowed by its doppelgänger: the troll internet of 4chan, which incubated memes and cultivated disruption as a form of attention harvesting.**
    - 4chan, founded in 2003 by fifteen-year-old Christopher 'moot' Poole, popularized trolling and the image macro meme—a format defined by mimicry and remix, as media scholar Limor Shifman analyzed—and became the nursery of far-right internet culture.
    - By fall 2016, #MAGA had eclipsed #BlackLivesMatter as the most popular political hashtag on Twitter, and Trump had retweeted an image of himself as Pepe the Frog—the troll internet had entered the White House.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-029"></span>**From 2015 onward, Musk became extremely online—posting an average of sixty times a day by 2024—not merely for psychic rewards but as a core business strategy: social media let him control his narrative directly, stoke investor hype, and use Twitter as a money machine that could move markets with a single post.**
    - In 2018, Musk tweeted 'Am considering taking Tesla private at $420. Funding secured'—the number was a weed joke, but Tesla stock jumped 11 percent and civil suits from the SEC followed, resulting in $20 million in fines each for Musk and Tesla.
    - In January 2021, he added '#bitcoin' to his Twitter bio and the cryptocurrency jumped 20 percent within an hour—what journalist Marco D'Eramo observed was that Musk's followers were his 'real capital.'
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-030"></span>**Dogecoin—a 2013 'peer-to-peer parody cryptocurrency' based on the Shiba Inu meme—became an instrument of Musk's attention alchemy: his repeated tweets about Doge drove its price up more than 2,000 percent, illustrating how the coin could be understood as 'a tradable electronic token representing the value of Elon Musk's attention.'**
    - "Bloomberg columnist Matt Levine observed that Musk had 'a magic lamp'—he could 'whisper price go up or price go down into the lamp, and it will happen instantly,' making it 'as close to a free-money perpetual motion machine as you'll ever see in finance.'" —Matt Levine
    - "Dogecoin co-creator Jackson Palmer described Musk as someone 'who was and always will be a grifter but the world loves grifters—they love the idea that they may also be a billionaire one day, and that's the dream he's selling.'" —Jackson Palmer
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-031"></span>**The Covid-19 pandemic supercharged Musk's attention alchemy by combining massive fiscal and monetary expansion—which flooded markets with liquidity—with lockdowns that pushed people online, driving Tesla's stock up 740 percent in 2020 and making Musk the world's richest man through a process of becoming a 'human meme stock.'**
    - 'Gamestonk!!' Musk tweeted in January 2021, linking to WallStreetBets—the Reddit forum where retail investors coordinated to rescue GameStop from oblivion—and followed with 'I am become meme, Destroyer of shorts,' turning meme stocks into his declared terrain.
    - The first meme stock wasn't GameStop but Tesla itself: Musk had been inflating its valuation through posting for years, and by 2021 his holdings had made him the richest man in the world—financial fabulism had reached its logical apex.

### Cybernetic Collectives

Musk's immersion in social media produced a coherent theory of the cyborg future: humans and machines have already merged into 'cybernetic collectives' through social media, and the only way to prevent AI from annihilating humanity is to accelerate this fusion—but when the pandemic arrived, Musk concluded that these same collectives were being weaponized to spread a 'woke mind virus' that threatened civilization.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-032"></span>**Musk co-founded OpenAI in 2015 not as an AI pessimist but as someone who believed the only solution to the existential risk of superintelligent AI was to accelerate human-machine merger: if humans 'become an AI–human symbiote,' he argued, there is no evil dictator AI because 'we are the AI collectively.'**
    - Musk told a symposium at MIT in 2014 that AI was 'our biggest existential threat' and praised Nick Bostrom's 2014 book Superintelligence—but his solution was not less technology but more, through merger rather than resistance.
    - "When AI researcher Timnit Gebru asked why he considered AI more dangerous than climate change, Musk clarified: 'Climate change is bad, but it's not going to kill everyone. AI could render humanity extinct.'" —Elon Musk
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-033"></span>**Musk theorized that social media platforms are 'giant cybernetic collectives' whose virality is driven by limbic emotional resonance rather than reason, meaning that user activity is collectively programming AI systems to reflect humanity's combined impulses—making AI not merely superintelligent but a reflection of our id writ large.**
    - "Facebook and Twitter and Instagram are giant cybernetic collectives—their success is a function of how much limbic resonance they achieve with people, and the more limbic resonance, the more engagement." —Elon Musk
    - The ultimate legacy of the cybernetic collectives of social media would be humanity training its own replacement: 'The percentage of intelligence that is not human is increasing,' Musk said, and 'humanity was becoming the biological bootloader for AI.'
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-034"></span>**Musk founded Neuralink in 2016 to build a brain-computer interface that would widen the bandwidth of human-machine merger from the 'tiny straw' of ten fingers on a keyboard to a 'giant river' of direct neural connection—simultaneously treating the brain implant as a safeguard against AI apocalypse and as the logical endpoint of 'never log off.'**
    - Neuralink drew on decades of DARPA-funded brain-computer interface research, including a 2006 DARPA program to build a robotic arm 'directly controlled by neural signals' and a Biological Technologies Office that invested more than $200 million in neurotechnology over two years.
    - "In January 2024, quadriplegic Noland Arbaugh became the first human recipient of a Neuralink chip; two months later he posted a tweet: 'Twitter banned me because they thought I was a bot. @X and @elonmusk reinstated me because I am.'" —Noland Arbaugh
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-035"></span>**The Covid-19 pandemic triggered Musk's political transformation: he interpreted lockdowns as fascist coercion of his manufacturing operations, the government's shelter-in-place orders as a 'mind virus' spread through social media's limbic resonance, and eventually the George Floyd protests as proof of a civilization-threatening 'woke mind virus.'**
    - Musk restarted the Fremont factory in defiance of Alameda County's shelter-in-place order on May 11, 2020, daring officials to arrest him—the plant promptly recorded around 450 Covid cases through year's end.
    - His first use of 'woke mind virus' came in December 2021 with the tweet 'traceroute woke_mind_virus'—diagnostic language from information security applied to what he saw as ideological contagion spreading through the cybernetic collective.
* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-036"></span>**Musk's anti-trans campaign—launched with 'Pronouns suck' in July 2020 and intensifying after his daughter Vivian came out as trans—reveals the deeper logic of Muskism's 'cyborg conservatism': the dissolution of some boundaries (human-machine) must be accompanied by the hardening of others (gender, race, class) to prevent the cyborg synthesis from destabilizing the existing distribution of power.**
    - Donna Haraway's 1985 'Cyborg Manifesto' warned that the fusions of animal and machine could serve to strengthen traditional social hierarchies rather than undermine them, describing the endpoint as 'the final imposition of a grid of control on the planet.'
    - Musk told Jordan Peterson he considered his trans daughter to be dead—'killed by the woke mind virus'—illustrating how the personal and the ideological were inseparable in his rightward turn.

### Godwin's Engine

Musk's acquisition of Twitter in 2022 and founding of xAI in 2023 transformed his anti-woke campaign from commentary into infrastructure, using algorithmic amplification, paid verification, and AI model training to build an anti-woke information weapon—but his attempt to construct a politically calibrated AI produced the pathological output of 'MechaHitler,' illustrating that Godwin's Law had become Godwin's Engine.

### State X

Musk's leadership of DOGE in the second Trump administration represented Muskism's attempt to treat the state itself as a glitchy codebase to be debugged—deleting agencies, integrating databases, and automating governance—but the effort revealed both the limits of imposing cyborg logic on human institutions and the success of implanting surveillance infrastructure and contractor power deeper into the federal government.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-042"></span>**DOGE emerged from a longer bipartisan project of state digitization—from Obama's U.S. Digital Service to the PATRIOT Act's data fusion centers—but combined these precedents with the gaming and meme culture of Muskism to frame federal restructuring as a speedrun dungeon crawler in which the 'woke parasite' in government had to be slain.**
    - "Musk explained DOGE through Star Trek II's Kobayashi Maru—an unwinnable simulation beaten only by reprogramming it: 'The only way to achieve success is to reprogram the matrix such that success is one of the possible outcomes. That's what we're doing.'" —Elon Musk

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-043"></span>**DOGE's central strategy was data integration—breaking down silos between agencies including the IRS, DHS, Social Security Administration, and voting rolls—to build a 'mega API' giving omniscient access to all government data, a direct extension of Silicon Valley's platform logic applied to the state with Palantir as a key partner.**

### Conclusion: Four Futures for Muskism

The authors map four possible futures for Muskism—Carbon Musk (electrification and geoengineering), Contractor Musk (deepening state symbiosis through defense contracts), Compound Musk (demographic panic, white fertility politics, and private settlement-building), and Cyborg Musk (humanoid robots and digital superintelligence as the main quest)—each extrapolating a different strand of the worldview toward its logical endpoint.

* <span id="muskism-an-operating-system-for-the-twenty-first-c-047"></span>**Carbon Musk represents a path where Musk returns to electrification and climate technology as his primary focus—including a $112 million XPRIZE for carbon removal—but this future is undermined by his simultaneous commitment to energy-intensive AI and robot manufacturing, making Carbon Musk and mass robot production mutually exclusive.**
    - Musk's fourth Tesla 'Master Plan' centers an Optimus robot stowing groceries on its cover, with Musk predicting 80 percent of company profits from rolling out Optimus at scale—prioritizing robot production over decarbonization.
