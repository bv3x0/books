# Topology of Violence

## Metadata
- Thesis: Violence never disappears but is protean, shifting from the visible macro-physical violence of negativity (sovereignty, discipline, the friend/enemy antagonism) to an invisible micro-physical violence of positivity, in which the free, self-exploiting achievement-subject becomes perpetrator and victim at once, producing depression and burnout.
- Topics: violence and power, the achievement society and self-exploitation, negativity versus positivity, sovereignty, discipline, and biopolitics, transparency and communication
- Categories: philosophy, social science, political science
- Author: Byung-Chul Han
- Year: 2018
- Collections: Byung-Chul Han
- Mode: quick

### Introduction

Violence does not disappear in modernity but changes form according to social constellation, shifting from visible negativity to invisible positivity so that it merges with freedom itself. The topological stages of this transformation are decapitation in the sovereignty society, deformation in the disciplinary society, and depression in the achievement society.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-001"></span>**Violence is protean and shifts from the visible, frontal, and physical to the invisible, viral, and psychological, becoming completely invisible when it merges with its opposite, freedom, so that it appears to have disappeared while persisting as anonymized, desubjectified systemic violence.**
    - Han criticizes Jan Philipp Reemtsma's thesis of modernity's aversion to violence for attending only to raw corporeal violence while ignoring systemic and subtle forms.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-002"></span>**Beyond the violence of negativity (which is defamatory, degrading, and immunologically resisted) there is a violence of positivity, an accumulation of the positive expressed as overachievement, overproduction, and hyperactivity, which is more disastrous because it is neither visible nor evident and evades immunological defense; infarction replaces infection.**
    - Once exterior repression is overcome, pressure builds within, so the free achievement-subject develops depression: violence merely shifts to the interior, becoming internalized, psychologized, and self-referential.

## Part I: The Macro-physics of Violence

### The Topology of Violence

Premodern society displayed and staged deadly violence as a signifying insignium of sovereign power, but modernity delegitimizes and hides brute violence, subjecting it to a topological transformation from the visible/frontal to the invisible/viral and from external to internalized psychic conflict. The three stages of this internalization are decapitation, deformation, and finally the self-directed violence of the achievement-subject.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-003"></span>**In archaic culture and antiquity violence was mundane, visible, and eloquently staged as a central component of societal communication, so that rulers exhibited power through blood and the theater of brutality, exemplified by the Roman munus gladiatorium and midday executions (damnatio ad gladium, ad flammas, ad bestias) that made deadly violence an insignium of sovereign power.**
    - The Greeks called torture anankai, from anankaios (necessary), so torture was tolerated like fate or a law of nature (ananke); Greek myth is a blood-drenched culture of fervor, as when the boar claims it wanted only to caress Adonis with its eroticized teeth.
    - The munus gladiatorium was an important component of the imperial cult; violence had no shame and was eloquent and signifying rather than silent.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-004"></span>**Modernity delegitimizes and conceals brute violence, moving executions out of public showplaces so that violence hides in shame and becomes mute annihilation; the concentration camp exemplifies this shift as a non-place at the edge of the city, distinct from the prison, which remains a place.**
    - The bloodless gas chamber withdrawn from public view replaces the theater of bloody violence; the camp slang term Muslim (Muselmann) names a victim of a violence that has become shameful and renounces itself.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-005"></span>**Violence undergoes topological transformation from confrontation to contamination and from the physical to the psychic, so that internalization turns aggression against others into aggression against the self via conscience, an intrapsychic surveillance entity, and this internalization serves both rule (symbolic violence naturalizes domination) and discipline (subcutaneous deformation).**
    - "Civilization, therefore, obtains mastery over the individual's dangerous desire for aggression by weakening and disarming it and by setting up an agency within him to watch over it, like a garrison in a conquered city." —Sigmund Freud
    - The wholesale violence of decapitation in sovereignty society yields to the successive, subcutaneous deformation of disciplinary society.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-006"></span>**The late modern achievement-subject is subjugated by no one and positivizes itself into a project, but self-compulsion replaces outside compulsion because self-exploitation is more efficient and productive than external exploitation, being accompanied by a sense of freedom; the achievement-subject exploits itself until burned out, and the project proves to be a projectile aimed at the self.**
    - The transformation from subject to project does not cause violence to disappear; auto-aggressive tendencies intensify to the violence of suicide.

### The Archeology of Violence

Against Girard's mimetic-rivalry theory, Han argues that archaic violence follows a capitalist rather than mimetic logic: killing has intrinsic value because accumulating deadly violence (mana) wards off death and increases power. This archaic thanatological economy persists in the nuclear arms race, capitalist accumulation, and the Christian economy of salvation, all of which are techniques for spiriting death away.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-007"></span>**Girard's concept of mimetic rivalry does not capture the essence of violence, because violent conflicts often concern things of intrinsic rather than mimetic value (primary needs, money as value itself), and the archaic blood feud is controlled not by mimesis but by a capitalistic principle in which killing has intrinsic value.**
    - Etymologically rivals derive from rivus (waterway): a rival does not desire water because others desire it; Achilles avenges Patroclus by indiscriminate killing and slaughtering vast numbers of animals, where the object is killing as such.
    - "in its perfection and paroxysm mimesis becomes a chain reaction of vengeance." —René Girard
* <span id="topology-of-violence-008"></span>**Violence is the first religious experience: prehistoric people externalized traumatizing natural and predatory violence into deities, interpreted every death as a violent external influence, and treated archaic religion as a complex of interactions with externalized violence, in which sacrifice is not merely preventive (as Girard claims) but active and productive of exalting, power-heightening violence.**
    - The Aztecs waged ritualized wars led by priests to take sacrificial prisoners, making mass killing a religious act; the skulls heaped on temple frames accumulate like capital, speaking the language of active violence production, not prevention.
    - "What would be the value of a god who knew nothing of anger, revenge, envy, scorn, cunning, violence?" —Friedrich Nietzsche
* <span id="topology-of-violence-009"></span>**Archaic power was not a ruling relationship but an impersonal, accumulable power-substance (mana) that leapt from victim to victor, so that killing was a thanatological technique to survive death and the undirected blood feud avenged every death (even natural) indiscriminately to restore lost power, making it uncontrollably destructive.**
    - Marquesas Islanders called this substance mana; a warrior absorbed the mana of those he killed, eating flesh and wearing relics like a bone, dried hand, or skull to bind its empowering influence.
    - Against Clastres, archaic society cannot consciously reject the state; Clastres mistakes desire for ability, since archaic power-as-substance did not establish the hierarchical domination that constitutes a state.
* <span class="pull-quote">Killing has an intrinsic value.</span>
* <span id="topology-of-violence-010"></span>**Punishment rationalizes revenge by turning violence into a personal action placed in a context of guilt rather than power, following the logic of objective legal mediation rather than counterviolence, so that unlike revenge it is designed to prevent rather than produce violence.**
    - Released from the context of power, violence makes me guilty rather than powerful, so no spiral of violence begins.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-011"></span>**The archaic economy of violence persists in modernity: the nuclear arms race accumulates destructive potential like mana to create the illusion of invulnerability, and capital behaves like modern mana, since the etymology of Geld links money to sacrifice and both blood and money function as instruments against death.**
    - Capital creates the illusion of endless time because others can be paid to work in one's stead, so accumulating capital works against the absolute lack of time; alchemy's transmutation of lead (associated with Saturn, god of time) into gold conjures away impermanence.
    - For Calvinists economic success alone generated the certitudo salutis; the archaic economy of mana, the capitalist economy of capital, and the Christian economy of salvation are all thanatological techniques for spiriting death away.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-012"></span>**Capitalism absolutizes bare survival over the good life, stripping life of its liveliness and narrative content so that health becomes a self-referential goddess, functionality without function, producing a society of the undead capable neither of living nor dying; Freud's counter-formula demands giving death more space in life.**
    - "Si vis vitam, para mortem — If you wish life, prepare for death." —Sigmund Freud
    - The loss of ideals leaves the attention-desperate self with only exhibition value and health value; mere survival is obscene because it robs life of liveliness.

### The Psyche of Violence

Freud's psychic apparatus is a system of negativity built on repression, prohibition, and the internalized other (superego, melancholic identification), so psychoanalysis is possible only in repressive societies. The achievement society, governed by can rather than should, produces a post-Freudian ego whose depression and burnout arise not from repression but from an excess of positivity and self-referential auto-aggression.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-013"></span>**Freud's psychic apparatus is a repressive system of negativity organized by resistance, negation, and repression, in which the superego is an internalized other (god, sovereign, father) dominating the ego with its dictatorial Thou shalt, so its violence issues from the other within the self.**
    - "the harshness and cruelty exhibited by the ideal—its dictatorial 'Thou shalt.'" —Sigmund Freud
    - In melancholia the abandoned object is internalized through narcissistic identification, so self-reproach is really directed at the other now part of the ego, transforming sadism into masochism.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-014"></span>**Because psychoanalysis presumes the negativity of prohibition and repression, it cannot broach contemporary afflictions like depression, burnout, and ADHD, which point not to negation but to an excess of positivity, the inability to say no rather than that which is not allowed, and the ability to do everything.**
    - The modal verb of achievement society is can, not the Freudian should; the achievement-subject is poor in negation, a post-Freudian ego of affirmation.
    - The hysteric displays a characteristic morph and is a subject of character, while the depressive is a-morphous, a man without character.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-015"></span>**The lack of relationship to the other causes a crisis of gratification, because gratification as recognition requires the authority of a third party (God for Kant), so the achievement-subject, lacking such an authority and lacking closure in open-ended work, feels compelled to achieve endlessly and falls into narcissistic self-reference.**
    - "Self-absorption does not produce gratification, it produces injury to the self... One drowns in the self." —Richard Sennett
    - Against Sennett, the narcissistic subject does not intentionally avoid closure to bolster the self; rather it is unable to reach a goal because socially valid forms of closure have become impossible, which economically facilitates growth.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-016"></span>**Ehrenberg mistakenly rescues the psychoanalytic conflict model by treating depression as a democratized melancholia and a hidden conflict, but depression completely eludes the conflict model because it lacks negativity; burnout is instead the pathological consequence of voluntary self-exploitation driven by the economic imperative to flexible, ever-changing identity.**
    - "depression is the manifestation of the democratization of the exceptional." —Alain Ehrenberg
    - Industrial disciplinary society relies on unchanging identity, while the postindustrial achievement society requires flexible people to ramp up production.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-017"></span>**In the transition from disciplinary to achievement society the repressive superego positivizes into the ego ideal, so that instead of debasing itself before prohibitions the self bases itself on an unattainable ideal, and auto-aggression develops out of the gap between the real ego and the ego ideal, making external violence into more devastating self-generated violence whose victims imagine themselves free.**
    - Self-referential absolute competition means the achievement-subject competes only with itself, trying to outdo itself, which is fatal like trying to chase your own shadow.

### The Politics of Violence

Schmitt, Benjamin, and Agamben all conceive the political through the negativity of the friend/enemy distinction, the identity of law and violence, and sovereignty, but Han argues that power differs fundamentally from violence and that genuine politics is mediation, friendship, and the common motivation to live together. These thinkers are anachronistic because they project the negativity of the sovereignty society onto today's postimmunological achievement society, whose violence is one of consensus and the same.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-018"></span>**For Schmitt the essence of the political is the existential friend/enemy distinction, so a community becomes political only when an enemy poses an existential threat in war, and enmity is constitutive of identity because the ego owes its Gestalt to immunological defense against the other as enemy.**
    - "The enemy is our own question as Gestalt." —Carl Schmitt
    - Existentialization deprives factual domains (moral, religious, economic) of their factuality; a competitor is not an enemy because a competitive relationship lacks existential tension.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-019"></span>**Schmitt's politics of violence is a politics of identity resting on the cutting violence of decision (decidere: to cut off) that renders discussion superfluous, so he disparages parliamentarianism (parler), compromise, romanticism, and the fluid sea as characterless, revealing a psyche compulsively committed to dichotomy that is at odds with the flexible, character-free achievement-subject.**
    - "The sea has no character, in the original sense of the word, which comes from the Greek charassein, meaning to engrave, to scratch, to imprint." —Carl Schmitt
    - Character is a phenomenon of negativity presupposing exclusion, but the ideal achievement-subject is character-free and flexible because rigid identity hinders the acceleration of production.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-020"></span>**Against Schmitt's friend/enemy immunology, Han proposes a politics of friendship as an active yes to the other's being that generates maximum solidarity from minimum commonality; this is more open than tolerance, which merely permits otherness from a stable dominant self-image, and would free the narcissistic self from its debilitating self-reference (which can otherwise manufacture imaginary enemies, as in xenophobia).**
    - The more different the other, the keener the kindness; faced with the same, neither friendship nor enmity is possible.
* <span class="pull-quote">Thus one could invert Habermas's phrase and speak of the violence of consensus.</span>
* <span id="topology-of-violence-021"></span>**Against Benjamin, who traces law to lawmaking and law-preserving violence and dismisses parliamentarianism, Han insists that law is not based on violence but on the communicative essence of speech: parliament, compromise, and contract are effects of speaking with one another, and stable legal order rests on assent, not the threat of violence.**
    - "Representational thought often imagines that the state is held together by force; but what holds it together is simply the basic sense of order which everyone possesses." —G. W. F. Hegel
    - Benjamin invokes the Niobe legend to claim all legal violence is mythical, but Hesiod already distinguished right, which Zeus gave to mankind, from the violence by which beasts devour one another.
    - Benjamin's one concrete example of pure divine violence, God's annihilation of Korah's company, is not pure but ratifies Moses's rule, so it is contaminated by domination like mythical violence.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-022"></span>**Only power, not violence, can build a legal space, because power develops along a yes and organizes a symbolic order (symballein) while violence is diabolic, divisive (diaballein), destructive, and space-annihilating; genuine politics is Aristotelian mediation aimed at the good life and self-sufficiency, founded on friendship rather than the will to power.**
    - "And if men are friends, there is no need of justice between them." —Aristotle
    - A political community arises from a sense of deficiency, not a will to power; Hobbes's commonwealth is founded on covenant and common power (not violence), so subjects are authors of the sovereign's acts.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-023"></span>**Agamben anachronistically collapses law into violence (violenza) and sovereignty, projecting the negativity of the state of exception onto a postimmunological achievement society whose crisis is precisely that no state of exception is possible because everything is absorbed into the immanence of the same, the hell of the same, producing a violence of consensus.**
    - Contemporary violence is based more on the conformity of consensus than the antagonism of dissent; politics has positivized into work in the immanent space of capital, filling political emptiness with media spectacle.
    - Against Agamben's claim that media dispense glory and doxology, contemporary media generate only glory without a kingdom; advertisements are capitalist liturgical hymns and acclamation of capital's domination is now called consumption.

### The Macro-logic of Violence

Macro-physical violence is a phenomenon of negativity that develops in the tension between interior and exterior, self and other, taking the forms of infiltration, invasion, and infection and de-internalizing its bearer by penetrating and annihilating it. Power differs categorically from this violence because it is relational, constructive, space-building, and symbolic, whereas the positivizing society shifts toward a non-immunological micro-violence of the same, a terror of the same.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-024"></span>**Macro-physical violence is a violence of negativity constituted by the other: it is an uninternalizable event that forces its way inside and destroys the interior, expressing itself as de-internalization by an exterior, so that where an external effect can be consented to and appropriated into a relationship, it is no longer violence.**
    - Violence is the rift that does not allow mediation; encapsulated trauma is violence because it cannot be processed and discarded like a theme.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-025"></span>**Power is categorically distinct from violence: power forms a connective, hierarchical, symbolic continuum that leaves room for action and uses the freedom of others (leadership and misleading), organizes and works, and builds space, whereas violence is a non-relational, diabolic, destructive hiatus that annihilates space and crushes freedom.**
    - "The feeling of power first conquering, then ruling (organizing)—it regulates that which has been overcome for the benefit of its own preservation." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Power bends otherness until it bows; violence bends otherness until it breaks. Massive resistance and violence arise from insufficient power, so violence is a desperate attempt to convert powerlessness into power.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-026"></span>**Neither power, violence, nor even love (Heidegger's amo volo ut sis) can let the other simply be, because the intentionality of volo places love under the regime of the self; only friendship approaches the utmost freedom toward the other, yet friendship too, as another self (allos autos), remains within the regime of the self.**
    - "You love in him not what he is, but what you would have him be." —Augustine
    - Arendt suspects that volo ut sis can mean a desire for mastery that makes the other's essence an object of one's own will.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-027"></span>**As society positivizes, power loses significance as a socio-immunological medium and hierarchies are dismantled, but this does not end violence, because the micro-physics of violence follows the non-immunological logic of the same: infarction replaces infection, the enemy positivizes into the competitor, and violence de-internalizes the subject by dissipating it with an excess of positivity (as in ADD/ADHD).**
    - Macro-physical violence is disjunctive, exclusive, and casts victims into radical passivity; micro-physical violence is additive, inclusive, and destructive through an excess of activity expressed as hyperactivity.
    - Sartre's L'enfer, c'est les autres is only thinkable in an immunological era; today otherness gives way to consumable difference that provokes no immune reaction, so promiscuity and hybridity require the disappearance of the immunological other.

## Part II: The Micro-physics of Violence

### Systematic Violence

The structural (Galtung), symbolic (Bourdieu), and objective (Žižek) theories of violence all remain within a negativity model in which a ruling class exploits an excluded other, thereby confusing power with violence. Han argues that today's systematic violence has no acting subject and no antagonism: it affects all members indiscriminately as a violence of positivity that turns everyone into self-exploiting captives who are perpetrator and victim at once.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-028"></span>**Galtung's structural violence and Bourdieu's symbolic violence both fail to distinguish power from violence, because structures that perpetuate injustice or naturalize domination through habitual assent are actually rulership techniques that allow ruling without violence; unequal educational opportunity is injustice, not violence.**
    - "violence is present when human beings are being influenced so that their actual somatic and mental realizations are below their potential realizations." —Johan Galtung
    - Symbolic violence collapses comprehension of what is into compliance with rule, stabilizing power by making it seem natural; banality is the affirmation of established power relations.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-029"></span>**Žižek's objective violence, like symbolic-structural violence, remains a negativity model of exclusion (homines sacri, minorities, the unemployed) in which one class exploits the other, so he misses the systematic violence without domination that leads to self-exploitation and affects everyone, since men as well as women undergo cosmetic procedures and top dogs as well as underdogs suffer burnout in a doping society that knows no class or gender.**
    - The victims of systemic violence are not the excluded homines sacri but the achievement-subjects trapped within the system, the homo sacer of themselves, so systemic violence turns everyone into captives forced to exploit themselves.
    - Žižek treats Western women's voluntary cosmetic surgery and African genital mutilation as not fundamentally different; the compulsion to optimize the body encompasses everyone indiscriminately, producing muscle, steroid, and fitness zombies.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-030"></span>**Systematic violence as violence of positivity has no acting subject and requires no antagonism or hierarchy between classes, because it is exercised by the system itself; it lacks the negativity of prohibition or exclusion and manifests as excess, overproduction, and exhaustion, so it is not perceived as violence and produces psychic bulimia and neuronal disruptions like burnout.**
    - It is not only too little that leads to violence but also too much, not the negativity of prohibition but the positivity of the ability to do everything; the difference between exploiter and exploited disappears and violence becomes self-referential.

### The Micro-physics of Power

Foucault's theory that sovereign deadly power gave way to disciplinary power and biopower actually describes a new form of society rather than a new form of power, and it lacks the sensory faculties to detect violence (in torture, suicide, the camps). His disciplinary society of prisons has been superseded by the achievement society, whose free, self-exploiting subject makes violence self-targeting, producing burnout rather than deformation.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-031"></span>**Foucault's micro-physics of power describes disciplinary power and biopower as life-fostering, normalizing techniques that supplant the sovereign's deadly power, but Han argues Foucault is really describing a new form of society (disciplinary society) rather than a form of power and rule, since disciplinary and biopolitical methods affect all classes and lack a defined ruling subject.**
    - Foucault defines power as nonsubjective and intentional at once, but nonsubjective intentionality is an oxymoron, since power necessarily requires subjectivity and a relation of domination; he later reintroduced subjectivity.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-032"></span>**Foucault lacks the sensory faculties to detect violence, conceiving torture as a regulated production of truth rather than an economy of violence and pleasure, dismissing the increase in suicide as a persistent natural event, and (as Agamben notes) never dwelling on the camp, because his biopolitics that invests life through and through cannot access the deadly violence of the camp as a non-place.**
    - The prison is a constitutive locality within disciplinary society, but the camp is a non-place, and this difference eludes Foucaultian biopolitics.
    - Against Agamben, who blames Foucault's untimely death, Han argues Foucault's theory is structurally unable to include the camp's deadly violence.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-033"></span>**The disciplinary society of prisons, barracks, and factories has been replaced by a society of glass towers, malls, fitness centers, and beauty clinics, so the achievement-subject is free (governed by can, not should) and dominated by no one, which transforms freedom into compulsion and replaces exploitation of the other with self-exploitation that continues until collapse.**
    - The three stages of violence are decapitation (sovereignty, medium blood), deformation (discipline, social orthopedics), and depression (achievement society, where freedom is indistinguishable from compulsion); burnout arises from a battle without domination or enmity, sounding from the psyche itself.

### The Violence of Positivity

Religion is a system of negativity that holds social entropy low, and the modern orgy of deregulation and deritualization generates a glut of positivity whose accumulation congests and collapses the system. Baudrillard's theory of virulence and terrorism remains trapped in the immunological negativity model, missing that today's exemplary illnesses arise from an excess of the same, so the coming catastrophe is a self-referential implosion, a global burnout.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-034"></span>**Religion is a system of negativity whose commands, prohibitions, and rituals hold the entropy of the social system low, so the ongoing orgy of liberation, deregulation, and deritualization reduces negativity and generates a glut of positivity that blocks circulation until the system collapses, since after a certain point information is no longer informative and communication no longer communicative.**
    - "The uninterrupted production of positivity has a terrifying consequence. Whereas negativity engenders crisis and critique, hyperbolic positivity for its part engenders catastrophe." —Jean Baudrillard
* <span id="topology-of-violence-035"></span>**Baudrillard's theory of virulence and his genealogy of the enemy (wolf, rat, beetle, virus) remain trapped in the immunological negativity model, but HIV, cancer, and computer viruses all still assume the negativity of the immunological other that can be met with antibodies, so viral violence is a violence of negativity and our era is not truly a viral one.**
    - The pathology of the positive has nothing to do with the immune system: fat can be burned, not repelled; too much of the same causes a digestive-psychic abreaction (vomiting), not an immunological defense; disgust is an immune reaction because directed at the other.
    - Baudrillard totalizes the viral violence of terrorism as the exemplary violence today and, like Negri, lapses into a postmodern romanticism of singularity that misreads contemporary antagonism.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-036"></span>**The new world war takes place without an enemy who could be battled: one is at war with oneself, so enmity becomes self-referential and cannot be won but only ended by global collapse, a global burnout, through implosive rather than explosive violence, since the lack of an exterior makes the system push inward and overheat until it implodes.**
    - "Were we to suppose that the human race represented an illness for some higher organism completely inconceivable to us... just so do the race of bacilli strive to annihilate the 'ailing' human individual!" —Arthur Schnitzler
    - Acceleration that overshoots its purpose becomes not growth but an excrescence, a rapid increase in entropy; the achievement-subject's burnout is a pathological precursor to the system's implosion, lending plausibility to Freud's death-drive thesis.

### The Violence of Transparency

The compulsion for transparency reflects a society dominated by excess positivity that reduces negativity and levels all otherness into the light of the same. Transparency is a dictatorship of the same that eliminates secrecy, thresholds, and the incommensurable other; it converges pornographic self-exhibition with panoptic control, so that in the digital panopticon freedom and control fuse and exposure becomes exploitation.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-037"></span>**The compulsion for transparency belongs to a society dominated by excess positivity, so it dismantles thresholds and cannot be separated from hypercommunication, hyperinformation, and hypervisibility; it destroys the negativity of the sacred (threshold experience of the completely other), of faith, of trust, and of thinking, which unlike transparent calculation gathers experience by making other.**
    - "I live on what others don't know about me." —Peter Handke
    - Only a machine is transparent, so total transparency would reduce humans to functional system elements; the German Geist originally meant agitation, showing psyche is never fully transparent.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-038"></span>**The violence of transparency is the reduction of the other to the same, a dictatorship of the same that accelerates communication in a chain reaction of the same and annihilates the vague, opaque, and narrative; counting replaces recounting and transparent time is time without scent, without events, without narration.**
    - Memory has narrative structure and is constantly reordered and rewritten, whereas saved data always remain the same.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-039"></span>**Politics as strategic action requires spheres of secrecy (Schmitt's arcana) and absolute negativity in the state of exception, so total transparency degrades politics into theatrocracy and reduces the exposed society to a pornographic, obscene society in which everything is on display, valued only by exhibition value, and hypervisibility itself is obscene.**
    - "To every great politics belongs the 'arcanum.' Everything will take place on stage (before an audience of papagenos)." —Carl Schmitt
    - "visible things do not terminate in obscurity and in silence; they vanish into what is more visible than the visible: obscenity." —Jean Baudrillard
* <span id="topology-of-violence-040"></span>**Contemporary transparency society converges pornographic self-exhibition with panoptic control in the digital panopticon (Google, Facebook), which requires no walls but operates through interconnection and voluntary self-exposure, so that the transparent customer is the homo sacer of the economic panopticon and freedom and control become indistinguishable: exposure is exploitation, communication is commerce.**
    - Control society reaches its apogee when subjects expose themselves through a self-generated need rather than outside coercion; the transparent user is victim and perpetrator at once, diligently building the panopticon of the net.

### The Medium Is the Mass-age

Language has both a symbolic (connective, communicative) and a diabolic (divisive, injurious) function, and the positivizing of society produces a violence of language that arises not from negating the other but from a mass of the same. Overcommunication and the hypertrophied post-Cartesian I-am generate spamification, positivized garbage that seeks attention rather than territory, and a mass without message that dulls and paralyzes.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-041"></span>**Language expresses itself both symbolically (symballein: connective, communicative, constructive) and diabolically (diaballein: divisive, injurious, destructive), so raising consensus to the whole essence of language misses its diabolic function while aligning language too closely with violence misses its symbolic dimension.**
    - The new violence of language is not the negative violence of defamation and disavowal directed against the other, but a positive violence arising from a mass of the same, an accumulation of the positive.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-042"></span>**Overcommunication causes a spamification of language accompanied by a hypertrophy of the ego and a post-Cartesian shift, so that the tentative Cartesian I think therefore I am becomes the solid, redundant post-Cartesian I-am that no longer needs to negate the other to position itself; Bauman's I shop, therefore I am should be inverted to I am, therefore I shop.**
    - "I am thinking, therefore I exist." —René Descartes
    - Communication becomes merely cumulative rather than communicative and information becomes de-formative rather than in-formative; tweets reduce to I-am, a postimmunological self that courts the attention of the other instead of warding it off.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-043"></span>**Overcommunication increases entropy and produces waste-communication, but unlike Serres's Cartesian animals who repel others with foul-smelling, territorial (negative) garbage, today's garbage is positivized: cloaked in the beautiful advertisement, it seeks attention and inclusion rather than exclusion, so post-Cartesian nightingales twitter for attention rather than to drive others out.**
    - "Imperious images and letters force us to read, while the pleading things of the world are begging our senses for meaning." —Michel Serres
    - Overproduction and overaccumulation are transeconomic, breaking the tie between means and end so growth diabolizes into proliferation; the positive mass without message scatters, dulls, and paralyzes.

### Rhizomatic Violence

Violence originates not only in repressive overcoding but also in unbounded decoding and deterritorialization, which Deleuze one-sidedly celebrates as liberation while masking its diabolic side. The schizophrenic and, correspondingly, the capitalist machine fuse construction with destruction, so the rhizomatic addition of the same produces a violence of positivity, an infarction of the system, that is more calamitous than the negative violence of deprivation.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-044"></span>**Violence originates not only in repressive overcoding but also in unbounded decoding and dissolution of boundaries; Deleuze undialectically celebrates decoding, deterritorialization, the body without organs, and the rhizome as liberation while masking their diabolic side, since cancerous proliferation that nullifies organic difference is itself a diabolic form of decoding.**
    - "the fabric of the rhizome is the conjunction, 'and … and … and. …'" —Gilles Deleuze
    - The accelerated addition of the same causes a psychic bulimia and infarction rather than an immunological defense, because there is no immune reaction to and or too much.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-045"></span>**Deleuze's schizo, who scrambles codes and carries a death drive fusing construction with destruction, corresponds directly to the capitalist machine, since Deleuze himself makes decoding and deterritorialization the most characteristic tendency of capitalism, so schizophrenic production does not differ meaningfully from unregulated capitalist production.**
    - "death is its motor" —Félix Guattari
    - Deleuze romanticizes schizophrenia as joyous and free (comparing it to Tinguely's harmonious machines), but true schizophrenia causes disruptions, blockages, and compulsion; his idealized joyous system actually forms a harmonious continuum bound by friendship, not real schizophrenic rifts.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-046"></span>**Not all negativity is destructive: forms of negativity like hesitation, pausing, boredom, waiting, and rage are constructive, and only the negativity of a constraint allows cadence, rhythm, and time itself to develop, so both Hegel's paranoid hyperencoding and Deleuze's schizophrenic decoding are destructive and annihilate time; without negativity thought becomes mere calculation and computers are autistic calculation machines.**
    - The positive violence of excess is more calamitous than the negative violence of deprivation, because deficiency ends at saturation but excess knows no end.

### The Violence of the Global

Hardt and Negri's opposition of Empire and multitude relies on the historically obsolete categories of class and class struggle, ignoring that in global capitalism everyone self-exploits so that perpetrator and victim merge and no counterforce can form. The only conceivable end is an implosion of the overheated system, and their proposal to accelerate the movement of the market would guarantee total burnout.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-047"></span>**Hardt and Negri conceive Empire's violence as exploitation of the other by a vampire regime, but the multitude is basically the only class, so there is no ruling class to struggle against; everyone in the capitalist system exploits themselves, and Hardt and Negri fail to recognize this self-exploitation without domination.**
    - "a vampire regime of accumulated dead labor that survives only by sucking off the blood of the living." —Michael Hardt and Antonio Negri
    - When perpetrator and victim are one, no resistance is possible; in Empire combatants soon prove to be collaborators.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-048"></span>**Contemporary society is characterized by the universal disappearance of a we and by ego-ification and atomization, so socius gives way to solus and the world is populated not by a resisting multitude but by isolated, mutually antagonistic, self-exploiting selves; Hardt and Negri argue counterfactually by imputing communication and communal intention to unconnected struggles.**
    - The struggles they cite (Tiananmen Square, the 1992 Los Angeles unrest, the Intifada, Chiapas, French rail strikes) share no common language or enemy, yet they impute a nonexistent communal intention, calling the lack of communication a new type of communication.
    - Contrary to their thesis of declining private property, the communal is in increasing decline and nothing is sacred to expanding privatization, making collective action ever less likely.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-049"></span>**Because capitalism after the fall of communism has no exterior that could threaten it, only an implosion through overheating is conceivable, so Hardt and Negri's Deleuzean proposal to accelerate and intensify the movement of the market would be devastating; the total commercialization of the world (which is not identical to the world market) would violate it and make total burnout unavoidable.**
    - "Empire can be effectively contested only on its own level of generality and by pushing the processes that it offers past their present limitations." —Michael Hardt and Antonio Negri
    - War now fuses completely with social relations and presents itself as freedom, extending into the soul of every individual who wages it with themselves, so classic resistance assuming a clear friend/enemy separation tilts at windmills.

### Homo liber

Agamben's theory that human life is politicized only through abandonment to sovereign death and that the state of exception threatens to become the norm is anachronistic, because today's society of positivity makes the state of exception impossible by totalizing the interior. The achievement-subject who considers itself the free sovereign of itself (homo liber) proves to be the homo sacer of itself, so victim and perpetrator, master and slave, freedom and violence merge.

* <span id="topology-of-violence-050"></span>**The state of exception is a state of extreme negativity requiring an exterior that punctures the interior of a system, so the fascination with Agamben's sovereignty theory attests not that the exception is becoming the norm but that we live in a society of positivity in which a state of exception is no longer possible, since positivization totalizes the interior and obliterates any exterior.**
    - Han's long endnote argues Agamben's homo sacer is a fiction: sacer was originally divine banishment, and homo sacer cannot be sacrificed because he is already in the possession of the offended deity, so Agamben erases the religious origins and conflates sacratio with sovereignty.
    - The terror of positivity may be more catastrophic than the terror of negativity because it evades immune defense.
* <span id="topology-of-violence-051"></span>**The achievement-subject is the free sovereign of itself (homo liber, entrepreneur of itself), subjugated to no one but itself, so freedom and compulsion merge and self-exploitation without domination is more efficient than exploitation by the other; the sovereign of achievement society proves to be the homo sacer of itself, and unlike Agamben's clearly distinguished sovereign and homo sacer, they are identical.**
    - The capitalist system shifts from exploitation of the other to self-exploitation, from should to can, in order to accelerate; the achievement-subject is at once perpetrator and victim, master and slave, where freedom and violence are indistinguishable.
    - "The history of violence culminates in this merging of victim and perpetrator, of master and slave, of freedom and violence." —Byung-Chul Han
* <span id="topology-of-violence-052"></span>**The late modern homo sacer is centrally located rather than at the edge: unlike the Muselmann at the outermost edge of the totalitarian order, every achievement-subject carries the camp with it as prisoner and warden at once, so the achievement-subject with its burnout and depression is itself a Muselmann and cannot defend itself from a violence committed by itself.**
    - Against Ehrenberg, Nietzsche's sovereign man is not the depressive achievement-subject but a culturally critical countermodel, a man of leisure who conserves his peace and finds hyperactivity repugnant; the self-exploiting subject is Nietzsche's last man, not sovereign man.
    - The homines sacri of the achievement society have bare life reduced to vital functions and performance, made sacred by the new goddess Health, and they are impossible to kill: too alive to die and too dead to live, like the undead.
