# The Palliative Society

## Metadata
- Thesis: Contemporary society is a 'palliative society' governed by a universal fear of pain (algophobia) that, by suppressing and medicalizing pain, empties life of meaning, truth, art, and revolution, reducing existence to mere painless survival within a 'hell of the same.'
- Topics: algophobia and pain avoidance, neoliberal performance and happiness, medicalization and depoliticization of pain, pain as source of truth, art, and meaning, survival, biopolitics, and the pandemic
- Categories: philosophy, social science, psychology
- Author: Byung-Chul Han
- Year: 2021
- Collections: Byung-Chul Han
- Mode: quick

## Algophobia

A universal fear of pain (algophobia) produces a permanent social anaesthesia that, through a paradigm of positivity and the 'like,' eliminates negativity from politics, art, and life, smoothing everything into agreeableness and foreclosing catharsis and experience of the wholly other.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-001"></span>**A generalized fear of pain (algophobia) produces a permanent anaesthesia in which all painful conditions are avoided, and this extends into politics as 'palliative democracy' that avoids conflict, surrenders to systemic compulsion, and cannot enact painful radical reform.**
    - "'There is no alternative': this is a political analgesia." —Byung-Chul Han
    - Chantal Mouffe's demand for an 'agonistic politics' that does not shy away from debate is invoked against the palliative 'centre ground.'
* <span id="the-palliative-society-002"></span>**Algophobia rests on a paradigm shift to a society of positivity that extinguishes negativity; positive psychology subjects pain to a logic of performance, treating trauma as a 'catalyst' and promoting resilience to produce a permanently happy, pain-insensitive performance subject.**
    - The neoliberal ideology of resilience speaks of 'post-traumatic growth,' reframing suffering as increased performance.
    - The US opioid crisis is emblematic: an ideology of permanent well-being allowed a palliative-medicine drug to be administered en masse to healthy individuals.
    - "Americans today probably belong to the first generation on earth that looks at a pain-free life as something like a constitutional right. Pain is a scandal." —David B. Morris
* <span id="the-palliative-society-003"></span>**The palliative society coincides with the performance society: pain is read as a sign of weakness incompatible with performance, condemned to be mute, and never permitted to become an articulated passion.**
    - The passivity of suffering has no place in an active society dominated by 'ability.'
* <span id="the-palliative-society-004"></span>**The palliative society is a 'society of the like' in which everything—art, morality, and life itself—is smoothed into agreeableness and made 'instagrammable,' driven by the mutual reinforcement of the economization of culture and the culturalization of the economy.**
    - An art auction report notes even a gigantic Louise Bourgeois spider (Spider, sold for thirty-two million) is more decorative than threatening; Ai Weiwei's morality inspires 'likes' while Jeff Koons makes morality-free decorative art whose only adequate response is 'Wow.'
    - Creativity as economic strategy permits only 'variations of the same' and lacks the negativity of a break that hurts, so pain and commerce are mutually exclusive.
    - Old master portraits by Cranach and Rubens are color-matched with designer clothes and framed as precursors to the selfie.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-005"></span>**Because pain purifies and has a cathartic effect, its elimination leaves 'residues of positivity' accumulating beneath the culture of likes; genuine art must be foreign, disturbing, even painful, since pain is the tear through which the wholly other enters.**
    - "Goosebumps are the first aesthetic image." —Theodor W. Adorno
    - "Consciousness without shudder is reified consciousness." —Theodor W. Adorno
    - Experience 'is in essence the suffering in which the essential otherness of beings reveals itself in opposition to the tried and usual' (Heidegger).

## The Compulsion of Happiness

Where torture and disciplinary society maintained an affirmative, constructive relationship with pain, the neoliberal performance society decouples power from pain and installs 'Be happy' as its formula of rule, medicalizing and privatizing suffering to depoliticize it and forestall revolution.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-006"></span>**Pain is a culturally structured means of rule whose form shifts with the mode of domination: the society of torture displayed pain publicly as an insignia of power, while the disciplinary society moved pain into closed spaces (prisons, barracks, factories, schools) to construct docile, productive bodies.**
    - "A few decades saw the disappearance of the tortured, dismembered, amputated body... The body as the major target of penal repression disappeared." —Michel Foucault
    - Jünger describes discipline as 'the way man maintains contact with pain'; his heroic 'worker' hardens 'like steel' and treats the body as a deployable outpost rather than an end in itself.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-007"></span>**The post-heroic, hedonistic body treats pain as senseless and to be avoided at all cost; in the neoliberal performance society negativities (orders, punishments) give way to positivities (motivation, self-optimization), power becomes 'smart' and decoupled from pain, and subjugation occurs through exploited freedom.**
    - "The appeal to 'be free' produces a compulsion that is far more devastating than the injunction 'be obedient.'" —Byung-Chul Han
    - Smart power operates seductively and permissively, so freedom and surveillance become indistinguishable; the subjugated exploit themselves believing they are self-realizing.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-008"></span>**The neoliberal 'dispositif of happiness' depoliticizes society by forcing psychological introspection: suffering is privatized and psychologized so people blame themselves rather than social conditions, replacing revolutionaries with motivational speakers and yielding depression instead of revolution.**
    - "The need to lend voice to suffering is a condition of all truth." —Theodor W. Adorno
    - "a very different and more numerous breed of ideologues promulgated the opposite message – that all was well with our deeply unequal society... embrace 'change', no matter how terrifying; grasp it as an opportunity." —Barbara Ehrenreich
    - Chronic pain and tiredness in the burnout society are apolitical 'I-tiredness'—symptoms of the overstretched narcissistic subject that isolate rather than bind into a 'We,' the best defence against revolution.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-009"></span>**True happiness cannot be reified or optimized because it inherently contains negativity: pain bears happiness and gives it endurance, so 'painful happiness' is no oxymoron and those closed to pain are closed to deep happiness.**
    - "[pain and happiness are] two siblings and twins who either grow up together or... remain small together!" —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Nietzsche: only one 'always open to pain from all sides and down to the deepest level' can be open to the subtlest and highest kinds of happiness.

## Survival

The palliative society reveals itself in the pandemic as a society of survival that sacrifices the good life, faith, enjoyment, and metaphysical meaning to the absolute value of prolonging bare biological life, so that algophobia is ultimately thanatophobia and life becomes indistinguishable from the undead proliferation of the virus.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-010"></span>**The society of survival grants survival an absolute value and sacrifices everything that makes life worth living—enjoyment, fundamental rights, faith—so that algophobia becomes thanatophobia and quarantine becomes a viral variant of the camp reducing life to bare life.**
    - During the pandemic churches suspended services even at Easter, priests wore masks and practised 'social distancing,' and virology deprived theology of its power as the narrative of resurrection gave way to the ideology of health.
    - The neoliberal labour camp is called the 'home office,' differing from despotic labour camps only in its ideology of health and the paradoxical freedom of self-exploitation.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-011"></span>**The mania for survival strips life of its metaphysical and narrative dimension, degrading it into a countable, measurable biological function via self-tracking and digital hypochondria, so that having lost life-stabilizing cultural practices we can no longer die at the right time and 'age without becoming old.'**
    - Life is no longer a matter of what can be recounted but of what can be counted; symbols, narratives, and rituals (such as ancestor worship that gave vitality even to the dead) have faded.
    - Whoever cannot die at the right time must perish in non-time.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-012"></span>**Capitalism lacks any narrative of the good life and elevates survival to an absolute value, accumulating capital as a 'capacity for survival' against death; the pandemic shocks but does not suspend capitalism, forcing production to stop without providing leisure, lingering, or a counter-narrative.**
    - The German 'Vermögen' means both 'capacity' and 'wealth'; capital is imagined as a capacity for survival, accumulated against death.
    - "There will not have been a viral revolution." —Byung-Chul Han
* <span id="the-palliative-society-013"></span>**Globalization was a de-immunizing process that dismantled barriers and weakened resistance to the other; the virus triggers an immunological crisis marking the 'return of the enemy,' returning society to the mode of immunological defence in a war against an invisible foe akin to the response to terrorism.**
    - The immunologically organized society of the Cold War was divided into fenced blocs; globalization dismantled such barriers to accelerate the flow of commodities and capital.
    - Like terrorism, which makes everyone at airports a potential terrorist subjected to humiliating security, the virus makes everyone a suspected carrier, leading toward a biopolitical surveillance regime.

## The Meaninglessness of Pain

Modern pain is experienced as meaningless because medicalization has stripped away the cultural and narrative programs that once situated it in a symbolic order, and this meaninglessness reflects that life itself—reduced to a bare biological process that no longer narrates—has become empty of meaning.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-014"></span>**Medicalization and pharmacology have destroyed all 'cultural programs for dealing with pain,' so contemporary pain lies outside the symbolic order as purely physical, mute agony; Valéry's Monsieur Teste, having lost the Christian narrative, embodies this modern subject for whom 'pain has no meaning.'**
    - "Pain has no meaning." —Paul Valéry (Monsieur Teste)
    - Valéry's sentence belongs beside Nietzsche's 'God is dead'; nineteenth-century physiologists removed Christian semantics from the cultural body, so 'the coldness of the cosmos penetrates our bones.'
    - Teresa of Ávila is the counter-image: for her the Christian narrative makes pain highly articulate and erotic, transforming the angel's fiery dart into a superabundant sweetness of union with God.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-015"></span>**Pain does not merely dam the flow of narrative but initiates it: following Benjamin's scene of the mother stroking and telling stories to a sick child, narration is a mental faculty that can heal by carrying pain along a 'navigable river' down to the sea, whereas reified bodily pain traps the sufferer in a mute, historyless present.**
    - "whether every illness might be cured if it could only float along the river of narrative – until it reached the mouth." —Walter Benjamin
    - For Freud psychogenic pain is a symptom of a block in the personal story; Monsieur Teste's pain is a 'thing,' a 'terrible object' that escapes narration.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-016"></span>**In a post-narrative age determined by counting rather than recounting, the retreating mind cedes power to the body and the threshold of pain at which the mind admits impotence lowers rapidly, producing a narcissistic, hypochondriac hypersensitivity.**
    - Andersen's The Princess and the Pea is an allegory of late-modern hypersensitivity: increasing suffering results from fewer and fewer causes ('princess-and-the-pea syndrome').
    - Docile bodies coping with much externally caused pain are less sensitive and outwardly directed; obsessive introspective listening to one's own body amplifies hypersensitivity.
    - "What is painful, after all, is the persisting meaninglessness of life itself." —Byung-Chul Han

## The Cunning of Pain

Following Jünger, pain is an ineradicable elementary force subject to a 'cunning of pain' that, when suppressed, disperses into life drop by drop, so that the pain-averse palliative society breeds silent chronic pain, self-harm, and isolation rooted in a vacuum of meaning that no analgesia can address.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-017"></span>**Jünger holds that pain is an ineradicable elementary force governed by a 'cunning of pain': suppressed pain amasses as 'hidden capital accruing compound interest' and permeates a sealed life drop by drop, so that widespread comfort merely disperses pain rather than defeating it.**
    - Jünger's image of Sinbad feasting on what turns out to be a whale's back—and of wanderers on a thawing frozen lake—figures humanity's false security over the elementary forces.
    - "Boredom is nothing other than the dissolution of pain in time." —Ernst Jünger
    - In the Anthropocene humans are more vulnerable than ever; the palaeontologist Andrew H. Knoll notes humans are 'evolution's icing' while 'the bacteria are the cake.'
* <span id="the-palliative-society-018"></span>**Alongside the negative violence of repression inflicted by others, the neoliberal performance society generates an auto-aggressive 'violence of positivity'—over-performance, over-communication, over-excitability—in which the performance subject wages war on itself, producing depression and chronic pain.**
    - The slave takes the whip from the master's hand and whips himself in order to become master and free; internal pressures plunge the subject into depression.
    - Self-harming 'cutting' is becoming a pandemic, with images of self-inflicted wounds circulating on social media as the 'bloody obverse of the selfie.'
* <span id="the-palliative-society-019"></span>**We are moving away from the 'primordial scene of healing'—Weizsäcker's image of a child soothing a sibling's pain by touch, making the little Samaritan the first doctor—as loneliness, competitiveness, and the weakening of solidarity amplify pain, and chronic pain and self-cutting become bodily cries for the touching hand of the other that no analgesia can replace.**
    - "the little Samaritan becomes the first doctor." —Viktor von Weizsäcker
    - Chronic pain's diverse aetiologies are intensified above all by the vacuum of meaning: it reflects a society devoid of meaning and a time without narration, which more analgesia or psychology only obscures.

## Pain as Truth

Following Weizsäcker, pain is the incarnation of truth: it reveals genuine relationships, articulates attachment, difference, and reality, so a painless palliative society is a truthless 'hell of the same' whose in-difference and unreality drive narcissistic selves to seek artificial pain and extremism.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-020"></span>**Pain is the 'incarnation of a truth' and a reliable criterion for distinguishing the genuine from the fake: only where a genuine belonging is threatened does separation hurt, so where a being can suffer pain it is truly integrated in living togetherness, and without pain we have neither loved nor lived.**
    - "Where these separations hurt, there the relationships had been genuine and had become incarnated." —Viktor von Weizsäcker
    - A lifeless, functional side-by-side feels no pain even as it falls apart, unlike a living togetherness.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-021"></span>**Pain is attachment, difference, and reality: it marks borders and articulates the body's 'organ dialects,' so a world without pain becomes a 'hell of the same' ruled by in-difference where the incomparable disappears, and love as painless consumption is the opposite of Eros as desiring the other.**
    - Alain Badiou cites a dating site slogan—'Get perfect love without suffering!'—as the elimination of the other as pain.
    - Han's wordplay: Hölle des Gleichen (hell of the same), Gleich-Gültigkeit (in-difference), das Unvergleichbare (the incomparable).
    - "only pain tells me how dear they are to me." —Viktor von Weizsäcker
* <span id="the-palliative-society-022"></span>**Because we perceive reality first as painful resistance, the palliative society's anaesthesia—intensified by digitalization and the ubiquity of the 'like'—makes the world unreal, and only a painful shock of reality (as with the virus, a viral 'anti-body') re-establishes reality against the post-factual apathy of fake news.**
    - 'Digitalization is anaesthetization'; Han's neologism 'anti-body' (Gegen-Körper) names a body that opposes us, distinct from an immunological antibody.
    - "The virus re-establishes reality." —Byung-Chul Han
* <span id="the-palliative-society-023"></span>**Pain intensifies self-perception and gives the self its contours, so when pain is removed the narcissistic, depressive self seeks artificial pain—extreme sports, risk-taking, drugs, violence, horror—to feel it exists; in the absence of a culture of pain a barbarism of extremists emerges.**
    - "Increasingly stronger stimuli are needed to provide people in an anesthetic society with any sense of being alive." —Ivan Illich
    - 'I feel pain, therefore I am': self-harm is a desperate attempt by the depressive self to reassure itself of its own existence.

## The Poetics of Pain

Great art and literature are born of pain, which enlivens the imagination and lets the mind erect a healing counter-world of beauty; the palliative society's general anaesthesia destroys this poetics of pain, permitting only a frictionless 'prose of pleasure' that perpetuates the hell of the same.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-024"></span>**Great writing is wrested from suffering: Kafka owed his writing to 'dark violence' that saved him from madness, Proust made his lifelong pain and even his hero Bergotte's death serve his writing, and Schubert's Winter Journey was born of syphilis and its torturous mercury therapy.**
    - "When I would sing of love, it turned to pain. And again, when I would sing of pain, it turned to love." —Franz Schubert
    - Proust corrected proofs of Bergotte's death scene on his own deathbed, 'slapping death in the face' by listening to the process of dying.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-025"></span>**Beauty is pain's complementary color: confronting pain, the mind imagines beauty and erects a healing, liveable counter-world, so that pain enlivens the imagination and art becomes, for Nietzsche, a 'saving sorceress' whose tragic, orgiastic feeling affirms life amid extreme suffering.**
    - "art [is a] saving sorceress with the power to heal." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Nietzsche derives the concept of 'tragic feeling' from the psychology of the orgiastic, 'where even pain acts as a stimulus.'
* <span id="the-palliative-society-026"></span>**Society's general anaesthesia abolishes the poetics of pain: because pain is made a matter of medicine alone, it is deprived of language and cannot become narrative; the noise of accelerated communication and the 'like' perpetuate the hell of the same, allowing only a 'prose of pleasure' rather than a language of pain.**
    - "For ten or twenty years almost nothing has happened in literature... The cause for this is a crisis in communication." —Michel Butor
    - Communication is fastest where the same meets the same; pain counteracts it, creating an 'inclination to fall silent' that would allow something wholly other to occur.

## The Dialectic of Pain

Following Hegel and Nietzsche, pain is the negativity that drives the mind's formation, experience, and genuine thought, distinguishing thinking from mere calculation; the palliative society's cult of health is non-dialectical and, fleeing negativity, forecloses transformation, revolution, and history.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-027"></span>**The mind is pain: following Hegel, the mind preserves itself in contradiction and pain, and its formative path (Bildungsweg) is a via dolorosa in which it wins truth only by 'looking the negative in the face and lingering with it,' while the positive that avoids the negative withers into 'dead being.'**
    - "The Other, the negative, contradiction, rupture, thus belongs to the nature of mind." —Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel
    - Genuine experience (Erfahrung) is a painful process of transformation, unlike a mere experienced event (Erlebnis) that amuses rather than transforms.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-028"></span>**The negativity of pain is constitutive of profound thought and distinguishes thinking from calculating and artificial intelligence: intelligence merely chooses between what is already there, while only what is living and can suffer pain can bring forth the wholly other, so there will be no 'pain algorithms' and no 'deep computing.'**
    - "we must constantly give birth to our thoughts out of our pain and maternally endow them with all that we have of blood, heart, fire, pleasure, passion, agony, conscience, fate, and disaster." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - AI may achieve deep learning but is incapable of experience; only pain can transform intelligence into mind.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-029"></span>**Health is non-dialectical, so the palliative society's cult of health is trapped in the hell of the same and lacks the transformative force of pain; Nietzsche derives a 'higher health' and his 'revaluation of all values' from pain, which grants 'dialectical clarity par excellence' and the ability to shift perspectives, without which there is no revolution or history.**
    - "Only great pain, that long, slow pain that takes its time, is the liberator of the spirit." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Nietzsche links the 'eternally creative' to 'that which must eternally destroy,' bound to pain, which compels the creator to feel previous things as untenable and ugly.

## The Ontology of Pain

Reading Heidegger against Jünger, Han develops an ontology in which 'being is pain'—the painful seam binding the readable to the unreadable and the near to the distant—arguing that the terrestrial order of concealment, care, and non-availability is being succeeded by an anaesthetic digital order of transparency, dataism, and total availability that forgets being.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-030"></span>**Against Jünger's reifying treatise on our 'relation to pain,' Heidegger seeks pain's essence through being, holding that 'being is pain': though immeasurable suffering rages over the earth, its essence remains concealed and we remain 'unpained,' not brought into the ownership of pain's essence.**
    - "Tell me your relation to being... and I will tell you how, and whether, you will 'concern' yourself with 'pain'." —Martin Heidegger
    - Heidegger criticizes Jünger's treatise for a 'decisively reifying attitude' that never treats of pain itself or asks after its essence.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-031"></span>**Pain is the seam through which the non-available breaks into thinking: moods pre-reflexively disclose the world, being 'de-termines' (be-stimmt) human Dasein through a silent voice, and only poetry lets us hear the 'ringing of stillness' when the word breaks, so that the 'remainder that can be sung' rhymes with pain.**
    - "The mood has already disclosed, in every case, Being-in-the-world as a whole." —Martin Heidegger
    - Han plays on the German Stimmung/bestimmen/Stimme cluster (mood, to determine, voice); Heidegger's 'seamstress' guards pain as the painful seam tying the readable to what can be sung.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-032"></span>**Pain is the fundamental mood of human finitude, thought from death ('Pain is death on a small scale'), and it bears the gravity of Dasein; the melancholy spirit (Schwermut) receives its gravity from pain, in which highest joy and deepest sadness play into each other by letting the near be remote and the remote near.**
    - "The play itself which attunes the two by letting the remote be near and the near be remote is pain." —Martin Heidegger
    - Death is 'the shrine of the nothing'; the unavailability of the other keeps Eros—the desire for an other who escapes my grasp—alive.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-033"></span>**The terrestrial order of concealment, mystery, and care—in which the earth is 'essentially self-secluding' and treating it with care demands distance—is being succeeded by a digital order whose telos is total availability and transparency, reifying language into information and substituting addition for narration under 'dataism.'**
    - "Earth shatters every attempt to penetrate it." —Martin Heidegger
    - The digital order transforms nearness into the painless absence of distance; algorithms make behaviour transparent, calculable, and controllable, and 'digital' means numerical.
    - Non-availability protects the other's alterity as a 'thou'; without 'primordial distance' the other is reified into an 'it' and consumed.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-034"></span>**Pain enables another kind of visibility, temporality, and perception that the anaesthetic digital order abolishes; against the compulsion of immediate access, patient 'intentionless waiting'—a renunciation that gives and makes us receptive to the non-available—reveals pain not as a lack but as a reception of being, and thus as a gift.**
    - The enduring and slow is a 'laggard' (Nachzügler), a 'lagging light' (Nachleuchter) that 'hesitates in withdrawing'; 'at once' is the temporality of the digital.
    - "The 'mournful bearing of the need to renounce and to give away' is a 'receiving'." —Martin Heidegger

## The Ethics of Pain

Ethical sensibility for the other depends on a primordial pain of exposure and vulnerability, but the palliative consumer society—through pornographic violence images, a deluge of pain that decouples perception from action, and the disappearance of the resistant other—produces a callused, narcissistic ego that replaces fear for others with diffuse fear for oneself.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-035"></span>**Whereas Jünger saw photography and film as disciplinary techniques that render the human eye insensitive to pain through a 'cruel way of seeing,' today's digital media are consumer rather than disciplinary media, turning images of violence into pornographic 'violence porn' that acts as an analgesic making us insensitive to the pain of others.**
    - Jünger: a camera 'captures a man at the moment an explosion tears him apart,' embodying a disciplined, cold, cruel seeing.
    - The deluge of pain and violence images decouples perception from action, so they no longer issue Sontag's moral imperative: 'put an end to this, intervene, act.'
* <span id="the-palliative-society-036"></span>**The decline of empathy stems not from voyeuristic pleasure but from the disappearance of the other: the palliative society eliminates the other as pain by reifying it into a consumable object stripped of its resistance, a tendency intensified by narcissism, digital availability, and pandemic 'social distancing' that becomes mental distancing.**
    - "The other as an object does not cause pain." —Byung-Chul Han
    - In the pandemic the suffering of others dissolves into 'numbers of cases'; people die alone in intensive care as nearness comes to mean infection.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-037"></span>**An ethical sensibility for the other presupposes a 'primordial pain' of exposure and vulnerability that precedes the ego's pleasure in itself: love as an emphatic relation 'invades and wounds us,' the painful wound is a primordial opening to the other, and the invulnerable, resilient performance subject with its 'being able to' destroys the other, who reveals themselves in 'being-able-not-to-be-able.'**
    - Levinas: sensibility requires an 'exposure' that 'offers itself even in suffering'; this pain toward the other is more passive than any passivity of the ego.
    - Love as consumption involves no injury and is free of pain, unlike love as an emphatic wounding relationship.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-038"></span>**Canetti's 'naked soul,' defenceless and vulnerable before the other, generates a restless fear for others that makes indifference impossible and teaches me who I am; today this nakedness is lost—the soul forms a callus—so that a definite fear for others gives way to a diffuse fear for oneself, cutting off access to the pain of the other.**
    - "Only in fear am I completely myself... But it is fear for others." —Elias Canetti
    - Digital bubbles increasingly shield us from the other; without pain toward the other we do not have access to the pain of the other.

## The Last Man

Nietzsche's pain-averse 'last man,' whom Fukuyama tied to liberal democracy, is really a phenomenon of modernity as such—thriving equally under autocracy—and the pandemic pushes the survival society toward a prototalitarian digital biopolitical surveillance regime, while transhumanism's dream of a pain-free, deathless life would abolish human life itself.

* <span id="the-palliative-society-039"></span>**Fukuyama's The End of History is pervaded by ambivalence: liberal democracy, by purging heroic megalothymia and substituting isothymia and rational consumption, produces Nietzsche's pain-averse 'last man'—a society of permanent anaesthetization that 'invented happiness' and blinks.**
    - "'We invented happiness' say the last human beings, and they blink." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - "to the extent that liberal democracy is successful at purging megalothymia from life and substituting for it rational consumption, we will become last men." —Francis Fukuyama
* <span id="the-palliative-society-040"></span>**Contrary to both Fukuyama and Jünger, the last man is not bound to liberal democracy but is a phenomenon of modernity as such—today China is as populated by last men as the US—so as a survival society the palliative society does not depend on liberal democracy and, under the pandemic, drifts toward a biopolitical surveillance regime that abandons liberal principles.**
    - Jünger wrongly predicted the last man's age already lay behind us and invoked its end against Bellamy's pain-free utopia; in fact the twenty-first century is precisely the age of the last man, and the heroic interwar age was a brief episode.
    - In the fight against the virus the focus must be on individual behaviour, but biopolitical surveillance of the individual does not sit well with liberalism.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-041"></span>**Domination becomes total when it coincides with freedom: in the digital 'dialectic of freedom,' limitless communication turns into total surveillance and capitalism becomes surveillance capitalism, so the last man—valuing comfort and health over freedom—erects an internal dictatorship, monitors himself, and feels free precisely under a biopolitical regime imposed in the name of health.**
    - In the 1980s West German census, even schoolchildren protested the collection of innocent data like profession and religion; today we voluntarily give away the most intimate data out of an inner need.
    - Naomi Klein: the moment of shock is the perfect opportunity to establish a new system of domination; digital biopolitics closes capitalism's systemic gap against the pandemic.
* <span id="the-palliative-society-042"></span>**Transhumanism (Pearce's Hedonistic Imperative) promises to eradicate the biological substrates of suffering, love's cruelties, and even boredom through biotechnology, but a pain-free life of permanent happiness is not human life: because death and pain belong together, abolishing pain abolishes death and yields only 'undead life,' so in seeking to survive, humans abolish themselves.**
    - "Over the next thousand years or so, the biological substrates of suffering will be eradicated completely." —David Pearce
    - "They may succeed in becoming immortal, but only at the expense of life itself." —Byung-Chul Han
