# Psychopolitics

## Metadata
- Thesis: Neoliberalism has replaced the coercive, disciplinary power that repressed freedom with a 'smart' psychopolitical power that exploits freedom itself, turning subjects into self-optimizing, self-exploiting 'projects' whose emotions, data, and psyches are mined for profit while the feeling of freedom masks a new and more total subjugation.
- Topics: neoliberalism and self-exploitation, psychopolitics vs. biopolitics, Big Data and digital surveillance, emotional capitalism, freedom, immanence, and resistance
- Categories: philosophy, political science, social science
- Author: Byung-Chul Han
- Year: 2014
- Collections: Byung-Chul Han
- Mode: quick

## The Crisis of Freedom

Freedom under neoliberalism switches over into compulsion because the imperative of 'Can' generates unlimited self-coercion, transforming the exploited subject into an auto-exploiting 'project' who is master and slave in one, while transparency and Big Data convert this exploited freedom into total control and the end of free will.

* <span id="psychopolitics-001"></span>**Neoliberal subjects no longer experience themselves as subjugated 'subjects' but as free 'projects' constantly reinventing themselves; yet this self-projection is a more efficient form of subjugation because the freedom of 'Can' has no limit, so its compulsion is unlimited and manifests pathologically as depression and burnout.**
    - 'Should' has a limit and issues prohibitions; 'Can' has no limit, so the compulsion it entails is boundless.
    - The 'I' as a project deems itself free of external limits but subjugates itself to internal self-constraints of compulsive achievement and optimization.
* <span id="psychopolitics-002"></span>**The achievement-subject exploits itself without a master and is therefore an 'absolute slave'; neoliberalism transforms allo-exploitation into classless auto-exploitation, making a 'dictatorship of the proletariat' structurally impossible because the isolated, self-combating entrepreneur turns aggression against himself, producing depression rather than revolution.**
    - Under allo-exploitation the exploited can show solidarity and unite; under auto-exploitation people blame themselves, feel shame, and cannot form a 'political We'.
    - "It is not the individuals who are set free by free competition; it is, rather, capital which is set free." —Karl Marx
    - Negri's cooperative 'Multitude' misdescribes the present: conditions are defined by the solitude of the isolated, auto-exploiting entrepreneur.
* <span id="psychopolitics-003"></span>**Capital functions as a new transcendence and a new God that generates its own needs and makes us perpetually guilty and debt-ridden; following Benjamin, capitalism is a religion that creates guilt without atonement, so the state of unfreedom perpetuates itself.**
    - "the first case of a cult that creates guilt, not atonement" —Walter Benjamin
    - Politicians invoke high debt to explain their restricted freedom to act; we may run up debts perpetually precisely so we need not be free and responsible.
* <span id="psychopolitics-004"></span>**The digital panopticon surpasses Bentham's because inmates voluntarily communicate and expose themselves, outsourcing surveillance to themselves; the dispositive of transparency exteriorizes persons, strips away interiority and otherness, and enforces total conformity as 'everyone watches over everyone else'.**
    - Bentham's panopticon isolated inmates and forbade interaction; the digital panopticon thrives on inmates who collaborate through voluntary self-illumination and self-exposure.
    - Transparency turns everything into freely circulating, context-free information, accelerating communication by removing thresholds, secrets, and interiority.
* <span id="psychopolitics-005"></span>**Neoliberalism turns citizens into passive consumers, producing a 'spectator democracy' in which transparency serves to scandalize politicians rather than inform political decisions, while digital psychopolitics and Big Data move from passive surveillance to active steering, positivizing persons into calculable 'things' and ending the person with free will.**
    - The smartphone is a 'devotional object of the Digital' and a mobile confessional; 'Like' is the digital 'Amen', and Facebook is the church of the Digital.
    - The 1984 German Federal Constitutional Court census ruling on informational self-determination is now obsolete because people voluntarily expose themselves without external constraint.
    - Big Data is a psychopolitical instrument that predicts behaviour, making the future calculable and enabling pre-reflexive intervention in the psyche.

## Smart Power

The highest form of power is invisible and permissive rather than coercive; neoliberal 'smart power' sheds negativity, presents itself as freedom, and guides the will seductively toward self-optimization, making people dependent rather than compliant.

* <span id="psychopolitics-006"></span>**The greater power is, the more quietly it works; power based on violence is weak, so neoliberal power abandons the inhibitive negativity of discipline and becomes permissive, friendly, and seductive, saying 'yes' more than 'no' and meeting the subject halfway to exploit rather than repress freedom.**
    - Disciplinary power operates by forbidding and depriving (making people compliant); smart power pleases and fulfils (making people dependent).
    - Free choice (Wahl) is eliminated to make way for free selection (Auswahl) from among items on offer.
* <span id="psychopolitics-007"></span>**Neoliberalism is the 'capitalism of Like': its signal and seal is the Like button, through which people subjugate themselves by consuming and communicating, and it requires no expenditure of energy or violence because it simply happens through voluntary self-organization.**
    - "Protect me from what I want" —Byung-Chul Han

## The Mole and the Snake

Deleuze's shift from disciplinary confinement to control society is figured as the passage from the mole to the snake: neoliberalism replaces the confined, subjugated labourer-subject with the open, self-propelling entrepreneur-project, a mutation and intensification of capitalism governed by psychopolitics rather than biopolitics.

* <span id="psychopolitics-008"></span>**The disciplinary mole moves within closed systems of confinement while the neoliberal snake makes space through its own movement; the passage from mole (subject/labourer) to snake (project/entrepreneur) is not a new way of life but an intensification of capitalism that eliminates the mole's productivity limits.**
    - Deleuze's 'Postscript on the Societies of Control' diagnoses a general crisis of all milieus of confinement whose rigidity no longer suits networked immaterial production.
    - The disciplinary regime organizes as a 'body' (biopolitical); the neoliberal regime seems like a 'soul', governed by psychopolitics through motivation, competition, and optimization.
    - "constantly introducing an inexorable rivalry presented as healthy competition … wonderful motivation" —Gilles Deleuze

## Biopolitics

Foucault's biopolitics, tied to disciplinary capitalism's administration of the biological body and population, cannot describe the neoliberal regime, which has discovered the psyche as its productive force; where demography and statistics fail, Big Data can construct collective psychograms and exploit the unconscious.

* <span id="psychopolitics-009"></span>**Foucault traces the shift from sovereign power (the might of the sword, the right to deal death) to disciplinary power (a power over life that invests and administers bodies), which is normative, orthopaedic, and yokes the body into a docility-utility relation for industrial production.**
    - Sovereign power seizes life to suppress it; disciplinary power invests life through and through via the calculated management of life.
    - "methods which [make] possible the meticulous control of the operations of the body … [imposing] upon them a relation of docility-utility" —Michel Foucault
    - Bentham's optical panopticon observes only from outside and has no access to inner thoughts or needs; disciplinary power is too crude to penetrate the deeper layers of the soul.
* <span id="psychopolitics-010"></span>**Biopolitics, based on population statistics and demography, has no access to the psychic realm and cannot produce a psychogram; Big Data, by contrast, can establish an individual and collective psychogram—perhaps even a psychogram of the unconscious itself—and so may exploit the psyche entirely.**
    - Demography is not psychography; statistics and Big Data lie worlds apart because only Big Data can shine a light into the depths of the psyche.

## Foucault's Dilemma

Foucault failed to make the turn to psychopolitics because he clung to the disciplinary categories of population and biopolitics when analyzing neoliberalism, and did not see that the neoliberal regime co-opts the 'technologies of the self' so that self-optimization becomes a highly efficient mode of domination and self-exploitation.

* <span id="psychopolitics-011"></span>**Foucault's 1978–9 lectures set out to analyze biopolitics but ended up discussing neoliberalism without ever recognizing that population and biopolitics—genuine categories of disciplinary society—cannot describe the neoliberal regime, so he never made the necessary turn to psychopolitics.**
    - "I really did intend to talk about biopolitics, and then, things being what they are, I have ended up talking at length … about neoliberalism." —Michel Foucault
    - Against Agamben, Foucault's early death did not prevent him from developing biopolitics but from abandoning it; today's homines sacri are not shut out of the system but shut into it.
* <span id="psychopolitics-012"></span>**Neoliberalism, a mutated capitalism, discovers the psyche as productive force: immaterial production means physical discipline gives way to mental optimization, the docile body is released from production and becomes an object of aesthetic and health optimization (fitness, cosmetic surgery), and sexiness and fitness become economic resources.**
    - Foucault ties biopower to the biological, somatic, and corporal; neoliberalism produces immaterial objects like information and programs, so orthopaedic intervention yields to aesthetic intervention.
    - Stiegler rightly says biopower is no longer the force behind our age and proposes 'psychotechnological psychopower,' but his overemphasis on television and neglect of the internet and social media makes him miss neoliberal psychopolitics.
* <span id="psychopolitics-013"></span>**The blind spot in Foucault's late 'technologies of the self' is that the neoliberal regime utterly claims them for its own purposes: perpetual self-optimization and the self-as-work-of-art are a deceptive illusion in which self-optimization and submission, freedom and exploitation, fall into one to effect self-exploitation.**
    - "The art of living is the art of killing psychology... intentional and voluntary actions by which men … seek to transform themselves … to make their life into an oeuvre" —Michel Foucault
    - Neoliberal power ensures individuals act on themselves so that power relations are interiorized and then interpreted as freedom.

## Healing as Killing

Neoliberal self-optimization, dressed up as therapeutic 'healing,' serves only to make people function perfectly within the system; because it demands boundless positivity and eliminates the negativity that keeps the soul alive, it amounts to total self-exploitation that leads to mental collapse—healing turns out to mean killing.

* <span id="psychopolitics-014"></span>**The neoliberal imperative of self-optimization is driven not by concern for the good life but by systemic market constraints that make everything comparable, measurable, and subject to therapeutic elimination of weakness; inaugurating an 'age of exhaustion' where the psyche itself is exploited and depression and burnout define the times.**
    - Self-management workshops, motivational retreats, and personality/mental-training seminars capitalize on the whole person and their entire life, not just working time.
    - Sovereignty was the age of levying, disciplinary society the age of production; the neoliberal regime destroys value at the root and inaugurates the age of exhaustion.
* <span id="psychopolitics-015"></span>**The ideology of self-optimization has religious, fanatical traits resembling Protestant self-examination—hunting down negative thoughts instead of sins—and it exploits even pain; but the violence of positivity is as destructive as the violence of negativity, because without negativity life degrades and the soul, which owes its depth to suffering, collapses.**
    - "That tension of the soul in unhappiness which cultivates its strength … was it not granted through suffering, through the discipline of great suffering?" —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - "There is a power in the pressure of dissatisfaction, in the tension of temporary discomfort. This is the kind of pain you want in your life." —Tony Robbins
    - 'Healing' is the magic word of American self-help; the soul is not a machine of positivity (Positivmaschine), so healing turns out to mean killing.

## Shock

Naomi Klein's shock doctrine, which parallels Ewen Cameron's electroshock 'depatterning' with Milton Friedman's disaster-driven neoliberalism, mistakes a fundamentally disciplinary technique of negativity for the workings of neoliberal psychopolitics, which operates through positivity, seduction, and Liking rather than shock, paralysis, and bitter medicine.

* <span id="psychopolitics-016"></span>**Klein's two 'Doctor Shocks'—the CIA-funded psychiatrist Ewen Cameron, who used electroshock, sensory deprivation, and drug-induced sleep to erase memory and rebuild a 'clean slate' personality, and Milton Friedman, who saw post-catastrophe shock as the ideal moment to depattern societies into pure capitalism—share a dream of returning to a 'natural' prelapsarian state through deliberately inflicted painful shocks.**
    - Cameron's isolation-chamber panopticon plunged patients into chaos for up to thirty days—hands in cardboard tubes, forbidden to speak—as a cruel Cold War immunological intervention against the Other/Enemy.
    - "only 'bitter medicine' could clear those distortions and bad patterns out of the way" —Naomi Klein
* <span id="psychopolitics-017"></span>**Klein's theory of shock blinds her to actual neoliberal psychopolitics: electroshock is a disciplinary, orthopaedic, biopolitical technique whose essential trait is negativity (paralyzing and annihilating psychic contents), whereas neoliberal psychopolitics is SmartPolitics—dominated by positivity, positive stimuli, and Liking, seducing and pre-empting the soul rather than shocking it.**
    - Instead of administering 'bitter medicine' it enlists Liking; instead of depatterning desires it protocols and anticipates them through calculated prognoses, acting ahead of actions rather than cancelling them out.

## Friendly Big Brother

Orwell's negative surveillance state of scarcity, torture, and destroyed words is fundamentally different from the digital panopticon, which operates through positivity, superabundance, and voluntary self-exposure; Big Brother now wears a friendly face, and the very feeling of freedom—absent in Orwell—is precisely what makes the new surveillance so efficient.

* <span id="psychopolitics-018"></span>**Orwell's 1984 is dominated by Cold War negativity—Newspeak destroys words to abolish free thought, the Ministry of Plenty maintains artificial scarcity, and psychotechnical brainwashing uses electroshock and torture—whereas today's society multiplies words without end, maximizes consumption, and generates a superabundance of positivity.**
    - Orwell's state produces scarcity, represses needs, and extracts confession by force; the digital panopticon maximizes consumption, stimulates needs, and relies on voluntary disclosure.
    - Smartphones have replaced torture chambers; transparency and information have taken the place of truth and the Ministry of Truth.
* <span id="psychopolitics-019"></span>**Bentham's interiorized Big Brother made inmates feel watched, but occupants of the digital panopticon never feel watched or threatened and instead feel free; this feeling of freedom is now the problem, because self-exploitation and self-illumination follow the same logic of voluntarily laying oneself bare.**
    - Apple's legendary 1984 Superbowl ad cast the company as liberator from the Orwellian state, yet 1984 marked not the end of surveillance but the inception of a control society that surpasses Orwell—now everyone is his or her own panopticon.

## Emotional Capitalism

The contemporary boom in emotion derives from neoliberalism's discovery that emotions—being dynamic, performative, and pre-reflexive—can be deployed as productive resources; as immaterial production and acceleration render rationality too slow and rigid, emotionality is exploited to boost productivity and consumption and to steer the whole person at a pre-reflexive level.

* <span id="psychopolitics-020"></span>**Han distinguishes feeling, emotion, affect, and atmosphere/mood: feeling is objective, constative, narratable, and enduring (a matter of 'wherefore'); emotion is subjective, performative, dynamic, and directional (a 'whither'); affect is eruptive and momentary; and mood is a wholly objective state one finds oneself in (a 'where')—distinctions that emotion researchers and Eva Illouz confuse.**
    - One has a 'feel for language' (Sprachgefühl) or 'com-passion' (Mitgefühl) but never an 'emotion for language' or 'com-emotion'; anxiety (a feeling without an object) differs from fear (intentional).
    - Digital communication is an affect-medium fostering immediate catharsis; shitstorms are streams of affect, exemplary of digital communication.
* <span id="psychopolitics-021"></span>**Illouz's Cold Intimacies fails to explain the emotion boom because it conflates feeling and emotion and roots emotional capitalism in Weber's ascetic accumulation-capitalism, whereas emotion becomes a means of production only with the switch to immaterial, consumer capitalism that sells emotive and cultic value rather than use value.**
    - Weber's Protestant ethic describes rational ascetic accumulation, which does not feed into emotion-driven consumer capitalism; anxiety is a feeling, not an emotion.
    - "canonical sociological accounts of modernity contain … numerous references to them: anxiety, love, competitiveness, indifference, guilt" —Eva Illouz
* <span id="psychopolitics-022"></span>**Because the neoliberal economy dismantles continuity and integrates instability, and because acceleration favors fast emotionality over slow rationality, a 'dictatorship of emotion' arises: consumer capitalism uses Emotional Design to mould emotional patterns so that we consume emotions (which are limitless) rather than things, opening a boundless new field of consumption.**
    - Rationality is objective, steady, and slow (the medium of disciplinary society); emotionality is subjective, situative, volatile, and fast, arising when circumstances change.
    - Corporate management shifts from rational technique to emotional management; managers become motivation coaches, as at Daimler-Chrysler (assessing social/emotional skills) and Hewlett-Packard ('an affective relationship').
    - Emotions are steered by the limbic system where the drives are seated, so psychopolitics seizes emotion to cut deep and steer the integral person on a pre-reflexive, half-conscious level.

## Gamification

Emotional capitalism gamifies work and communication, exploiting homo ludens so that the player subjugates himself in the very act of playing; against this, true freedom would come only from the 'Other of Work'—an unproductive mode of living, profanation, and play that restores immanence and escapes the theology of Capital.

* <span id="psychopolitics-023"></span>**Gamification lends work an emotional, dramatic charge with immediate success and reward to generate more motivation and yield, exploiting homo ludens who subjugates himself in the very act of playing; but this destroys human communication and destroys play's own liberating potential, since anything that matures slowly (like farming) cannot be gamified.**
    - Hunting matches the game's temporality of immediate reward; farming, which depends on slow ripening, cannot be gamified—and life cannot be turned into hunting alone.
    - The gamification logic of 'Likes', 'Friends' and 'Followers' plugs social communication into a game mode whose corollary is commercialization.
* <span id="psychopolitics-024"></span>**Against the Krisis-Group's Manifesto against Labour (which condemns the Left for mystifying labour instead of seeking liberation from labour) and Marx's own primacy of labour (where free time becomes 'human capital' feeding fixed capital), true freedom requires a mode of living that is no longer a mode of production—something altogether unproductive that makes use of the useless.**
    - "A corpse is running society – the corpse of labour" —Krisis-Group / Robert Kurz
    - Marx capitalizes even leisure: 'idle time' as 'time for higher activity' transforms its possessor into a more productive subject and contributes to the production of fixed capital, so the realm of necessity colonizes the realm of freedom.
    - True freedom would come only if life were entirely freed from Capital as transcendence; freedom is based on luxuriance—deviation, getting away from necessity—which stands close to asceticism.
* <span id="psychopolitics-025"></span>**Profanation—returning things withdrawn to the gods to free human use—is a practice of freedom that opens a 'playful margin of immanence' liberating us from transcendence and subjectivation, exemplified by Greek children who found banknotes in ruins and played with them, tearing them apart as a sign from the future.**
    - In debt-crippled Greece, children found a bundle of banknotes in a ruined house and profaned money by playing with it and tearing it to shreds—an emblematic sign of a future in which 'the world lies in rubble; in the ruins, we are playing too'.
    - Following Agamben, religion (relegere) means watchful separation of the holy; profanation is an 'attitude of conscious negligence' toward that watchfulness. Heidegger's late 'time-play-space' (Zeit-Spiel-Raum) gestures toward a realm of the event beyond labour where psychology is surpassed.

## Big Data

Big Data functions as an aperspectival digital panopticon and a 'Dataism' second Enlightenment that promises absolute, transparent, theory-free knowledge; but because it is purely additive correlation without narration, causality, or Hegelian Spirit, it amounts to absolute ignorance and remains blind to the singular event that actually shapes the future.

* <span id="psychopolitics-026"></span>**Digital surveillance is 'Columbus's egg' for control society because it is aperspectival: unlike Bentham's perspectival optics with unavoidable blind spots, digital optics eliminates all blind spots, watches from every angle, and can peer into the human soul itself.**
    - Acxiom promises clients a '360-degree customer view'; in US elections data-mining yields highly precise voter profiles enabling micro-targeting as data-driven psychopolitics that may outpace free will and herald the end of freedom.
* <span id="psychopolitics-027"></span>**'Dataism' arrives as a second Enlightenment, echoing the first Enlightenment's statistical faith (Voltaire) with the buzzword transparency; but though it claims to leave ideology behind, dataism is itself an ideology of data totalitarianism, and by the Dialectic of Enlightenment its false clarity is a new myth leading to the 'barbarism of data'—Dataism is digital Dadaism and nihilism.**
    - "With enough data, the numbers speak for themselves." —Chris Anderson
    - "False clarity is only another name for myth." —Theodor Adorno / Max Horkheimer
    - Numbers and data become sexualized and fetishized in the 'Quantified Self'; 'datasexuals' find data 'sexy' and the digitus starts to play the part of the phallus.
* <span id="psychopolitics-028"></span>**The 'Quantified Self' ('Self Knowledge through Numbers') is a Dadaist technology that empties the self of meaning, because counting is not recounting: self-knowledge derives from narrative account, and self-tracking, unlike the ancient publicatio sui tied to truth and ethics, is mere self-surveillance in which the subject is both perpetrator and victim, a panopticon of itself.**
    - "keeping notebooks in order to reactivate for oneself the truths one needed" —Michel Foucault
    - The auto-exploiting entrepreneur of the self carries around its own labour camp and its own internal panopticon, where there is no difference between guard and inmate.
* <span class="pull-quote">Totalized data-knowledge amounts to absolute ignorance: the absolute zero of Spirit.</span>
* <span id="psychopolitics-029"></span>**The extension of Web 2.0 (internet of persons) to Web 3.0 (internet of things) completes digital control society by logging life in full: unlike forgetful Big Brother, Big Data never forgets, and it generates a digital class society via a 'ban-opticon' that scores people and excludes those without economic value as 'waste'.**
    - Acxiom trades the data of ~300 million US citizens in seventy categories: high-value consumers are 'Shooting Star' (36–45, married, no kids, jog, travel, watch Seinfeld) while low-value people are designated 'waste'/'trash' and denied credit.
    - The classical panopticon disciplined incarcerated prisoners; the ban-opticon identifies and excludes those outside or hostile to the system to ensure its security and efficiency.
* <span id="psychopolitics-030"></span>**Digital memory differs fundamentally from human memory: human memory (and autobiography) is a living, narrative process of retranscription and rearrangement that requires forgetting, whereas digital memory is seamless additive accumulation of 'undead' points of presence that lacks the temporality of the living, so digitalized life lacks animacy.**
    - "memory is present not once but several times over, that it is laid down in various kinds of indications" —Sigmund Freud
    - Big Data may function like a movie camera, disclosing a 'digital unconscious' of micro-actions ('actomes') and collective behaviour patterns—turning the Freudian id into an exploitable ego.
* <span id="psychopolitics-031"></span>**Big Data promises absolute knowledge but delivers only correlation, the most primitive level of Hegel's Logic; because it lacks causality, reciprocity, and above all the comprehending 'Concept' (the syllogism/Schluss that gathers elements into a meaningful whole), it lacks Spirit—purely additive data never reaches a conclusion, and totalized data-knowledge coincides with absolute ignorance.**
    - Correlation says A often occurs with B (probability, not necessity); causality says A causes B; only the Concept comprehends A and B as moments of a higher whole. Big Data replaces 'How so?' with 'That's-how-it-is'.
    - "The syllogism is what is rational, and it is everything that is rational" —G.W.F. Hegel
    - Because everything becomes additive and all narrative/vertical tension is lost, total acceleration sets in; the age of Big Data is 'an epoch without reason'—Anderson's 'end of theory' means taking leave of Spirit.
* <span id="psychopolitics-032"></span>**Today's Big Data euphoria repeats the eighteenth century's short-lived statistical euphoria (Arbuthnot's divine providence, Kant's teleological history, Rousseau's communication-free volonté générale) but data-mining is only statistics: it calculates averages and is blind to the singular; since only the improbable event shapes the future, Big Data is blind to the future, and (following Nietzsche) statistical reason only proves the growing sameness of the herd.**
    - "Statistics proves that there are laws in history. Yes, it proves how vulgar and disgustingly uniform the masses are." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Romanticism's fundamental affect is horror at the average and normal; Nietzsche's phrase 'die Menschen zunehmen im Gleichwerden' means both that people grow equal and 'batten' (grow fat) as they become the same—a Gleichschaltung intensified by dataism.
    - "Calculators, it is now up to you: count, measure, compare." —Jean-Jacques Rousseau

## Beyond the Subject

Against psychopolitical steering, which perpetuates the system by ensnaring the subject ever deeper in subjection, the event and transformative 'experience' (Erfahrung) wrench the subject out of itself, so that the art of living becomes a practice of freedom through de-psychologization and de-subjectivation.

* <span id="psychopolitics-033"></span>**The event—following Nietzsche's 'naturification' and Foucault's genealogy—is the absolutely sudden, incalculable reversal of a relationship of forces that annuls the standing order, brings an 'outside' that breaks the subject open, and calls a wholly different constellation of Being into place.**
    - "Event means the reversal of a relationship of forces, the usurpation of power, the appropriation of a vocabulary against those who had once used it" —Michel Foucault
    - Erlebnis (experiencing) keeps the subject in subjection; Erfahrung (experience) is founded on discontinuity and transformation, wrenching the subject from itself.
* <span id="psychopolitics-034"></span>**The art of living as a practice of freedom must proceed by de-psychologization—the 'art of killing psychology'—which disarms neoliberal psychopolitics as a means of submission; when the subject is de-psychologized and 'de-voided' (ent-leert), it opens onto an unnamed mode of existence, an unwritten future.**
    - "The art of living is the art of killing psychology, of creating with oneself and with others unnamed individualities, beings, relations, qualities." —Michel Foucault

## Idiotism

Idiotism—philosophy's original heretical vocation of playing the fool—is a practice of freedom against total communication and surveillance: by refusing consensus, immunosuppression, and networked intelligence, the idiot guards silence and solitude and gains access to the vertical dimension, the event, and pure immanence as 'a life'.

* <span id="psychopolitics-035"></span>**Philosophy has always been tied to idiotism: every philosopher who creates a new idiom is an idiot (Socrates, Descartes' idiotic cogito), and idiotism discloses a field of immanence of events and singularities that eludes subjectivation and psychologization; but digital networking has massively amplified the compulsion to conform and suppressed idiotisms through the violence of consensus.**
    - "To the letter, I'd say they play the fool [ils font les idiots]. Playing the fool, that's always been one function of philosophy." —Gilles Deleuze
    - "it's as if everyone else were speaking in subtle attunement with each other: brought down to the most agreeable level" —Botho Strauss
    - Deleuze's old idiot wanted indubitable truths through doubt; the new idiot wills the absurd, turning it into the highest power of thought.
* <span id="psychopolitics-036"></span>**The idiot is a modern heretic (heresy means 'choice'/free choice) who resists coercive communication through idiosyncrasy—an immunological defence against the Other that acceleration requires be suppressed; against the 'Inferno of the Same' where communication of the Same reaches maximum velocity, idiotism creates 'spaces for guarding silence' and solitude where what truly deserves to be said can be said.**
    - "Repressive forces don't stop people from expressing themselves but rather force them to express themselves. What a relief to have nothing to say, the right to say nothing" —Gilles Deleuze
    - Immunosuppression of idiosyncratic immune responses is necessary so information circulates faster and capital accelerates; communication is fastest when the Same reacts to the Same.
* <span id="psychopolitics-037"></span>**Whereas intelligence merely chooses between options within a system (inter-legere) and is confined to the horizontal, system-immanent plane, the idiot savant abandons intelligence for the vertical dimension and the wholly Other, becoming a receptive 'antenna' or stylite for events and 'broadcasts from the future'.**
    - "Stylite, pillar dweller, antenna. The waves of the overflowing message generate, in the mouth of the holy man, the same noise as the faint signals the idiot receives from the world." —Byung-Chul Han
* <span id="psychopolitics-038"></span>**Following Deleuze's Pure Immanence, the idiot accesses 'a life'—the immanence of immanence, self-sufficient and subject to nothing, upon which no order of domination can be erected—whereas Capital is a transcendence that estranges life from itself; the idiot, defined by singularity rather than subjectivity and akin to the child, is the matrix of de-subjectivation and de-psychologization.**
    - "We will say of pure immanence that it is A LIFE, and nothing else. … A life is the immanence of immanence, absolute immanence: it is complete power, complete bliss." —Gilles Deleuze
    - Very small children resemble one another with hardly any individuality but have singularities—a smile, a gesture—infused with an immanent life that is pure power and bliss; the idiot is like Deleuze's 'homo tantum', 'more like a flower: an existence simply open to light'.
