# Introduction to Christianity

## Metadata
- Thesis: Christianity centers on the fundamental decision that the invisible divine reality supports all visible existence, revealed definitively through Jesus Christ who embodies both the personal and transcendent nature of God as Trinity. This faith requires ongoing conversion and represents humanity's dialogical relationship with God, expressed through the Church community and sustained by the Holy Spirit.
- Topics: Trinity and divine nature, Christology and incarnation, faith versus reason, Church community and sacraments, resurrection and eternal life
- Categories: religion, philosophy, history
- Collections: Christianity
- Author: Pope Benedict XVI
- Year: 1968

## Introduction: I Believe, Amen

### Chapter I: Belief in the World of Today

### 1. Doubt and Beliefmans Situation before the Question of God

- The parable of the clown and the burning village reveals theology's crisis of credibility
    - The clown (theologian) cannot make people take his urgent message seriously
    - His costume and role predetermine how he will be received
    - Simple costume changes (aggiornamento, demythologization) cannot solve the deeper problem
- The situation is more complex than the parable suggests
    - Both believer and unbeliever share doubt and uncertainty
    - The believer is threatened by uncertainty and temptation (example of Saint Thérèse of Lisieux)
    - The unbeliever is troubled by doubts about his unbelief
- Paul Claudel's image of the shipwrecked Jesuit
    - "Fastened to the cross, but the cross fastened to nothing, drifting over the abyss"
    - Captures the modern believer's situation of uncertainty
- The dilemma is unavoidable: "Anyone who makes up his mind to evade the uncertainty of belief will have to experience the uncertainty of unbelief"
- Martin Buber's Jewish story: the terrible "perhaps" that breaks resistance
- Both believer and unbeliever must face doubt as "the unavoidable temptation"

### 2. the Origin of Beliefprovisional Attempt at a Definition of Belief

- Christian faith centers on the decision that "I believe" expresses a fundamental choice about reality
- Belief signifies the view that the invisible represents true reality
    - What cannot be seen supports and makes possible all visible reality
    - Requires an "about-turn" or conversion from natural inclination toward the visible
- Belief has always involved adventure and risk
    - Not the "natural" attitude corresponding to human inclination
    - Demands daily renewal and lifelong conversion
- Modern difficulty is compounded by the gulf between "then" and "now"
    - Belief appears old-fashioned, bound to yesterday
    - Tradition vs. progress creates additional stumbling block
- Christian "positivism": the scandal of logos becoming sarx (word becoming flesh)
    - God encountered in history, in the man Jesus
    - Creates the "death of God" as God becomes killable
- Historical-critical method creates new barriers
    - Like physics, limits itself to the "phenomenal"
    - Can see Jesus but has difficulty discovering Christ
    - Historical science both reveals and conceals history

### 3. the Dilemma of Belief in the World of Today

- Attempts to escape the faith-history dilemma take various forms:
    - Establishing Christology on demonstrable historical grounds
    - Reducing Christianity to what is historically verifiable
    - Escaping history altogether as irrelevant to faith
- The modern "Jesus or Christ" dilemma
    - Flight from Christ to the "historical Jesus" (Harnack)
    - Flight from Jesus back to pure kerygma (Bultmann)
    - Return to Jesus as symbol after "death of God"
- All approaches fail because Jesus and Christ cannot be separated
    - One is bound to be pushed from one to the other
    - Jesus only subsists as the Christ, Christ only as Jesus

### 4. the Boundary of the Modern Understanding of Reality and the Place of Belief

#### a. The first stage: The birth of the historical approach

- Vico's revolutionary formula: "verum quia factum" (true because made)
    - Replaces medieval "verum est ens" (being is truth)
    - We can truly know only what we have made ourselves
    - Marks end of old metaphysics, beginning of modern attitude
- Medieval view: all being is thought by God, therefore meaningful and knowable
- Modern view: human work becomes the realm of certain knowledge
    - History becomes a true science alongside mathematics
    - Everything is historicized (philosophy, theology, economics, natural science)
- Man can only know his own work but must accept himself as chance product

#### b. The second stage: The turn toward technical thinking

- Marx's formula: "verum quia faciendum" (true because to be made)
    - Philosophy must change the world, not just interpret it
    - Truth lies in the makable, the feasible
    - Future and action become primary over past accomplishment
- The faciendum (makable) takes precedence over factum (made)
    - Repeatable experiment becomes the criterion
    - Techne becomes the real potential and obligation of man
    - Future orientation replaces concern with past or eternal

#### c. The question of the place of belief

- Both movements (historical and technical) contain Christian elements
    - Christianity lives on history and aims at transformation
    - But exclusive adoption of either approach conceals faith's true meaning
- Belief belongs to the "stand-understand" relationship, not "know-make"
    - Two complementary approaches to human existence
    - Heidegger's distinction between calculating and reflective thought
- Both approaches are necessary but neither can absorb the other

### 5. Faith as Standing Firm and Understanding

- Biblical foundation: "If you do not believe, then you do not abide" (Isaiah 7:9)
    - Hebrew root 'mn encompasses truth, firmness, loyalty, trust
    - Faith as taking one's stand on the ground of God's word
- Greek translation: "If you do not believe, then you do not understand"
    - Preserves essential meaning despite different imagery
    - Standing and understanding are interconnected
- Faith operates on different plane from "making" and "makability"
    - Entrusts to what has not been made by oneself
    - Cannot be demonstrated through practical knowledge
- Two basic forms of human attitude to reality:
    - Both legitimate and necessary
    - Neither can be reduced to the other
- Faith addresses basic decisions every human must make
    - Concerns the realm of meaning that bears us up
    - Meaning cannot be manufactured from proven knowledge alone

### 6. the Rationality of Faith

- The relationship between "I believe" and "Amen"
    - Both derive from same Hebrew root meaning firmness, trust
    - Express abandonment to ground that upholds us
- Faith is movement toward the logos, toward truth itself
    - Not blind surrender to irrational
    - The ground of existence must be truth revealing itself
- Practical knowledge vs. belief:
    - Practical knowledge achieves success by limiting itself to function
    - No longer inquires after truth itself, only whether things work
    - Truth of being eludes calculating knowledge
- Christian faith combines thought and love
    - Absolute thought is a kind of love
    - Love is higher than mere thought
    - Truth and love are originally identical

### 7. "I Believe in You"

- Christian faith is fundamentally personal
    - Not "I believe in something" but "I believe in you"
    - Encounter with Jesus reveals meaning of world as person
- Jesus as presence of eternal meaning
    - Not just witness to God but presence of God itself
    - His life is complete devotion to others
    - Meaning of world appears as love that guarantees eternity
- Faith, trust, and love are ultimately one
    - All Christian theses express the fundamental "I believe in you"
    - Discovery of God in Jesus of Nazareth
- Even believers face the question: "Are you really he?"
    - Intellectual honesty and reason's responsibility require this question
    - Love wants to know more in order to love more
    - All reflections subordinate to basic confession of faith in Jesus

### Chapter 2: The Ecclesiastical Form of Faith

### 1. Introductory Remarks on the History and Structure of the Apostles' Creed

- Origin in 2nd-3rd centuries in connection with baptism ceremony
- Based on Matthew 28:19: baptizing "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"
- Original form: three baptismal questions with "Credo" responses
- Development:
    - 2nd-3rd centuries: expansion of middle section (Christ)
    - 4th century: continuous text emerges
    - 5th century: legend of apostolic origin develops
    - Charlemagne secures unified text for his empire
- East never adopted this Roman symbol, had variety of creeds
- Western vs. Eastern emphasis:
    - West: more concerned with salvation history and Christology
    - East: cosmic and metaphysical perspective relating Christology to creation

### 2. Limits and Meaning of the Text

- Text reflects tensions of first millennium church history
    - Common ground but also East-West split
    - Rome's special position and resulting tensions
    - Political utilization of faith for imperial unity
- Shows necessity for faith to break through political manipulation
- Despite checkered history, represents accurate echo of ancient Church's faith
- Must be interpreted in light of New Testament aims

### 3. Creed and Dogma

- Original meaning found in baptismal context
- Faith as conversion: triple renunciation + triple assent
- "I believe" = "I hand myself over to" or "I assent to"
- Not recitation of doctrines but all-encompassing movement of existence
- Interaction of "I" and "We" expresses whole image of man:
    - Highly personal process (individual "I")
    - Made in response to question (dialogue structure)
- Early church struggles were about fundamental questions:
    - What happens when one becomes Christian?
    - What kind of shift in being does this accomplish?

### 4. the Creed as Expression of the Structure of Faith

#### a. Faith and word

- Creed preserves original dialogue: "Do you believe—I do believe"
- "Faith comes from what is heard" (Romans 10:17)
    - Not result of lonely meditation
    - Expression of hearing, receiving, answering
- Faith differs structurally from philosophy:
    - Philosophy: thought precedes word
    - Faith: word takes precedence over thought
    - Word is foreordained, ahead of thinking
- Social character of belief vs. individualistic philosophy:
    - Philosophy is work of solitary individual
    - Faith is call to community through unity of word
    - Primary factor is proclaimed word, not private thought
- Reveals both image of man and image of God:
    - Man deals with God through dealing with fellowmen
    - Relationship to God and fellowship with man intertwined
    - God approaches man only through man

#### b. Belief as "symbol"

- Symbolum = "coming together" or "throwing together"
- Ancient usage: corresponding pieces as tokens of identity
- Symbol points to complementary other half, creates unity
- Creed as symbol:
    - Facilitates common profession and worship
    - Not isolated doctrine but form of worship of God
    - Arrangement of words that unites people in common confession
- Every person holds faith only as symbol (incomplete piece)
    - Must be combined with others for completeness
    - Church herself holds faith only as symbol
    - Points beyond itself to the entirely Other
- Augustine's story of Marius Victorinus:
    - Philosopher thought he possessed Christianity's essentials intellectually
    - Came to understand Church as more than organization of ideas
    - Christianity is way, not system of knowledge
    - Belief embraces profession, word, unity, fellowship

## Part 1: God - "I Believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of Heaven and Earth"

### Prolegomena to the Subject of God

#### 1. THE SCOPE OF THE QUESTION

- Religious experience has two main roots:
    - Experience of one's own existence (God the Son/Savior)
    - Confrontation with world's powers (God the Creator/Father)
- Three basic paths in human history regarding God:
    - Monotheism, polytheism, atheism
    - All three share conviction of unity of absolute
    - Differ in how absolute behaves toward man and how man relates to it
- Marxism attempts to incorporate belief into practical knowledge
    - Promises faciendum (makable) as meaning of life
    - Cannot turn this into demonstrable knowledge
    - Must leave decision to belief

#### 2. THE PROFESSION OF FAITH IN THE ONE GOD

- Christian creed takes up Israel's daily profession: "Hear, O Israel, Yahweh, thy God, is an only God"
- Faith develops through "linked steps" - never complete discontinuity
- Israel's profession as renunciation of surrounding gods:
    - Rejection of worship of bread, love, and power
    - Declaration of war on multiplication of divine
    - Renunciation of "gods of one's own"
    - Assent to one God of heaven as power over all
- Early Christian decision parallels Israel's:
    - Renunciation of emperor cult, pleasure worship, superstition
    - Struggle over shape of public life in ancient world
- Political implications:
    - Breaks totalitarian claims of political power
    - Establishes absolute value of individual through God
    - Relativizes all political communities
    - Provides protection against collective power

### The Biblical Belief in God

#### 1. THE PROBLEM OF THE STORY OF THE BURNING BUSH

- Central text: Exodus 3 (burning bush story)
- God's revelation of name to Moses
- Dialogue between Moses and God about divine name
- God's response: "I am who I am" / "I am has sent me to you"
- Text establishes "Yahweh" as definitive name of God in Israel
- Church Fathers saw confirmation of Greek philosophy's discovery of Being
    - Identified biblical "I AM" with philosophical concept of Being
    - Modern scholarship questions this identification
    - Debate over whether this represents decline into Hellenism

#### 2. THE INTRINSIC ASSUMPTION OF THE BELIEF IN YAHWEH: THE GOD OF [ISRAEL'S] FATHERS

- Yahweh explained as "God of our fathers" (Abraham, Isaac, Jacob)
- El-religion characteristics:
    - Numen personale vs. numen locale (personal vs. local god)
    - Not bound to place but present wherever man is
    - God on plane of I-You relationship, not spatial
    - Reaches into transcendence of unlimited
- Three persistent elements:
    - Personal and person-centered nature
    - Highest power above all else
    - God of Promise (future-oriented, not cyclical)
- Development to "Elohim" (plural form):
    - Expresses God's uniqueness through transcendence of categories
    - Points toward Christian concept of triune God
    - "He is everything divine"

#### 3. YAHWEH, THE "GOD OF OUR FATHERS" AND THE GOD OF JESUS CHRIST

- Name revelation appears as rebuff: "I am who I am"
    - Refusal to be placed on level with other gods
    - Gesture of withdrawal into mystery
    - Maintains infinite distance while enabling encounter
- Positive meaning: God's presence for Israel
    - "I am here for you"
    - Being-for rather than Being in itself
    - Endurance and persistence amid transience
- Yahweh as "he who is" in contrast to all becoming
    - Deutero-Isaiah's development: "I am the first and the last"
    - God's lasting victory over declining powers
- New Testament continuation in John's Gospel:
    - "I am" becomes central formula of Christology
    - Christ as revealer of God's name
    - John 17: Jesus as manifestation of God's name

#### 4. THE IDEA OF THE NAME

- Difference between concept and name:
    - Concept: perceives nature of thing as it is in itself
    - Name: makes thing "nameable" and "invocable"
    - Name establishes relation and social incorporation
- God's naming himself = making himself accessible
    - Not expressing inner nature but enabling invocation
    - Entering into coexistence with humans
- Christ as real, living name of God:
    - Fulfillment of what "name of God" always meant
    - God becomes truly nameable and accessible
    - Name becomes flesh, enters into coexistence with us

#### 5. THE TWO SIDES OF THE BIBLICAL CONCEPT OF GOD

- Personal element: proximity, invocability, self-bestowal
    - God of fathers, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob
    - God with face, personal God
    - Giving of name enables encounter
- Transcendent element: timeless power above space and time
    - "I am" - the enigmatic Being
    - God of heaven standing over all
    - Not national god but God of all peoples
- Biblical paradox: Being as person, person as Being itself
    - Hidden as near, inaccessible as accessible
    - One who exists for all and for whom all exist

### The God of Faith and the God of the Philosophers

#### 1. THE DECISION OF THE EARLY CHURCH IN FAVOR OF PHILOSOPHY

- Early Christianity chose God of philosophers over gods of religions
- Declared traditional religions as deceit and illusion
- Identified with highest being of philosophical thought
- This meant opting for logos against myth
- Definitive demythologization of world and religion
- Parallels between:
    - Greek philosophers' criticism of myths
    - Prophets' criticism of gods in Israel
    - Both movements toward logos
- Christianity succeeded where ancient religion failed:
    - United faith and reason rather than separating them
    - Ancient religion collapsed due to division between faith and philosophy
- Tertullian: "Christ called himself truth, not custom"
    - Rejection of consuetudo Romana
    - Truth over tradition and usefulness
- Ancient world's three theologies: physical, political, mythical
    - Attempt to justify separation of myth and logos
    - Weighed custom and utility against truth
- Two contemporary temptations:
    - Retreat to pure piety/faith without reason
    - "Interpreted Christianity" removing stumbling blocks

#### 2. THE TRANSFORMATION OF THE GOD OF THE PHILOSOPHERS

- Christian faith gave new significance to philosophical God
- Philosophical God: pure thought, self-contained, unchangeable
- Christian transformation revealed God as:
    - Not pure Being but agape (creative love)
    - Active in history and relationship
    - The burning bush experience vs. mathematical God
- Example from Luke 15: parables of lost sheep and coin
    - God as personal, emotional, caring
    - Not unfeeling geometry of universe
    - The "anthropomorphic" God is actually more divine
- Hölderlin's maxim: "Not to be encompassed by the greatest, but to let oneself be encompassed by the smallest—that is divine"
- Two fundamental differences from philosophical God:
    - Not self-centered but defined by relationship
    - Not pure thought but thought that is also love
    - Creative because it is love, love because it is thought
    - Truth and love originally identical

#### 3. THE REFLECTION OF THE QUESTION IN THE TEXT OF THE CREED

- "Father" and "Almighty" (pantokrator) express the paradox
- Pantokrator: originally cosmic significance, later political
- Family concept + cosmic power = Christian image of God
- Tension between absolute power and absolute love
- True meaning of almightiness revealed in Cross:
    - Highest power as complete renunciation of power
    - Powerful through freedom of love, not force
    - Revaluation of power and lordship concepts

### Faith in God Today

#### 1. THE PRIMACY OF THE LOGOS

- "I believe that God exists" = opting for primacy of logos over matter
    - Logos (idea, freedom, love) at beginning and end
    - All being is product of thought, not accident
    - Being itself is thought, comprehensibility, meaning
- Belief in creation as conviction of intellectual structure
    - Objective mind in things expresses subjective mind
    - Einstein: laws reveal "superior intelligence"
    - Mathematical structure points to thinking behind it
- Problem: how to relate cosmic mathematics to personal God
    - Mathematics discovers only mathematical side
    - But world also displays beauty and wonder
    - Creative imagination beyond pure mathematics
- Three basic philosophical positions:
    - Materialistic: matter as ultimate reality
    - Idealistic: all being ultimately mind/thought
    - Christian: being as being-thought by creative consciousness
- Christian view transcends both materialism and idealism:
    - Things are being-thought but given independent existence
    - Creative freedom sets thought free into actual being
    - Freedom as structural form of all being

#### 2. THE PERSONAL GOD

- Primacy of logos necessarily implies personal nature
    - Original creative thought must be conscious thinking
    - Thinks, knows, loves, and gives freedom to its thought
- Mind exists specifically as individuality, as person
    - "Objective mind" always derived from subjective mind
    - Cosmos moving toward unification in the personal
- Precedence of individual over universal
    - Person more than individual reproduction of idea
    - Minimum becomes maximum in personal reality
    - Plurality has definitive right alongside unity
- Leads to transcending mere monotheism toward Trinity
- Freedom as necessary structure of world
    - Final stage not cosmic necessity but freedom
    - Incalculability essential part of world structure
    - Risk of evil for sake of greater light of freedom and love

### Belief in the Triune God

#### 1. A START AT UNDERSTANDING

#### a. The point of departure of the belief in the triune God

- Doctrine developed from historical experiences, not speculation
- Three encounters with God:
    - Father: Creator and Lord (Old Testament)
    - Son: Jesus who calls God "Father" yet is God-with-us
    - Spirit: God's presence within us
- Paradox of Jesus:
    - Speaks to God as "You" (must be someone other than Father)
    - Is mediation of God to us (must be God himself)
    - Both "man himself" and "God himself" with equal reality
- Question: Does triplicity tell us only about human relationship to God, or about God himself?
- Faith's answer: "God is as he shows himself"
    - God doesn't show himself in way he is not
    - What we encounter in Christ is God's own reality

#### b. The guiding motives

Three basic attitudes shaped development:
1. Faith in immediate proximity to God
    - Jesus as fellow man who is truly God
    - Only if really human can he mediate
    - Only if really God does mediation reach goal
2. Unyielding monotheistic loyalty
    - "There is only one God"
    - Cannot erect region of false gods through mediator
3. Taking salvation history seriously
    - When God appears as Son saying "You" to Father, it's not play
    - Expression of reality, not divine show
    - "Roles" (personae) transformed into real Persons

#### c. The hopelessness of the solutions

Two inadequate approaches:
1. Subordinationism: Christ not God but being close to God
    - Removes difficulty but cuts man off from God himself
    - God becomes constitutional monarch
2. Monarchianism: Trinity as masks of God
    - Tells us about ourselves but nothing about God
    - Leads to man circling around himself
    - In Hegel/Marx becomes historicization of God
    - Political theology with dangerous implications

#### d. The doctrine of the Trinity as negative theology

- Church doctrine justified primarily negatively
- Demonstration of hopelessness of all other approaches
- Essentially negative—frontier notice pointing to incomprehensible
- All main concepts were once condemned
    - Accepted only as inadequate stammering
    - Usable only through negation and indirectness
- "Baffled theology" acknowledging mystery
- Heresies as ciphers for abiding truths
    - Valid only when held with other statements
    - Complementarity principle from physics applicable

#### 2. POSITIVE SIGNIFICANCE

#### Thesis No. 1: Unity and plurality

- Challenges Greek view that only unity is divine
- God as simultaneously monas and trias
- Plurality also primordial, grounded in God himself
- Not disintegration but creative fullness
- True unity is unity of love, not indivisible atom
- Final elimination of dualistic explanations

#### Thesis No. 2: The concept of person

- Person necessarily includes relatedness
- No such thing as person in categorical singular
- Greek prosopon = "look toward" (includes relatedness)
- Latin persona = "sounding through" (communication)
- God as person necessarily implies Trinity
- Explodes naïve anthropomorphic concept of person

#### Thesis No. 3: Absolute and relative

#### a. Dogma as speech form
- Formula "one Being—three Persons" largely matter of words
- Ensures both oneness and threeness are expressed
- Not the only possible formulation
- Language of theology purely tentative

#### b. The concept of person
- "Son of God" vs. "the Son" - different origins and meanings
- Biblical evidence reveals dialogue within God
- Category of relatio gains new significance
- Relation as primordial form of being alongside substance
- Augustine: "He is not called Father with reference to himself but only in relation to the Son"
- Person as pure relation, nothing else
- Revolution in view of world: end of sole dominion of substance

#### c. The connection back to biblical thought and Christian existence
- John's Gospel: "The Son can do nothing of his own accord"
- Son as pure relatedness to Father
- Complete openness = complete unity with Father
- Extension to Christians: "Apart from me you can do nothing"
- Christian existence as being "from" and "toward"
- Unity through complete openness in love
- Three concepts expressing same truth: Son, Mission, Word (logos)
- Augustine: "What is so much yours as yourself, and what is so little yours as yourself?"

## Part 2: Jesus Christ

### "I Believe in Jesus Christ, His Only Son, Our Lord"

#### A. THE PROBLEM OF FAITH IN JESUS TODAY

- Central difficulty: profession that one historical individual is decisive center of all history
- Alliance of logos and sarx (meaning and single historical figure)
- Stumbling block compounded by historical-critical method
- Like physics, limits itself to "phenomenal" or outer surface
- Historical science both reveals and conceals history
- Full truth of history eludes documentary verification

#### B. JESUS THE CHRIST: THE BASIC FORM OF THE CHRISTOLOGICAL PROFESSION OF FAITH

#### 1. THE DILEMMA OF MODERN THEOLOGY: JESUS OR CHRIST?

- Modern theology torn between Jesus and Christ
- Three inadequate approaches:
    - Establishing Christology on historical plane
    - Reducing to demonstrable facts
    - Escaping history as irrelevant
- Zigzag movement: Jesus vs. Christ
    - Harnack: "Not the Son but only the Father belongs in the Gospel"
    - Back to Jesus, away from divisive dogma
    - Bultmann: Historical Jesus unknowable, only kerygma matters
    - "Death of God" theology: Jesus as symbol after God's absence
- Jesus and Christ cannot be separated
    - Jesus only subsists as Christ
    - Christ only subsists as Jesus
    - One bound to be pushed from one to other

#### 2. THE CREED'S IMAGE OF CHRIST

- "Christ Jesus" - title become name
- Like "Kaiser Wilhelm" - function merged with identity
- Fusion expresses heart of Christian understanding
- With Jesus, office and person cannot be distinguished
    - Person IS the office
    - No private "I" separate from work
    - "I" is the work, work is the "I"
- Karl Barth: "Jesus is absolutely the bearer of an office"
    - No neutral humanity apart from messianic office
    - His being as man IS his work
- "Functional Christology" where function is all being
- Person of Jesus IS his teaching and deeds

#### 3. THE POINT OF DEPARTURE OF FAITH IN JESUS: THE CROSS

- Birthplace of Christian faith is the Cross
- Jesus didn't proclaim himself directly as Christ
- Pilate's execution notice became profession of faith
- Crucifixion as coronation, kingship as surrender
- Faith recognized identity of person, word, and work through Cross
- John's Gospel: same concentration on "I" in Jesus' words
- Cross as process of opening drawing all to unity

#### 4. JESUS THE CHRIST

- Recognition of Christ in Jesus means abandoning exclusiveness of faith
- Hyphen between Jesus-Christ = hyphen between love and faith
- Jesus' "I" is Being completely from "Thou" of Father for "You" of men
- Identity of logos (truth) and love
- Matthew 25: professing faith in Christ = discovering Christ in needy
- Faith that is not love is not truly Christian faith

#### C. JESUS CHRIST—TRUE GOD AND TRUE MAN

#### 1. THE FORMULATION OF THE QUESTION

- Christian faith affirms Jesus as Christ (person and work identical)
- Total openness as "pure Word," "pure Son"
- Two-sided consequence:
    - Must he not be identical with God who is love?
    - Is he not the most human of men, fulfillment of humanity?
- Classical christological dogma: triple Yes
    - Radical Christship presupposes Sonship
    - Sonship includes Godship
    - Radicality of service makes him most human
- Coincidence of theology and anthropology

#### 2. A MODERN STOCK IDEA OF THE "HISTORICAL JESUS"

- Common reconstruction today:
    - Prophetic teacher in eschatological atmosphere
    - Preached proximity of Kingdom of God
    - Condemned to death as failure
    - Resurrection belief arose mysteriously
    - Hope projected back onto historical Jesus
    - Hellenistic transformation into "divine man" (θειος ἀνήρ)
- This theory historically absurd
    - "Divine man" concept not found in New Testament
    - No connection between "divine man" and "Son of God"
    - Two completely different patterns of thought

#### 3. THE CLAIM OF CHRISTOLOGICAL DOGMA

#### a. The question of the "divine man"
- Concept θειος ἀνήρ occurs nowhere in New Testament
- "Divine man" never described as "Son of God" in antiquity
- Two concepts historically unconnected
- Even concept of "divine man" barely attested pre-Christian

#### b. Biblical terminology and its relation to dogma

**i. "Son of God"**
- Stems from Old Testament "king" theology
- Psalm 2:7 as classical example
- Demythologized oriental coronation ritual
- King becomes son through election, not begetting
- Royal theology becomes theology of hope
- Applied to Jesus in Resurrection context
- Cross paradoxically fulfills royal promises
- Son and servant concepts intertwine
- Philippians 2: self-emptying leads to universal lordship

**ii. "The Son"**  
- Distinct from "Son of God"
- Comes from Jesus' prayers, especially "Abba"
- Aramaic "Abba" = intimate address to God (like "Papa")
- Shocked contemporaries by its familiarity
- Jesus' self-description as Son implies total relativity
- John develops this into full Christology
- Son as pure being "from" and "for"
- Total unity through complete relatedness

#### D. THE DIFFERENT PATHS TAKEN BY CHRISTOLOGY

#### 1. THEOLOGY OF THE INCARNATION AND THEOLOGY OF THE CROSS

- Two divergent approaches in Church history:
    - Incarnation theology: focuses on God becoming man
    - Cross theology: focuses on event of Cross and Resurrection
- Incarnation theology tends toward static optimism
- Cross theology tends toward dynamic interpretation
- Both needed as correctives to each other
- Properly understood, they pass over into each other
- Being of Christ is actualitas, stepping beyond oneself
- Doing reaches into depths of being and coincides with it

#### 2. CHRISTOLOGY AND THE DOCTRINE OF REDEMPTION

- Historical separation of Christology (Jesus' being) and soteriology (redemption)
- Led to both problems becoming incomprehensible
- Anselm's satisfaction theory:
    - Man's sin infinitely offends God
    - Infinite reparation required but impossible for finite man
    - God-man provides necessary infinite expiation
- Theory has merits but creates problems:
    - Makes God appear sinister
    - Reduces redemption to legal mechanism
    - Different when person and work are unified

#### 3. CHRIST, "THE LAST MAN"

- Jesus as exemplary man (Paul's "last Adam")
- Oversteps bounds of humanity by being truly human
- Man comes to himself by moving beyond himself
- Full "hominization" presupposes God becoming man
- Jesus as goal of humanity's development
- Teilhard de Chardin's cosmic perspective:
    - Evolution toward increasing complexity
    - Christ as leap to new stage beyond biological
    - Drawing scattered humanity into unity
    - Socialization without loss of individuality
- John 12:32: "When I am lifted up I will draw all to myself"
- Future of man lies in "being for"
- Cross as opening up new humanity

#### EXCURSUS: CHRISTIAN STRUCTURES

#### I. The individual and the whole

- Christianity not based on atomized individual
- Man exists only in relationship to whole
- "Corporality" principle: we depend on each other
- Möhler: "Man cannot come to himself through himself"
- No such thing as mere individual "Cogito ergo sum"
- Christianity exists because of collective human entanglements
- Church as transformation of collective powers
- Individual important precisely because of relation to whole
- One is Christian to serve the whole, not for private salvation

#### 2. The principle of "for"

- Basic law of Christian existence expressed by preposition "for"
- Eucharist as existence "for the many"
- Cross as complete self-opening
- Christian prayer combines worship and brotherliness
- Election as embarking on common task
- "Way of the cross" as departure from self-enclosure
- John 12:24: grain of wheat must die to bear fruit
- Universal law: world lives on sacrifice

#### 3. The law of disguise

- God appears as opposite of expected
- Luther: God hidden "sub contrario"
- God's double mode of appearing:
    - Cosmic power (greatness of creation)
    - Sign of the lowly (Earth—Israel—Nazareth—Cross—Church)
- Series shows God's progressive concealment and revelation
- What is cosmically small represents true divine sign
- "For" is the really divine thing

#### 4. The law of excess or superfluity

- Tension between grace and ethos in New Testament
- Matthew 5:20: righteousness must "exceed" that of Pharisees
- Human righteousness always inadequate
- "Excess" as fundamental Christian principle
- Connected to miracle of loaves and marriage at Cana
- God's trademark: excess, not calculation
- Salvation history as "breathtaking outpouring" of God
- Excess as necessity for beings who need meaning beyond survival

#### 5. Finality and hope

- Christ as accomplished salvation and inaugurated future
- Revelation concluded in Christ (not because God stops speaking)
- God has uttered himself completely in Word
- Goal attained is open space, not rigid boundary
- Christian rejection of "third Kingdom" theories
- Faith makes definitive claim through finality
- Applies to dogma, marriage, personal commitments
- Finality enables progress rather than hindering it

#### 6. The primacy of acceptance and Christian positivity

- Cross reveals primacy of acceptance over action
- Contrast with Marxist principle of class struggle
- Man comes to himself through being loved
- Love can only be received as gift
- Cannot be "made" without others
- Louis Evely: Adam's false idea of God as autonomous
- God revealed as dependence, obedience, fellowship
- Relativizes works but liberates human activity
- Primacy of acceptance makes possible Christian positivity
- Necessity of "contingent" historical revelation

#### 7. Summary: The "spirit of Christianity"

- Six principles coalesce into principle of love
- Love includes faith (need to receive, inadequacy of achievement)
- Love includes hope (looks beyond moment to whole)
- Paul's trilogy: "faith, hope, love abide, these three; but the greatest of these is love"

### The Development of Faith in Christ in the Christological Articles of the Creed

#### 1. "CONCEIVED BY THE POWER OF THE HOLY SPIRIT AND BORN OF THE VIRGIN MARY"

- Origin of Jesus shrouded in mystery
- John 7:27: "We know where this man comes from" vs. true origin from Father
- Infancy narratives confirm mystery rather than eliminate it
- Luke's account in Old Testament language
- Mary as new Israel, true "daughter of Zion"
- Luke 1:35: new creation, not biological generation
- Spirit as power of creation (Genesis 1:2)
- Mary as new temple where God's presence descends
- Virgin birth often seen as mythological
- But immediate antecedents are Old Testament, not pagan mythology
- Key differences from pagan stories:
    - God not as fertilizing power
    - Conception as new creation, not begetting
    - Divine Sonship not based on Virgin Birth
- Divine Sonship is ontological, not biological fact
- Would not be affected if Jesus had human father
- "Physical" Divine Sonship means belonging by being to Trinity
- Luke 1:35: "Son of God" refers to election theology, not Divine Sonship
- Virgin Birth as theology of grace
- Series of miraculous births in salvation history
- Elizabeth continues pattern with John the Baptist
- Meaning: salvation comes from God's power alone
- Not human achievement but pure gift
- Isaiah 54:1: children of barren one more than married
- Jesus as new beginning from spirit of God

#### 2. "SUFFERED UNDER PONTIUS PILATE, WAS CRUCIFIED, DIED, AND WAS BURIED"

#### a. Righteousness and grace

- Cross often misunderstood as divine legal mechanism
- Anselm's satisfaction theory widely influential but coarsened
- Makes God appear to demand human sacrifice
- Biblical view completely different
- Cross as expression of radical love, not legal transaction
- Revolution compared to other religions
- Usually man goes to God with compensatory gift
- Christianity: God comes to man to give
- 2 Corinthians 5:19: "God was in Christ reconciling world to himself"
- Movement from above to below, not human expiation to God
- Christian worship as Eucharistia (thanksgiving)
- Worship by letting God act on us, not giving to God

#### b. The Cross as worship and sacrifice

- New Testament uses Old Testament cult theology
- Hebrews: Jesus' death as cosmic day of reconciliation
- All animal sacrifices bound to remain useless
- God seeks not bulls and goats but human Yes
- Psalm 50: God owns everything already
- Only love's free Yes cannot be replaced
- Christ as true priest who gives himself
- Death as cosmic liturgy, not limited temple ritual
- Blood not material gift but expression of total surrender
- Complete love extending "to the end" (John 13:1)

#### c. The nature of Christian worship

- Not surrender of things or destruction
- Consists in absoluteness of love
- New form of representation: Christ stands for us
- We accept gift of Christ's love and are united in it
- Addresses questions about worship:
    1. Cannot resolve Christianity into horizontal brotherly love alone
    2. Pain is secondary element resulting from love's character
    - Primary element is love itself
    - Pain results from love's stretching across infinite distance
    - Daniélou: Christ stretched from Trinity to human misery
    - Executioners not the priests; Jesus the priest who unites extremes

#### 3. "DESCENDED INTO HELL"

- Often dismissed as mythological
- Actually describes modern experience of God's absence
- "God is dead and we have killed him" (Nietzsche)
- Holy Saturday as day of God's silence
- Two biblical scenes illustrate:
    - Elijah mocking priests of Baal (1 Kings 18:27)
    - Emmaus disciples' hope dies but Christ present unknown
- God's silence part of Christian revelation
- Not only speech but silence reveals God
- Only through experiencing silence may we hear speech
- Jesus' death cry: "My God, why have you forsaken me?"
- Käsemann: prayer from hell, standard raised in God's absence
- "Hell" originally sheol = state after death
- Real question: what is death?
- Death as radical loneliness, complete abandonment
- Examples of loneliness producing fear:
    - Child walking alone in dark
    - Keeping watch with corpse
- Fear overcome by presence of loving person
- Hell = loneliness no "You" can penetrate
- Death and hell ultimately identical in Old Testament
- Christ entered gate of final loneliness
- Hell overcome because love dwells in death
- Gates of sheol opened
- Matthew 27:52: tombs opened at Jesus' death

#### 4. "ROSE AGAIN [FROM THE DEAD]"

- Expression of certainty that "love is strong as death"
- Love demands infinity but cannot grant it itself
- Man cannot exist alone, needs continuance in another
- Two inadequate human attempts at immortality:
    - Living on in children
    - Living on in fame/memory
- Both fail because the other also perishes
- Only God who "is" can give lasting stability
- Love must be more than life to be stronger than death
- Teilhard: decisive "complexification" when love encompasses bios
- Resurrection formulas: "Jesus has risen" / "God awakened Jesus"
- Love establishes immortality; immortality proceeds from love
- Applies even to God as Trinity (absolute relatedness)
- His resurrection is our life (1 Corinthians 15)
- Either love stronger than death or not
- Our immortality's mode depends on our mode of loving
- Risen life is zoe, not bios
- Beyond mortal realm of biological life
- Resurrection narratives show dialectical character:
    - Touching/not touching, recognizing/not recognizing
    - Complete identity yet complete transformation
- Emmaus story combines earthly elements with mystery
    - Unrecognizable to accustomed eye
    - Discovered in liturgy: word and sacrament
    - But also real event, not just interpretation

#### 5. "HE ASCENDED INTO HEAVEN AND IS SEATED AT THE RIGHT HAND OF THE FATHER"

- Seems to presuppose three-story mythical world
- But expresses total range of human existence's three dimensions
- Hell, history, heaven as dimensions of human nature
- Hell as depths of human existence (untouchable loneliness)
- Heaven as opposite possibility (contact with divine love)
- Hell: what man gives to himself through self-enclosure
- Heaven: can only be received, never self-made
- Heaven comes into existence through confluence of God and man
- Accomplished in Christ through death to new life
- Heaven as future opened in "definitive man"
- Explains New Testament "eschatology of imminence"
- Christ's Resurrection begins "eschatology"
- With crossing of death, future dimension opened
- Christ as center and "end" of history
- Prayer's meaning clarified:
    - God can hear (established earlier)
    - Can God answer? Problem of eternity and time
    - Eternity not imprisoned in unchangeableness
    - Eternity as power of present in all time
    - Incarnation as manifestation of God's dominion over time
    - Christ as "door" between God and man
    - Hans Urs von Balthasar: In Jesus, God has time for world

#### 6. "HE WILL COME AGAIN TO JUDGE THE LIVING AND THE DEAD"

- Bultmann considers this "disposed of" for modern man
- "World" means primarily human world, not physical cosmos
- Bible presents anthropological event in cosmological imagery
- Cosmos and man belong necessarily together
- Biblical view: only one world history embracing all
- Matter forms prehistory of spirit
- World advancing toward "omega point"
- Final "complexification" of material being through spirit
- Not victory of technology but unification by creative freedom
- World moving toward unification in person
- Justifies Christianity's apparent "positivism"
- Omega is a "you," a person, an individual
- Precedence of particular over universal
- Supreme factor not cosmic necessity but freedom
- Freedom includes responsibility, hence judgment
- Final stage based on decisions, not natural drift
- Judgment "according to works" despite message of grace
- Leo Baeck on Judaism's universalism vs. Christian exclusivism
- Christian must face paradox of grace and responsibility
- Jesus as judge brings hope rather than terror
- "Fear not, it is I" - the judge is the companion of faith

## Part 3: The Spirit and the Church

### The Intrinsic Unity of the Last Statements in the Creed

- Original Greek: "I believe in Holy Spirit" (no definite article)
- Originally salvation-historical, not trinitarian reference
- God's gift to history in believing community
- Trinitarian and salvation-historical viewpoints interact
- Later division between theological metaphysics and salvation history
- Text speaks of "God facing outward"
- Holy Spirit as power keeping risen Lord present in history
- Faith in Spirit and faith in Church interfere with each other
- Later separation harmed both ecclesiology and pneumatology
- Church teaching must start from Holy Spirit and his gifts

**Communion of saints:**
- Originally referred to eucharistic community
- Sanctorum = holy gifts, not persons
- Church defined from worship, not organization
- Unity around risen Christ's table
- Later included persons united by holy gifts
- Cosmic breadth: extends beyond death

**Forgiveness of sins:**
- Refers to baptism and penance
- Baptism as great sacrament of forgiveness
- Christianity requires conversion, not just birth
- Baptism as start of lifelong conversion
- Goes beyond baptized to all humanity
- Man must oppose natural inclination to become truly human

**Final words (resurrection and eternal life):**
- Unfolding of faith in Holy Spirit's transforming power
- Follow from Jesus' Resurrection crossing frontier of death
- Biological overtaken by spirit and love
- Barrier of death broken in principle
- Definitive future opened for man and world
- Cross as salvation of world through God becoming last letter

### Two Major Questions Posed by the Articles on the Spirit and the Church

#### 1. "THE HOLY, CATHOLIC CHURCH"

**Problem of calling Church "holy":**
- Church appears neither holy nor catholic
- Filled with human failure throughout history
- Vatican II spoke of "sinful Church"
- William of Auvergne: Church as "monster of terrible deformity"

**Understanding Church's holiness:**
- Holiness refers primarily to divine gift, not human persons
- Church holy because God exerts sanctifying power in her
- New Covenant: God bound himself to men despite their faithlessness
- Grace that abides despite human failure
- Paradoxical combination of holiness and unholiness
- "Nevertheless" of God's greater love
- True holiness as mingling with sinners (Christ's example)
- Not separation but union, not judgment but redeeming love
- Unholy holiness reveals God's true holiness as love
- Holiness begins with forbearance and leads to bearing up

**Catholicity of the Church:**
- Refers to unity in double way:
    - Local unity (community with bishop)
    - Unity of local churches with each other
- Episcopal structure serves this unity
- Organization understood from Church, not vice versa
- Unity of Word and sacrament primary
- Visible unity necessary as sign in torn world
- Challenge to make catholicity reality

#### 2. "THE RESURRECTION OF THE BODY"

#### a. The content of the New Testament hope of resurrection

**Biblical vs. Greek conceptions:**
- Greek: body-soul dualism, soul naturally immortal
- Biblical: undivided unity of man
- Greek seeks escape from body; biblical saves whole person
- Two different total answers, not complementary halves
- Biblical hope originally comprehensive, not supplement to soul's immortality

**Three characteristics of biblical immortality:**
1. **Personal immortality:** whole person continues, not just soul
2. **Dialogical immortality:** results from being known and loved by God, not intrinsic indestructibility
3. **Communal character:** related to whole of mankind, expected at "Last Day"

**New Testament development:**
- Christ as "second Adam" opens human future
- Being completely "from" God and "for" others
- Faith as entry into dialogue with God that is life
- "Intermediate state": life with Christ begins now, outlasts death
- Sleep-of-death theories untenable

#### b. The essential immortality of man

**Immortality as grace vs. nature:**
- Man's distinguishing mark: being addressed by God
- Capable of thinking God, opened to transcendence
- "Having spiritual soul" = being God's dialogue partner
- Substantialist and historical languages express same truth
- Man's immortality based on dialogical relationship with God
- Not special destiny for pious but essential immortality of man as man
- No neat distinction between "natural" and "supernatural"
- Christ as fulfillment of basic human longing

#### c. The question of the resurrected body

**Paul's answer in 1 Corinthians 15:**
- Key verse 50: "Flesh and blood cannot inherit kingdom of God"
- Not resurrection of physical bodies
- Soma (body/self) can be sarx (flesh) or pneuma (spirit)
- Resurrection of persons in different form of life
- "Physical body" vs. "spiritual body"
- Paul more radical than later theological speculation

**Relation to matter and Last Day:**
- If cosmos is history and matter is moment in history of spirit
- Final "complexity" where world finds omega and unity
- Connection between matter and spirit in final consummation
- "Last Day" when individual and mankind's destiny fulfilled
- Christian believes in future of whole world, not just private bliss
- Meaning cannot be destroyed, provides hope for historical work
- New world as certainty, not utopia: "Be of good cheer, I have overcome the world"
