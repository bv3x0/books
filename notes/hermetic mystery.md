# Hermetic Philosophy and Alchemy: A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery

## Part I: An Exoteric View of the Progress and Theory of Alchemy

### A Preliminary Account of the Hermetic Philosophy, with the More Salient Points of Its Public History

Atwood surveys the historical lineage of Hermetic philosophy from Hermes Trismegistus through the medieval and early modern adepts, arguing that the unanimous testimony of pious, learned, and morally serious practitioners across cultures and centuries constitutes a compelling prima facie case that Alchemy describes a real art, even if its true nature has been systematically concealed from the profane.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-001"></span>**The Hermetic tradition, traced to Hermes Trismegistus some 1900 years before Christ, is attested by the Smaragdine Table and the Golden Treatise as a coherent system whose compressed axiom—'that which is above is as that which is below'—encapsulates its entire operative doctrine.**
    - The Smaragdine Table survived as the sole Egyptian relic of the Sacred Art after Diocletian's edict of 284 CE ordered all chemical books burned, destroying the bulk of Egypt's alchemical record.
    - The Divine Poimander and Asclepian Dialogues, preserved in Latin by Ficinus and translated by Dr Everard, represent the theosophic complement to the practical Golden Treatise, together constituting the extant Hermetic canon.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-002"></span>**Greek philosophers including Pythagoras, Democritus, and Plato traveled to Egyptian temples specifically for initiation into the Hermetic art, demonstrating that the most celebrated schools of ancient theosophy were grounded in the same experimental science.**
    - Democritus of Abdera, called the father of experimental philosophy, treated the Hermetic art in his Book of Sacred Physics and is confirmed by Pliny as practicing occult sciences alongside Hippocrates' contemporary medicine at Athens.
    - Norton's Ordinal praises Anaxagoras as the plainest of all writers on the science, noting that Aristotle envied and rebuked him for disclosing too much of the natural ground.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-003"></span>**The medieval flowering of Alchemy—from Geber and Morienus through Albertus Magnus, Aquinas, Arnold di Villa Nova, Raymond Lully, and Nicholas Flamel—produced a body of testimony from pious, credentialed, and disinterested scholars that cannot be dismissed as mere fraud or self-delusion without impugning the moral character of the entire medieval intellectual tradition.**
    - "Flamel and his wife Perenelle, beginning from a mysterious gilded book he purchased for two florins, devoted their alchemical gains to founding fourteen hospitals, three chapels, and seven churches in Paris and Boulogne, declaring the Stone 'takes away from him the root of all sin and evil, changing his evil into good, and making him liberal, courteous, religious, fearing God.'" —Nicholas Flamel
    - Raymond Lully, brought to England by Abbot Cremer of Westminster, produced 50,000 pounds weight of gold in the Tower of London, which was coined into Nobles of the Rose described in Camden's Antiquities as being of the finest metal.
    - "Aquinas explicitly wrote 'Metalla transmutari possunt unum in aliud, cum naturalia sint et ipsorum materia eadem'—metals can be transmuted one into another since they are of one and the same matter." —Thomas Aquinas
* <span class="pull-quote">To declare a man an Alchemist in the present day would be to brand him as insane, and the Hermetic ground is as far out of the road of common thought as if it were tabooed.</span>
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-004"></span>**The explosion of fraudulent alchemical practice in the 14th and 15th centuries, and the resulting Parliamentary prohibitions and papal bulls against transmutation, produced a cultural confusion between genuine adepts and imposters that has permanently distorted the public reception of Alchemy—the world condemning an art it never rightly understood.**
    - Pope John XXII, who issued a bull forbidding transmutation on pain of death, is himself reported to have practiced the art extensively and enriched the public treasury by its means.
    - Ripley's 'Erroneous Experiments' verses catalog the wasted years, ruined clothes, unhealthy furnaces, and false tinctures produced by literal reading of alchemical receipts—sulfurs, vitriol, arsenick, egg-shells—proving that the failure belonged to the readers' misunderstanding, not to the genuine doctrine.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-005"></span>**First-person testimonies from Van Helmont, Paracelsus, Geber, Roger Bacon, and Pico di Mirandola, each affirming direct sensory experience of projection and transmutation, establish that if deception occurred it must have been willful and sustained across centuries by figures with no discernible motive for fraud.**
    - "'I have seen the Stone and handled it,' says Van Helmont, 'and projected the fourth part of one grain, wrapped in paper, upon eight ounces of quicksilver boiling in a crucible, and the quicksilver presently stood in its flux like yellow wax; after a flux by blast we found eight ounces all but eleven grains of the purest gold.'" —Jan Baptist van Helmont
    - "'I come now to relate what my eyes have seen plainly without veil or obscurity,' says Pico di Mirandola; 'one of my friends, who is now living, has made gold and silver several times in my presence, and I have seen it and done it myself.'" —Pico di Mirandola
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-006"></span>**The deliberate obscurity of alchemical literature—its cabalism, inversions, repetitions, pseudonyms, and contradictions—was a principled prophylactic against misuse, not a sign of fraud, because the Art carries real operative power that in vicious hands produces moral catastrophe rather than spiritual benefit.**
    - "'Is not our art cabalistic and full of mysteries?' asks Artephius; 'he that takes the philosophers' saying according to the outward sense has already lost the clue to Ariadne, and wanders in the labyrinth; it would be of the same benefit to him as if he had thrown his money into the sea.'" —Artephius
    - "Sendivogius warns in his Preface that 'this Art is for the wise, not for the ignorant; and that the sense of the philosophers is of another nature than to be understood by vaporizing Thrasoes, or the letter-learned scoffers, or vicious against their own consciences.'" —Sendivogius

### Of the Theory of Transmutation in General, and of the First Matter

Atwood argues that the Hermetic theory of transmutation rests not on the literal conversion of one metal-species into another, but on the reduction of all natural bodies to their homogeneous Universal Subject—a pure, incombustible Ethereal Spirit that the adepts call Mercury—which, once freed and fermented by its own proper Light, can re-inform any purified substrate with a superior Form.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-007"></span>**The philosophical foundation of metallic transmutation is not species-conversion but subject-reduction: alchemists maintain that species are indestructible, but their underlying 'radical moisture' or mercurial first matter can be liberated by art and drawn from inferior forms into a superior ferment, precisely as glass is made from flint by expelling one form and introducing another.**
    - "Roger Bacon states the principle explicitly: 'Species non transmutantur, sed subjecta specierum'—species are not transmuted, but the subjects of species; therefore the first work is to reduce the body into water, that is, into mercury, which is called Solution, the foundation of the whole art." —Roger Bacon
    - Arnold di Villa Nova advises that the subject be freed by an artificial reduction, since it is only when bodies are reduced to their first matter that new forms may be introduced, just as rustic operations make glass from stones and ashes.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-008"></span>**The Universal Subject or First Matter of the adepts is an occult Ethereal Spirit—described variously as a celestial water that wets not the hands, an unctuous vapor, a vegetable life, and the binding principle of all elements—which is everywhere immanent but nowhere separable without the specific Hermetic art.**
    - Albertus Magnus defines it as 'a watery element, cold and moist, a permanent water, an unctuous vapor, and the spirit of body'; Artephius calls it 'a white fume in substance like pure silver, resolving bodies into their original whiteness and making all things grow and multiply'; Lully calls it 'a clear compounded water most like argent vive, generated in every compound out of the substance of the air.'
    - "Arnold states: 'if thou say it is water, thou dost say truth; and if thou sayest it is not water, thou dost lie. Be not therefore deceived with manifold descriptions, for it is One Thing to which nothing extraneous is added.'" —Arnold di Villa Nova
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-009"></span>**The three principles—Salt, Sulphur, and Mercury—are not separate substances but three modal relations of the same Universal Spirit: agent, patient, and their offspring, paralleling the Platonic threefold causation and the Kabbalistic emanative Trinity, all of which the adepts claim to have verified in experiential practice.**
    - "Aquinas writes: 'It is Mercury alone which perfects in our work; the White and Red proceed from one root, for it dissolves and coagulates itself, whitens, rubifies, and makes itself to be both yellow and black; it unites with itself, conceives itself, and brings itself forth to the full perfecting of its intention.'" —Thomas Aquinas
    - "Sendivogius: 'thou hast in these three principles a body, a spirit, and an occult soul; which three, being of one only substance in a triple relationship, if thou shalt join them together, having been previously separated and well purified, will without fail, by imitating nature, yield most pure fruits.'" —Sendivogius
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-010"></span>**The phenomena of light, electricity, and magnetism studied by contemporary science are remote effects of the same Causal Ethereal force the alchemists claimed to have concentrated and proved—meaning Alchemy and modern physics address the same reality from opposite methodological poles, one beginning from effects, the other from causes.**
    - Bishop Berkeley in his Siris contends that ether—a pure invisible fire most subtle and elastic of all bodies—is the true source of the air's power of sustenance, in full accord with the alchemical tradition; Homberg and Boerhaave similarly identify a distinct substance universally diffused, the alone pure and active source of all things.
    - Modern phenomena of Mesmerism have helped to force again on observing minds the supposition of a New Imponderable or Odic Force; yet few believe, and the alchemists propose this not as a being of speculation merely but of experimental science—compact and tangible in its pure estate.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-011"></span>**The Hermetic process operates by an 'introversion of the generated life'—dissolving the fixed into volatile and the volatile into fixed—so that the Light occultated by natural generation is freed, and the foreign forms imposed through birth may be expelled, allowing the Universal Spirit to reform itself about a higher law of manifestation.**
    - The adept's maxim 'Si fixum solvas faciasque volare solutum, et volucrem figas, facient te vivere tutum: Solve, Coagula, Fige' encapsulates the entire operative doctrine of reciprocal dissolution and coagulation.
    - "Arnold: 'Convert the elements and you will find what you seek; for our operation is nothing else than a mutation of natures, and the method of conversion in our Argent vive is the reduction of natures to their first root.'" —Arnold di Villa Nova

### The Golden Treatise of Hermes Trismegistus Concerning the Physical Secret of the Philosophers' Stone, in Seven Sections

Atwood presents the full text of the Golden Treatise attributed to Hermes Trismegistus with extensive scholiastic commentary, arguing that this ancient document encodes in compressed, enigmatic form the complete sequence of the Hermetic operation—from the initial dissolution of the impure spirit through the stages of putrefaction, purification, and fermentation to the final manifestation of the Philosopher's Stone as a tincture of universal Light.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-012"></span>**The Seven Sections of the Golden Treatise together constitute a compressed description of the complete Hermetic process: Section One establishes the divine origin of the science and the ethical precondition of its recipients; Sections Two and Three describe the dissolution and purification of the matter; Sections Four and Five treat the conjunction and fermentation; Sections Six and Seven address the Stone's multiplication and projection as a universal ferment.**
    - The Scholiast confirms that the three things necessary for attainment—study, experience, and divine benediction—are mutually dependent: study is required for theory, theory for entering the central experience, and neither avails without God.
    - "Hermes: 'Know that the division made upon the Water by the ancient philosophers separates it into four substances; one to two, and three to one; the one third part of which is colour, that is to say a coagulating moisture; but the two third waters are the Weights of the Wise.'" —Hermes Trismegistus
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-013"></span>**The crow, vulture, dragon, and other symbolic beasts of the Golden Treatise designate specific stages of the Spirit's transformation: the crow signifies the putrefaction and blackening in which the fixed sensual compact is first dissolved; the vulture and its cry 'I am the White of the Black, and the Red of the White' announces the emergent quintessential spirit in its successive colorations.**
    - The Scholiast explains: 'the vulture standing upon the mountain is the new-born quintessential spirit or Proteus; in him all the multifarious virtues of nature are declared to be held in capacity, as in rapid evolution he passes about his axis, making the light manifest without refraction in every variety of its coloring.'
    - "'That which is born of the crow is the beginning of this Art,' Hermes declares; 'the crow, which in the blackness of the night and clearness of the day, flies without wings; from the bitterness existing in the throat the tincture is taken; the red goes forth from his body and from his back is taken a pure water.'" —Hermes Trismegistus
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-014"></span>**The Hermetic sublimation, repeatedly operated, is not a mere elevation of matter to the top of a vessel but a qualitative melioration that urges the life to its utmost vivacity to save itself from death—each dissolution killing a grosser form and reviving a subtler, until the seventh purification produces the crystalline permanence of the Stone.**
    - "Eirenaeus: 'Separate the light from the darkness seven times, and the creation of the philosophic mercury will be complete; this seventh day will be for thee a Sabbath of repose; from which period, until the end of the annual revolution, thou mayest expect the generation of the supernatural son of Sun, who comes about the last age into the world to purify his brethren from their original sin.'" —Eirenaeus Philalethes
    - Section Seven asserts that gold is the ferment Ixir that vivifies all metallic bodies, as dough cannot be fermented without a ferment—and that the key of the philosophers is this fermentation which melioration all bodies and, God assisting, consummates the work.

## Part II: A More Esoteric Consideration of the Hermetic Art and Its Mysteries

### Of the True Subject of the Hermetic Art and Its Concealed Root

Atwood makes her central esoteric argument explicit: the true laboratory of Alchemy is the human being himself, not any external mineral or chemical apparatus—a truth the adepts concealed beneath their metallurgical language but consistently implied through their assertions that the Mercury is 'in thee and about thee,' that Man contains the Universal Mercury in his own highest mineral virtue, and that Self-Knowledge is the root of all Hermetic tradition.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-015"></span>**Modern alchemy and pseudo-alchemy both err by treating the Mercury as an external mineral substance rather than as the vital spirit of human consciousness: the true adepts located the Universal Subject within the blood's arterial circulation and taught that its purification required an inward rational experiment, not laboratory metallurgy.**
    - "Morien tells King Calid: 'This matter, O King, is extracted from thee; in the which mineral thou dost even exist; with thee it is found; by thee it is received; and when thou shalt have proved all by the love and delight in thee, it will increase; and thou wilt know that I have spoken an enduring truth.'" —Morien
    - "Basil Valentine: 'He that knows exactly this golden seed or magnet, and searcheth thoroughly into its properties, he hath the true root of life, and may attain to that which his heart longs for; I intreat all true lovers of mineral science to inquire after this metallic seed, and be assured it is not an idle chimera but a real and certain truth.'" —Basil Valentine
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-016"></span>**Agrippa, Sendivogius, and the adepts collectively assert that Man is the supreme concentration of universal nature—symbolizing with matter, elements, plants, animals, heaven, and angels, and with God in all—and that by knowing himself in this comprehensive sense he would know all things, drawing the Central Salt of Nature to himself in abundance.**
    - "Agrippa states that the soul of man, being estranged from corporeal senses, 'adheres to a divine nature, from which it receives those things which it cannot search into by its own power; for when the mind is free, being stirred up in its proper essence, it comprehends all things'—and thus man is said to be the express image of God." —Cornelius Agrippa
    - The Moorish philosopher concludes: 'the universal orb of the earth contains not so great mysteries and excellences as Man reformed by God into his image; and he that desires the primacy amongst students of nature will nowhere find a greater reserve to obtain his desire than in himself.'
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-017"></span>**The three principles of universal motion—attraction, repulsion, and circulation—constitute the Alchemical Trinity of Salt, Sulphur, and Mercury within the blood's vital circulation; in the natural fallen state, repulsion predominates outwardly, dissipating consciousness in sense, and it is the purpose of the Hermetic art to restore the centripetal attraction so that the soul returns to its First Cause.**
    - "Boehme identifies the operative principle: 'The principle of the operation consists in the power of harmonizing the three discordant principles of Attraction, Repulsion, and Circulation'—this is the vital spirit of the arterial blood where the universal principles are in their natural generation unequally composed." —Jacob Boehme
    - The Hermetic formula 'Si fixum solvas faciasque volare solutum, et volucrem figas, faciunt tutum: Solve, Coagula, Fige' describes the practical reversal of this imbalance by dissolving the dominant fixed sensual compact and coagulating the previously volatile spiritual element.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-018"></span>**Atwood's synthesis of Neoplatonic, Kabbalistic, and Hermetic sources establishes that Self-Knowledge was always understood ontologically and experimentally—not abstractly—and that the ancient injunction 'Know Thyself' inscribed on the temple of Isis pointed to a real vital experiment into the Causal Nature of human consciousness, not a merely psychological reflection.**
    - Aristotle in the Metaphysics declares Wisdom to be the highest science and a philosopher to possess 'a science of all things in intellect—not derived from sensible particulars, but according to that which is universal and absolute in himself'; in the Twelfth Book he demonstrates that when the human Understanding passes into contact with its Antecedent Cause it becomes 'a life in energy' whose knowledge 'is inexpressibly blessed and not to be conceived by such as are not duly initiated.'
    - "Plato declares that 'to know oneself is Wisdom and the highest virtue of the soul; for the soul rightly entering into herself will behold all other things and Deity itself; as verging to her own union and to the center of all life, laying aside multitude and the variety of all manifold powers, she ascends to the highest watchtower of beings.'" —Plato

### Of the Mysteries

Atwood argues that the Greek and Egyptian Mysteries were not theatrical shows or astronomical allegories but real operative disciplines employing something analogous to—but far surpassing—modern Mesmerism, by which the Understanding Spirit was progressively purified of its phantastic defilements and elevated through successive initiations toward a direct intuitive contact with Divine Wisdom.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-019"></span>**Modern Mesmerism provides a first experimental key to the vestibule of the ancient Mysteries because it works in the same 'universal subject'—the medial ethereal spirit—but has remained at the level of therapeutic phenomena, failing entirely to reach the purificative and illuminative purpose that the Theurgists pursued with the same material.**
    - The ordinary mesmeric trance dissolves the sensible medium temporarily but does not purify the phantastic spirit, leaving subjects at the mercy of their own imaginative impressures rather than conducting them to rational self-knowledge; the ancients condemned this passive dreaming and proceeded at once to dissolve the medium more entirely.
    - "Synesius states that the phantastic spirit 'is situated on the confines of the rational and brutal life' and that 'many of the energies of the human life consist from this nature'; he adds that 'we are not accustomed to cogitate without imagination, unless some one should on a sudden be enabled to pass into contact with an intelligible essence.'" —Synesius
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-020"></span>**The Lesser Mysteries—open to all—dissolved the sensible medium and gave a passing experience of the interior life and its phenomena; the Greater Mysteries involved a genuine philosophic death and regeneration, purifying the soul of its natal defilements and elevating it to rational contact with its First Cause.**
    - "Clemens Alexandrinus exclaims: 'O Mysteries truly sacred! O pure Light! At the Light of Torches the veil that covers deity and heaven falls off. I am holy now that I am initiated; it is the Lord himself who is the Hierophant; he sets his seal upon the adept whom he illuminates with his beams.'" —Clemens Alexandrinus
    - "Iamblichus more particularly explains that 'it was by arts divinely potent, and not by theoretic contemplation only, or by mere doctrinal faith, or representations of reality; but by certain ineffable and sublime media that Theurgists became cognizant partakers in the Wisdom of true Being.'" —Iamblichus
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-021"></span>**The philosophical precondition for successful initiation—the purification of the phantastic spirit through willing mortification of its defilements—was scientifically administered in the Mysteries through specific theurgic disciplines, because without this clearing no true intellectual vision is possible, only the delusive 'Superinspection' of subjective imaginings.**
    - Pythagoras instituted long preparations before admitting disciples to deeper mysteries; his biographer relates that 'as dyers previously purify garments and then fix in the colors, so that divine man prepared the souls of those that are lovers of Wisdom; he did not impart specious doctrines or a snare, but possessed a scientific knowledge of things divine and human.'
    - "Olympiodorus: 'The phantasy is an impediment to our intellectual conception; hence when we are agitated by the inspiring influence of divinity and the phantasy intervenes, the enthusiastic energy ceases. Should it be asked whether the soul is able to energize without the phantasy, we reply that the perception of Universals proves that it is able.'" —Olympiodorus

### The Mysteries Continued

Atwood traces the initiatory descent into Hades as a real psycho-spiritual ordeal in which the consciousness, stripped of sensory illusions, encounters the dark chaotic life of its own mineral foundation—the 'Black Saturn' of the adepts, the realm of phantasmagoric demoniacal projections—through which it must pass with rational armament to reach the Elysian light of Wisdom.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-022"></span>**The descent to Hades described in Virgil's Aeneid and the Greek Mysteries corresponds to a real initiatory ordeal in which the consciousness, having passed the sensory medium, enters the dark chaotic ether of its own animal and mineral life—the region Atwood identifies with the 'Black Saturn' of the adepts and the First Matter before its purification.**
    - The vast dark cave Aeneis, 'destitute of elasticity, heavy like that of an enclosed cave, dangerous, giving forth a murky odor like that of graves' in Virgil's description, corresponds to what alchemists call the lapis niger, vilis, foetans—described as 'oritur sicut germinantia,' arising like germinating matter—which is the first manifestation of the Universal Subject in the interior life.
    - "Lully in his Compendium of Alchemy calls the first principles of the Art 'spiritus fugitivi in aero condensati, in forma monstrorum diversorum et animalium etiam hominum, qui vadunt sicut nubes'—fugitive spirits condensed in the air in the shapes of divers monsters, beasts, and men, moving like clouds—calling them 'first principles' because within this material extreme the seed of Spirit is at last found." —Raymond Lully
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-023"></span>**The demoniacal appearances—gods, monsters, and terrifying visions—that overwhelmed uninitiated souls in the Mysteries were not theatrical tricks but real projected images of the individual's own phantastic spirit, generated from within the chaotic life by the self-willed motive principle when it is first made conscious.**
    - "Proclus: 'In the most holy Mysteries, before the presence of the god, the impulsive forms of certain terrestrial demons appear, which call the attention off from undefiled advantages to matter; but on entering the interior part of the Temple, unmoved and guarded by the sacred rites, they genuinely receive into their bosom divine illumination.'" —Proclus
    - "Basil Valentine warns in his Alchemical Chariot that these demonic natures, 'endowed with senses and understanding, know Arts and have in themselves an occult operative life; giving testimony also of their virtue in healing and other secrets, by which they deceive and detain the unwary from the search of better things.'" —Basil Valentine
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-024"></span>**The ultimate goal of the Greater Mysteries—the epopteia, or direct vision of Divine Light—was not a subjective illumination but an objective ontological event in which the purified soul, having passed through all defilements, perceives itself as no longer separate from its First Cause, experiencing Wisdom as the Stoboeus excerpt confirms: 'shining plains and flowery meadows open on all hands, with hymns and dances, and sublime and sacred knowledges, and reverend and holy visions.'**
    - "Apuleius, initiated into the Mysteries of Isis, declares: 'I approached the confines of death, and, having trod the threshold of Proserpine, I returned from it, being carried through all the Elements. At midnight I saw the Sun shining with a splendid light; and I manifestly drew near to the gods above and beneath, and proximately adored them.'" —Apuleius
    - The Chaldaic Oracle distinguishes two kinds of vision: mere 'Superinspection'—seeing lights and hearing voices of the imagination—and true Introspection, when 'the initiated person sees the Divine Light itself without any form or figure,' of which the Oracle says 'Hear the voice of Fire.'

### The Mysteries (Concluded)

Atwood completes her account of the Mysteries by showing that the final Paternal Initiation—the soul's conscious unification with its First Cause—was both the summit of theurgic aspiration and the metaphysical foundation from which all physical transmutations and Hermetic effects were said to flow, with the promise that the regenerated soul would possess universal knowledge, health, wealth, and operative power over the three kingdoms of nature.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-025"></span>**The Elysian Fields—the Meadow of Divine Ideas celebrated in Plato's Phaedrus and by Proclus—represent a real intermediate state of intellectual vision in which the soul, having passed through Tartarus, abides in the living presence of Exemplary Causes and perceives the archetypal pattern of the entire universe in restored conscious union with the medial principle of nature.**
    - "Flamel describes the Mountain of the Seven Metals where 'the philosophers have a garden where the sun remains with a most sweet dew; in the topmost height you will find a royal herb triumphing, which some call mineral, some vegetable, some saturnine'—a description Atwood identifies with the cleansed medial receptacle into which the new-born light arises with all vegetable colors returning." —Nicholas Flamel
    - "Proclus: 'The plain of Truth is intellectually expanded to intelligible Light; the Meadow is the prolific power of life, and of all various reasons, and is the comprehension of the First Efficient causes of life and the generation of Forms; for the meadows which are here are productive of all various forms and bear water which is the symbol of vivification.'" —Proclus
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-026"></span>**The Paternal Initiation—the soul's final identification with the Divine Demiurge—was accomplished not by intellectual effort but by a triple act of love (conjoinment to beauty), hope (expectation of truth), and faith (uncial silence), through which the soul ceased to be a subject regarding an object and entered into simultaneous co-essence with its First Cause.**
    - "Proclus: 'The Discovery of the Father is not that which is doxastic or scientific; for that is syllogistic and composite and does not come into contact with the intellectual essence of the Intellectual Demiurgus. But it is that which subsists according to Intellectual Vision itself: a contact with the Intelligible, and a union with the Demiurgic Intellect.'" —Proclus
    - "Plotinus narrates his own experience: 'Often when by an intellectual energy I am roused from body and converted to myself, perceiving an admirable beauty, I am confident that I am of a more excellent condition than a life merely animal and terrene; I then energize according to the best life and become the same with a nature truly Divine, elevated above every other intelligible, fixed in this sublime eminence as in an ineffable harbour of repose.'" —Plotinus
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-027"></span>**The regenerate soul, having achieved the Paternal identification, was held to possess both intellectual omniscience (knowing all things in their causes) and operative omnipotence (governing all three kingdoms of nature), because the Universal Efficient that sustains all existence was now consciously allied to its will—making the soul what Iamblichus calls 'the familiar and domestic of the gods.'**
    - "Iamblichus confirms that theurgic rites impart 'the best dispositions of the soul as certain concauses; the things which properly excite the Divine will being the Divine Synthemata themselves; and thus things pertaining to the gods are moved by themselves and do not receive from an inferior nature the principle of their energy.'" —Iamblichus
    - The Chaldaic Oracle concludes: 'Having put on the completely armed vigour of resounding Light, with triple strength, fortifying the soul and the mind. O how the world hath Intellectual guides inflexible!'

## Part III: Concerning the Laws and Vital Conditions of the Hermetic Experiment

### Of the Experimental Method and Fermentation of the Philosophic Subject According to the Paracelsian Alchemists and Some Others

Atwood argues that the practical method of Alchemy is grounded in the same rational fermentation of the vital spirit that the Greek Mysteries employed, contrasting the inferior method of passive observation with the ancients' active a priori investigation into causal nature, and establishing that the true Separator—the Rational Light or purified intellectual will—is the sole efficient capable of initiating the Hermetic dissolution.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-028"></span>**Modern Mesmerism represents only a mechanical first-key into the vestibule of the Hermetic art, producing the natural trance without purifying the phantastic spirit or enabling the consciousness to pass under the dominion of another and higher law; the ancients proceeded from this same opening not to therapeutic practice but to a rational dissolution of the underlying medium.**
    - "Roger Bacon declares his intention: 'Volo ovum philosophorum dissolvere et partes philosophici hominis investigare, nam hoc est initium ad alia'—I wish to dissolve the philosophic egg and investigate the parts of the philosophic man, for this is the beginning toward other things." —Roger Bacon
    - The spontaneous fermentation which the Vital Spirit undergoes in the mesmeric trance 'is not taken advantage of in modern practice or pushed to the uttermost; much less is understood that exact art of grafting and transplanting which the ancients practiced, by means of which a growth and sublimation of the Spirit was effected, even to a third, fourth, and fifth degree of concentrated essentiality.'
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-029"></span>**The ancient a priori method of philosophizing—investigating nature from causes downward to effects rather than from effects upward—is demonstrably superior to inductive science because it alone can prove the Causal Efficient, without which knowledge remains perpetually empirical and unable to originate any supernatural result.**
    - "Basil Valentine: 'Learn and look for the first foundation which nature holds concealed; search for it with thine own eyes and hand, that thou mayest be able to philosophize with judgment and build upon the impregnable rock; but without this discovery thou wilt continue a vain and phantastic trifler, whose discoursings, without experience, are built upon sand.'" —Basil Valentine
    - "Vaughan: 'advise them to use their hands and not their fancies, and to change their abstractions into extractions; for verily as long as they lick the shell after their fashion, and pierce not experimentally into the center of things, they cannot know things intrinsically, but only describe them by their outward effects and motions, which are subject and obvious to every common eye.'" —Thomas Vaughan
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-030"></span>**The Rational Ferment—the purified intellectual will acting as a true Separator upon the vital spirit—is both the most difficult discovery and the sole efficient of the Hermetic art; it is what Paracelsus calls 'Separation, the greatest miracle in philosophy' and what adepts from Pontanus to Basil Valentine identify as the Key to which all subsequent operations reduce.**
    - "Pontanus confesses: 'seek to know this Fire with all thy soul, that so thou mayest attain to thy desire; for it is the key of all the philosophers which they have never openly revealed. But profound meditation alone can give it to thee so thou mayest discern.'" —Pontanus
    - "Basil Valentine and Kirchringius: 'Know that this true tincture of Antimony is not made of crude melted antimony such as the apothecaries sell, but is extracted from the true mineral as it is taken from the mountains; and how that extraction should be made is a principal secret in which the whole art of Alchemy consists.'" —Kirchringius
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-031"></span>**The heroic myths—Jason, Hercules, Orpheus, Aeneas, Achilles—are all Hermetic allegories in which the Rational Ferment (the hero) suffers the death of its Understanding companion (Patroclus, Eurydice, Misenus, Hylas) before its own divine armament is forged and it enters the transformative combat with the selfhood on the plain of truth.**
    - Just as Achilles' celestial armor is given by Thetis and forged by Vulcan only after Patroclus is slain, and just as Aeneas must celebrate Misenus' funeral rites before the Sibyl can lead him to the golden bough, the adept's heroic intellectual will is only aroused to its full power after the medial understanding life is sacrificed in the work.
    - The Palingenius verses summarize the process: 'Take this Arcadian slippery lad that's apt to fly / And in the glittering Stygian lake, drowned let him die / Then in a warm womb placed, his taint dissolve / Whose dropping limbs a spirit shall devolve / Dead by degrees, shall bring to life anew / All clad in robes of gold and silver hue.'

### A Further Analysis of the Initial Principle and Its Education into Light

Atwood examines the First Matter or Initial Principle of Alchemy through the convergent lenses of Kabbalistic ontology, German Idealism, Plotinian metaphysics, and theosophical testimony, arguing that what these traditions separately designate as the Unknown, the Non-Being, the Pure Subject, and the Abyss are all experientially equivalent descriptions of the same primordial state that is generated in human consciousness immediately antecedent to the regenerative birth of the Divine Light.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-032"></span>**The Kabalistic 'Unknown' or Ain Soph—described as neither mind, figure, nor self-comprehension until it produces a point of Light within itself—corresponds precisely to what the adepts call the First Matter: a state of primordial indefiniteness that is not true non-being but the infinite sufficience prior to all manifestation, accessible to the soul that has been brought through dissolution to its ultimate recessure.**
    - "Rabbi Ben Jochai describes the Ancient of Days: 'He is the Mystery of Mysteries, and most unknown of the unknown; from his head he shakes a dew which awakens the dead and brings them to new life—its aspect is white, as the diamond is white, the color which contains all.'" —Rabbi Ben Jochai
    - Hegel's declaration that 'pure Being is merely an indeterminate thought, simple and immediate—but this pure Being is no other than a pure abstraction, it is a term absolutely negative, which may also be called non-Being' is recognized by Atwood as reaching by rational abstraction the same frontier that the Kabalists affirm through ontological experience.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-033"></span>**Jacob Boehme's autobiographical account of his illumination—seeing 'the Bysse or ground or original foundation; and the Abysse that is without ground; also the birth or eternal generation of the Holy Trinity; the descent and original of this world'—is adduced as modern experimental confirmation that the Hermetic ontological ground is genuinely accessible and not a theoretical construct.**
    - Boehme: 'I was not without some satisfaction, I assure you; suggesting only that That which is, in his mind, so mere a nothing, becomes in that of a philosopher to be the All in all.'
    - "Boehme reports: 'the gate was opened to me, so that in one quarter of an hour I saw and knew more than if I had been many years at the University; I saw and knew the Being of beings, the Bysse or ground; I knew and saw in myself all the Three Worlds—the divine angelical or paradisiacal; and then the dark world; and the external world of substance expressed from the internal.'" —Jacob Boehme
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-034"></span>**Plotinus's technical account of how Matter is known—not as a positive object but as 'that which subsists according to the subject which is indefinite,' apprehended by a 'defective or ablative reasoning' that passes through reason to its own dissolution—confirms that the First Matter is met at the boundary where rational consciousness, having exhausted all determinate content, enters a state Atwood calls 'that obscure oblivion which is the Original of Life.'**
    - "Plotinus: 'When the mind is in the Night of Matter, she suffers such a passion as when she receives the being of that which is formless; her perception is obscure, vast, and infinite; until pained with the void of the retreating infinitude, the soul retracts, rallying about her last deserted Unit, and not enduring any longer to stop at nonentity, becomes into true Being.'" —Plotinus
    - "Porphyry: 'if you investigate nothing else, being established in yourself and in your own Essence, you will be assimilated to the Intelligible Universe; when any one is present with that which is present in himself, then he is present with true Being; but when you withdraw from yourself, then likewise you recede from real being.'" —Porphyry
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-035"></span>**The convergence of Empedocles, Thales, Anaxagoras, Parmenides, Pythagoras, and Plato on the same Ultimate Subject—each naming it differently (fire, water, intellect, one, number) but describing properties that are incompatible with common elements—reveals that all early Greek philosophy was grounded in a shared ontological experience, and that their apparent contradictions are differences of vantage point upon the same ineffable reality.**
    - "Empedocles: 'All things in union now through love conspire, and now through strife divulsed are borne along; hence when again emerging into light the One is seen, 'tis from the many formed. All mortals, too, so far as they are born, of permanent duration are deprived; but through this unmoved for ever they remain, like a sphere rolling round its center firm.'" —Empedocles
    - Aristotle himself, despite his critique of predecessors' methods, praises Anaxagoras for recognizing Intellect as First Cause: 'he appeared like a sober man among those who were drunk, by asserting that Intellect was the principle of beauty and of the right order of things.'

### Of the Manifestations of the Philosophic Matter

Atwood argues that the Philosophic Matter, once generated through the Hermetic dissolution, manifests as a real, sensibly perceptible, luminous Essence—described consistently across traditions as white, crystalline, and prolific—that is the immediate precursor of the Philosopher's Stone, and that its phenomenology, as attested by Boehme, Van Helmont, Basil Valentine, and others, constitutes physical evidence of the reality of the regenerative process.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-036"></span>**The Philosophic Matter appears to the regenerate consciousness as a luminous white substance—described by Sendivogius as 'a water white as snow that wets not the hands,' by Basil Valentine as the beginning of the Great Work in the cavern of the Saturnian Hill, and by Khunrath as the 'Green Lion' drawn forth from its hiding—representing the first visible manifestation of the Universal Spirit freed from its mineral imprisonment.**
    - "Khunrath describes the First Operation: 'From Adrop, which in its proper tongue is called Saturn (the lead of the Wise), our heart of Saturn being relaxed, the Green Duenech and the Vitriol of Venus appear—the Green Lion, lurking and concealed, is drawn forth from the Cavern of his Saturnine Hill; the blood copiously flowing from his wounds is diligently collected; the mud earth, partaking of Adam, the First Matter of the creation, is made manifest.'" —Khunrath
    - "Vaughan describes the Matter observed by an adept friend: 'a fat mineral nature it was, bright like pearls and transparent like crystal; it appeared somewhat spermatic; some of the liquor I took up in my hand, it was not common water but a certain kind of oil of a watery complexion—a stream more large than any river in her full channel, descending without any noise, like the still soft air.'" —Thomas Vaughan
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-037"></span>**The internal spectacle of the Philosophic Material—the visions of rivers, mountains, metals, vegetation, colored waters, and gemstones—described by the adept Grasseus as appearing in a circular vessel 'like the figure of the great world,' represents not fantasy but the soul's actual perception of the Universal Nature in its generic principles, as light upon the waters of its own archetypal creation.**
    - "Grasseus narrates: 'Within this fabric was a certain mass moving circularly, representing the very figure of the great world; earth standing in the middest of all, compassed about with most clear waters, rising up to several hillocks and craggy rocks, bearing many sorts of fruit; the waters full of a certain transparent salt—now white, now red, then yellow and purpled—all things actuated with their own appropriate fire, but imperceptible as yet and ethereal.'" —Grasseus
    - Atwood identifies this spectacle with Solomon's account: 'God hath given to me knowledge of the things that are, namely to know how the world was made, and the operations of the elements, the beginning, ending, and midst of the times; and all such things as are either secret or manifest, them I know. For Wisdom which is the Worker of all things taught me.'
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-038"></span>**The Hermetic practice is ultimately identical with Christian regeneration: the Philosopher's Stone is Ruach Elohim made manifest in the blood through a systematic death and resurrection of the vital principles—a claim Atwood grounds in the Divine Poemander's account of Hermes' own illumination and in Paul's doctrine of the spiritual body raised in incorruption.**
    - "Khunrath defines the Stone: 'Ruach Elohim, which moved upon the face of the waters, the firmament being in the midst, conceived and made body, truly and sensibly, in the virgin womb of the greater world; the Son, born into the light of the macrocosm, consubstantial and like his father the lesser world; universal, triune, hermaphrodite; visible, sensible, made manifest by itself regeneratively by the obstetric hand of the Physico-Chemical Art.'" —Khunrath
    - "Hermes in the Poemander: 'I saw an infinite Light; then a darkness made in part, fearful and hideous, changed into a certain moist nature unspeakably troubled; then from that Light a certain holy Word joined itself unto Nature, and out flew the pure and unmixed fire from the moist nature upward on high; and the Spirit was carried upon the waters—I am that Light, the Mind, thy God.'" —Hermes Trismegistus

### Of the Mental Requisites and Impediments Incidental to Individuals, Either as Masters or Students, in the Hermetic Art

Atwood systematically catalogs the moral, intellectual, and dispositional requirements for success in the Hermetic art—drawing on Geber's taxonomy of impediments, Norton's ordinal prescriptions, and the testimonies of numerous adepts—concluding that avarice, skepticism, instability, phantasy, and vice are absolute bars to the discovery, while rectitude of intention, patience, piety, prayer, and a cooperative partner are essential conditions.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-039"></span>**Geber's taxonomy of natural impediments to Alchemy identifies five disqualifying mental defects: physical impotence of the organs, souls lacking in rectitude or reason, fantastical and easily-opinionating minds, unstable wills that shift from purpose to purpose, and avarice—each of which prevents the discovery for structural reasons rooted in the nature of the Art itself.**
    - "Geber states: 'there are some who have a soul movable from opinion to opinion and from will to will, as those who suddenly believe a thing and will the same without any ground at all of reason; and a little after do believe another thing; these, being so changeable, can ill accomplish the least of what they intend, but rather leave it defective.'" —Geber
    - "Geber identifies the covetous as perhaps the deepest impediment: 'slaves loving money, who affirm this to be indeed an admirable science but are afraid to interposit the necessary charges—therefore our science comes not to them.'" —Geber
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-040"></span>**Prayer is not a superstitious adjunct to the Hermetic work but its essential operative complement: Iamblichus's three-stage theory of prayer—collective, concordant, and sealing—corresponds to the three stages of the Hermetic process, and adepts universally attest that crucial discoveries came not from rational calculation but as sudden infusions from above in moments of concentrated desire.**
    - "Kirchringius testifies: 'every man knows, that hath entirely devoted himself to this business, how effectual prayer is, and how often those things which he long sought and could not find have been imparted to him in a moment, as it were infused from above; that also is of use in solving riddles and enigmatical writings—if you burn with a great desire of knowing them, that is prayer; and when you incline your mind to this or that, variously discussing, this is cooperation.'" —Kirchringius
    - "Iamblichus: 'no operation in sacred concerns can succeed without the intervention of prayer; the continual exercise of prayer nourishes the vigour of our intellect and renders the receptacles of the soul far more capacious for the communications of the gods; it is the Divine Key which opens to men the penetralia of Wisdom.'" —Iamblichus
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-041"></span>**The practical organization of the Hermetic work—vessels, furnaces, servants, places, and astrological timing—all must be subordinated to the rational discernment of the One True Vessel and One True Fire, which nature reveals progressively to those who proceed rationally, but which no one can determine in advance from books alone.**
    - Norton's Ordinal provides detailed guidance on vessel shapes for different operations—solution, sublimation, calcination, circulation, precipitation—and concludes by revealing the Master's secret: 'Si Deus non dedisset nobis vas, Nihil dedisset—if God had not given us a vessel, He had given us nothing; and that vessel is glass.'
    - Norton specifies that the Gross Work requires eight servants working continuously in shifts, never resorting to ribaldry (lest they destroy parts of the work), and that either all must be men or all women—they must not be intermixed—and even then must have proper disport to relieve their great labors.

## Part IV: The Hermetic Practice

### Of the Vital Purification, Commonly Called the Gross Work

Atwood describes the first practical stage of the Hermetic art—the Gross Work—as a sustained manual labor upon the vital spirit aimed at the progressive dissolution of its phantastic defilements, which she identifies with a specialized application of vital magnetic technique guided by the rational ferment, requiring patience, diligence, and the repeated triturition of the natural life in its own blood.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-042"></span>**The Gross Work consists primarily in the application of the Hand as an operative instrument for dissolving and purifying the Philosophic Subject through a sustained manual trituration; this is what the adepts mean by 'Linear work,' 'the way of the Hand,' and Lully's maxim that 'the Mercury of the Wise comes not but by help of ingenuity and the Manual operations of man.'**
    - "Van Helmont: 'The Hand is the instrument of instruments, which the soul likewise useth, as a means by which it bears its image into operation'—and accordingly the adepts describe the whole Gross Work as performed by manual application rather than by any apparatus of stills, alembics, or furnaces understood literally." —Jan Baptist van Helmont
    - "Eirenaeus: 'I made not five wrong experiments before I found the true way; the Gross Work is no way easy that it should be considered as a mental recreation, since a concentrated attention is necessary; and it is by assiduity and industry that we accomplish the First Work—as Morien gravely warns King Calid, many philosophers have been overcome with the fatigue of this work.'" —Eirenaeus Philalethes
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-043"></span>**Excessive haste is the cardinal error of the Gross Work: the dissolution must proceed slowly and continuously because premature excitation of the internal agent before the circumferences are prepared causes a disseveration in the Chaos that destroys the work, as Basil Valentine's warning about Icarus's burnt feathers and Hermes' admonition to 'forbear the flying' indicate.**
    - "Basil Valentine: 'Cause wings to be prepared for the Matter by Juno, Bacchus, and Vulcan; but as you love your life, permit it not to fly suddenly, rather deliver it to Mercury to be instructed by him gradually to accustom itself to flying; bind it with a cord lest, like Icarus, having its unproved feathers burnt, it fall headlong into the sea.'" —Basil Valentine
    - Norton's Ordinal observes: 'the length of cleansing matters infected deceiveth much people; excess of one half quarter of an hour may destroy all; remember that water will bubble and boil, but butter must simmer and also oil; and so with long leisure it will waste and not with bubbling made in haste.'
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-044"></span>**The philosophic path follows the same mythological template as the Heroic quests—Aeneas seeking the golden bough, Jason seeking the fleece, Achilles avenging Patroclus—because all these allegories encode the same sequence: dissolution of the medial understanding companion, arousal of the heroic rational ferment, its descent through darkness into the mineral life, and its eventual extraction of the aurific seed.**
    - Atwood identifies the philosophic Gross Work with the descent to Hades in the Aeneid: 'facilis descensus Averni / Sed revocare gradum superasque evadere ad auras / Hoc opus, hic labor est'—the easy descent is the natural dissolution of the sensible medium; the labor is the re-ascent with the golden bough of purified intellectual faith.
    - The Green Lion Hunt poem—'And yet within he hath such heat / That when he hath the Sun up eat / He bringeth him to more perfection / Than ever he had by Nature's direction'—describes the initial phase of the Gross Work in which the crude mineral spirit, aroused, devours and transmutes the solar ferment into a higher conjunction.

### Of the Philosophic or Subtle Work

Atwood describes the Philosophic or Subtle Work as the triadic sequence of operations by which the purified spirit, freed through the Gross Work, is progressively sublimed through three successive circulations—corresponding to the mineral, vegetable, and divine kingdoms—until the Stone is fermented with its proper solar gold and the Universal Efficient is made manifest in the fourth and final Form.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-045"></span>**The Philosophic Work operates through a triadic causal structure—analogous to Aristotle's four causes but executed in vital practice—in which the First Principle is the essential cause (the Divine Will), the Second is matter (the passive ethereal body), the Third is the motive source (the Rational Ferment in operation), and the Fourth is the Final Cause (the Light of conscious union with Divinity) that the whole process serves.**
    - The alchemical maxim 'Primus dicatur in quo sensus dominatur; sensibus aequato gaudet natura Secundo; Tertius excedit, cujus tolerantia laedit; Destructor sensus nescit procedere Quarto' encapsulates the four ascending degrees of the Philosophic Fire and their operative relations.
    - "Boehme articulates the ultimate aim: 'meditate and study theosophically to reduce the Ternary by the Quaternary, through the rejection of the Binary, to the simplicity of the Monad; that thy body, soul, and spirit be gathered to rest in the name of Jesus—salvation is nothing else but a Transmutation.'" —Jacob Boehme
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-046"></span>**The Philosophic Work culminates in the fermentation of the Stone with its proper metal—silver for the White, gold for the Red—a process identical to the Eucharistic mystery in which the Divine leaven transforms the whole lump, and which Atwood explicitly equates with the Christian doctrine of regeneration through the Body of Christ.**
    - "Khunrath identifies the Stone with Ruach Elohim and concludes: 'After the ashy colour and the white and the yellow, thou shalt behold the Stone of the Philosophers; our King and Lord of hosts go forth from the chamber of his glassy sepulcher into this mundane sphere in his glorified body, regenerate and in perfection perfected; as a shining carbuncle, whose parts, most subtle and most pure, are inseparably blent together in the harmonious rest of union into one.'" —Khunrath
    - The Chaldaic Oracle describes the culminating radiation: 'Fire, the derivation and dispenser of Fire, whose hair pointed is seen in his native Light; hence comes Saturn, the Sun Assessor beholding the Pure Pole'—which Atwood identifies with the midnight Sun of Apuleius, the Triumphal Chariot of Antimony, and the Stone with the new name written in Revelation.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-047"></span>**The Quadrature of the Circle—the ancient mathematical-metaphysical problem—is said by adepts to be supernaturally demonstrated in the Philosophic Work: when active and passive principles are taken through three circulatory fermentations, their convergence forms a geometric progression from point to line to square to cube, which by further multiplication becomes a Fifth Essence circling back into its source.**
    - "Maier's emblem formula: 'Foemina masque unus fiant tibi circulus, ex quo Surgat habens aequum forma quadrata latus; Hinc Trigonum ducas, omni qui parte rotundam In sphaeram redeat: Tum Lapis ortus erit'—make of male and female one circle, from which rises the square, thence draw the triangle, let it return to the sphere: then the Stone will arise." —Michael Maier
    - The concluding Hermetic instruction: 'The Conjunction in which all the mysteries of the Microcosm have their consummation is the true circulated Form of Gold; the tetraptive Conjunction that so highly commended Fountain of Pythagoras, the Divine Tetractys—whence all our Wisdom springs and which contains Perennial nature's fountain, cause, and root.'

### The Six Keys of Eudoxus

Eudoxus's Six Keys provide a sequential practical guide to the Hermetic operation: Key One extracts the sulphur from the body and forms the initial Stone; Key Two performs the philosophic sublimation that separates and converts the elements; Key Three—the most extensive and most important—details the purification of Mercury through the separation and reunion of the three principles; Keys Four through Six treat the terrification of the spirit, its fermentation with the perfect body, and the multiplication of the Stone.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-048"></span>**The First Key works upon the impure compound of the Stone by separating the black crow-head from the luminous tincture within it, whitening the dark fumes through philosophical fire, until the resplendent white Stone appears containing the blood of the Pelican—a process Eudoxus calls 'cutting off the head of the Black Dragon and the Crow.'**
    - "Eudoxus: 'It is by separation of the black, foul, and stinking fume of the Blackest Black that our astral, white, and resplendent Stone is formed, which contains in its veins the blood of the Pelican; it is at this First Purification of the Stone and at this shining whiteness that the work of the First Key is ended.'" —Eudoxus
    - The philosophical sublimation of the Second Key is not 'a mere elevation of the subject to the top of the vessel' but a qualitative melioration each time it succeeds—urging life to its utmost vivacity to save itself from death; the Secret Fire that accomplishes it 'is the only instrument which can work this sublimation, and no philosopher has ever openly revealed it.'
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-049"></span>**The Third Key—the longest and most important—reveals the secret of the Two Mercuries: the White Mercury (Lunaria, 'the virgin's milk') and the Red Mercury (the sharp vinegar), which must be prepared separately from the same matter and never confounded, for their proper conjunction under the law of fermentation produces the Universal Medicine and the true Philosophical Gold.**
    - "Eudoxus: 'I have unfolded to you a great mystery which the philosophers who have spoken thereof have passed over very slightly: prepare the bath of the King with the blood of the Innocents, and you will have the animated Mercury of the Wise, which never loses its virtue if you keep it in a vessel well closed; for there is so much sympathy between the purified bodies and the spirits that they never quit one another when united, because this union resembles that of the soul with the glorified body.'" —Eudoxus
    - The Cosmopolite's formula is decoded: 'give our old man gold and silver to swallow that he may consume them, and then he dying may be burned, and his ashes scattered into the water, and thou shalt boil that water until it is enough, thou shalt have a medicine to cure the leprosy'—the Old Man being the Mercury whose satiation and death produces the Universal Tincture.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-050"></span>**Keys Four through Six detail the terrification of the spirit (reducing the freed Mercury to its incombustible earth through boiling and coagulation), its fermentation with pure Gold or Silver as the leaven of a new paste, and the virtually unlimited multiplication of the Stone by successive imbibitions, each tenfold increase raising the transmutative virtue beyond all calculation.**
    - "Eirenaeus confirms the multiplication: 'Take one part of your Perfect Stone and join it with three or four parts of the Mercury of the First Work; in seven days all previous operations will pass rapidly; the virtue is augmented a thousand times; repeat with fresh Mercury and the next multiplication completes in three days; a third repetition in one hour; a fourth in a moment—so that at the fifth multiplication you will no longer be able to calculate the strength of the medicine.'" —Eirenaeus Philalethes
    - The Lucerna Salis confirms that 'one drachm of gold is sufficient for the fermentation in the Red; with one single very small part the tincture may afterwards be augmented more and more; if this medicine be multiplied and again dissolved and coagulated by the water of its mercury, the tinging virtue will be augmented each time by ten degrees in perfection, which may be reiterated at will.'

### The Conclusion

Atwood concludes by summarizing the entire argument—that Alchemy is a real experimental art grounded in the vital purification of the human spirit, that its true Stone is the concentrated Light of Universal Nature made manifest through the soul's dissolution and regeneration, and that the recovery of this art is not only possible but urgently needed at a time when external science has exhausted its capacity to satisfy the rational and moral demands of humanity.

* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-051"></span>**The central thesis of the entire work is restated: Man is the true laboratory of Alchemy; the Universal Spirit circulating in the human blood is the genuine First Matter; and the Philosopher's Stone is that Spirit concentrated, fermented, and sublimed through repeated dissolution and regeneration until the Divine Efficient is made consciously manifest in the fully purified soul.**
    - The whole Hermetic art was shown to rest upon three interconnected propositions: that there is a Universal Subject in nature; that this Subject is susceptible of nourishment and development in Man above all other creatures; and that 'man should be able not only to find the Divine Nature but to effect It'—which Atwood calls 'the greatest mystery, of all mysteries the most wonderful.'
    - The convergence of Greek metaphysics, Hebrew Kabbalah, Neoplatonism, theosophical mysticism, and Christian doctrine upon the same experimental tradition confirms that the Hermetic mystery is not the eccentric invention of any one culture but the universal ground of all genuine religious science.
* <span id="hermetic-philosophy-and-alchemy-a-suggestive-inqui-052"></span>**Atwood's diagnosis of the present cultural crisis is that modern inductive science has irrevocably exhausted the path of external investigation without arriving at causal truth, while moral and religious institutions have lost the vital experimental ground that once made them efficacious—leaving both domains in urgent need of the recovered Hermetic Science.**
    - The Hermetic Science, if real, 'would include both external and internal philosophy in the ample compass of its experiment, as passing from either extreme of Mind and matter to prove them, it arrives at the Catholic Effect of Life; and here the external and internal worlds are said to blend together in confluent harmony, establishing each other, leaving reason nothing more to doubt or the senses to desire.'
    - Atwood closes with a vision of the coming Fourth Monarchy of the Intellect: 'when Wisdom shall have effected that individual reformation which above all things we now need, it is vain to look in externals for a perfection and felicity which have not been imaged there; we may alter, and improve, and educate, and prepare the way with advantage; but the notion will be variable and every plan defective without the Exemplary Light.'
