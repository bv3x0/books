## Introduction

The Secret of the Golden Flower is a lay manual combining Buddhist and Taoist methods for mental freedom, using the golden flower as a symbol for the awakening of the mind's original light and hidden potential.

* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-001"></span>**The golden flower symbolizes the quintessence of Buddhism and Taoism, where gold represents the light of mind itself and the flower represents the blossoming or opening up of that mental light**
    - The practice aims to restore the original God-given spirit and become a self-realized human being
    - In Buddhist terms, a realized person is conscious of the original mind in its spontaneous natural state
    - This original spirit is also called the celestial mind or natural mind
    - The method involves getting to the root source of awareness itself
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-002"></span>**The golden flower method requires no apparatus, dogma, or special ritual, but is practiced in daily life as a direct way to free the mind from arbitrary limitations imposed by habitual fixation on mental contents**
    - The practice is near at hand, being in the mind itself, yet involves no imagery or thought
    - It opens an avenue to an endless source of intuition, creativity, and inspiration
    - Once developed, the power of mental awakening can be renewed and deepened without limit
    - The experience is likened to 'light in the sky, a sky of awareness vaster than images, thoughts, and feelings'
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-003"></span>**Richard Wilhelm's 1929 German translation was the first Western version but was based on a garbled translation of a truncated and corrupted text, leading to dangerous misunderstandings despite C.G. Jung's influential commentary**
    - Jung credited the book with clarifying his work on the unconscious but maintained serious reservations about the practice
    - A critical communication gap occurred in transmission, yet the book became a major influence in Western psychology
    - Gary Baynes hailed it as 'the secret of the power of growth latent in the psyche'
    - Wilhelm's version contained 'dangerous and misleading contaminations'
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-004"></span>**The golden flower awakening is so fundamental that it brings out inner dimensions in all religions, allowing Taoist organizations to include Confucians, Buddhists, Jews, Christians, and Muslims without requiring them to break from their congregations**
    - Wilhelm noted this religious inclusivity in his time
    - The central experience transcends whether one calls it God, the Way, holy spirit, Buddha nature, or real self
    - The Tao Te Ching says 'Names can be designated, but they are not fixed terms'
    - The pragmatic purpose is to elicit experience, not inculcate doctrines

## Questions and Answers Opening up the Mysteries of the Doctrine of the Golden Flower

This section provides practical clarifications through dialogue format, addressing common misconceptions about quietude versus activity, the nature of mind observation, and the relationship between internal and external practice.

* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-050"></span>**People wrongly suppose attainment is possible in quietude but lost in activity, not realizing that loss through activity occurs because nothing is truly attained through stillness**
    - When you keep presence of mind, only then do you have autonomy
    - When you have autonomy, only then can you manage affairs
    - Presence of mind is easily interrupted but becomes unbroken through long practice
    - With continuity, light shines bright and energy becomes full
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-051"></span>**Observing mind means observing the purity of mind, which is basically nondual and one vital reality throughout past and future, allowing transcendence to enlightenment stage without leaving sense objects**
    - Observation can be deep or shallow, forced or spontaneous
    - There is observation outside sense objects, within sense objects, neither internal nor external, and panoramic
    - When observation is deep and illusion cleared, this is true emptiness
    - Turning light around is done by mind, not eyes - the mind is the eyes
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-052"></span>**The light seen before your eyes is 'rat-light,' not the light of tiger-eye or dragon-vitality, since the light of mind belongs neither inside nor outside**
    - Looking to see it with physical eyes is bedevilment
    - Everyone already has the lamp of mind, but it must be lit to shine for immortality
    - Don't forget the mind and allow spirit to be obscured
    - If you have no autonomy, vital spirit diffuses
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-053"></span>**The great Way requires transforming vitality into energy and refining energy into spirit, keeping the clear and removing the polluted, but external work has no connection to the great Way**
    - The minor technique of circulating energy can enhance body and extend life span
    - But supposing the great Way requires physical body work is a tangential teaching
    - True practice first requires vitality transformation, and this vitality is not sexual
    - Few are calm and serious, rare are those sincere and unified

## Translator's Afterword: Modern Applications of the Golden Flower Method

Thomas Cleary explains his personal experience with the golden flower method and explores its potential applications in modern psychotherapy, particularly for understanding mood and personality disorders through the host-guest metaphor.

* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-054"></span>**Cleary was introduced to the golden flower practice of 'turning the light around' long before knowing of this book's existence, finding the method extremely powerful and versatile for mindfulness work**
    - He spent years studying its use and looking for tested information about objective application
    - This led to studies of classical sourcebooks in Chinese, Sanskrit, and Japanese
    - He began translating relevant Buddhist and Taoist texts for their psychological insights
    - The practice revealed unsuspected dimensions in literature of other religions
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-055"></span>**Wilhelm's translation was practically dysfunctional due to enough flaws in grammar, terminology, and conceptual structures, while Jung's psychoanalysis was not actually equivalent to the golden flower practice**
    - Wilhelm had no facilities for learning the required languages and symbol systems
    - Jung warned against practice but professed psychoanalysis as Western equivalent
    - Jung's reasoning about Europeans lacking cultural basis had some truth but wasn't definitive
    - The problem was in cult behavior, not in the object of practice
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-056"></span>**The teaching distinguishes between 'original spirit' (formless essence of awareness) and 'conscious spirit' (conditioned mind-set), with the goal of refining conscious spirit to reunite with original spirit**
    - Intuition belongs to original spirit, intellect to conscious spirit
    - In Chan Buddhism, original spirit is host while conscious spirit is guest
    - Self-delusion occurs when servant takes over from master
    - Self-enlightenment restores master's autonomy in the center
* <span id="the-secret-of-the-golden-flower-the-classic-chines-057"></span>**The method could illuminate understanding and treatment of mood and personality disorders by providing objective perspective through the 'four relations between host and guest' in Chan teaching**
    - Guest within guest: ordinary mind going from mood to mood, alienated from the host
    - Host within guest: first stage of turning light around with contact established
    - Guest within host: free access to thoughts without being bound by them
    - Host within host: original spirit itself, the primal source of consciousness
