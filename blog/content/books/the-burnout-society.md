## Neuronal Power

The twenty-first century is a neuronal rather than immunological age: its illnesses stem not from the negativity of the foreign Other but from an excess of positivity—the too-much-of-the-Same—that immunological and viral models of violence cannot explain.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-001"></span>**The neurological illnesses that define the incipient twenty-first century—depression, ADHD, borderline personality disorder, burnout—are not infections but infarctions, following not from immunological negativity but from an excess of positivity, and thus elude all techniques aimed at combating what is foreign.**
    - A bacterial age ended with antibiotics and a viral age was left behind through immunological technology; the present is determined neither by bacteria nor viruses but by neurons.
    - These pathologies follow a dialectic of positivity rather than negativity: burnout occurs when the ego overheats from too much of the Same; the 'hyper' in hyperactivity is the massification of the positive, not an immunological category.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-002"></span>**The immunological paradigm, structured by the Cold War vocabulary of attack and defense against Otherness, is being superseded because otherness and foreignness are disappearing, replaced by a difference that provokes no immunoreaction and represents, immunologically, the Same.**
    - Every immunoreaction is a reaction to Otherness; postmodern difference lacks the sting of foreignness, so foreignness is deactivated into a formula of consumption—the alien gives way to the exotic and the tourist-consumer is no longer an immunological subject.
    - The immunologically organized world had a topology of borders, walls, and thresholds; globalization and general promiscuity are incompatible with immunologically effective Otherness.
    - When a paradigm becomes an object of reflection, its demise is often at hand—the currency of immunological discourse signals its vanishing, not its dominance.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-003"></span>**Roberto Esposito's theory of immunitas is refuted because his examples—epidemics, extradition of foreign heads of state, illegal immigration, computer viruses—are objects from the past; today even the immigrant is not an immunological Other but is perceived as a burden rather than a threat.**
    - Esposito reads unrelated news events (epidemic, extradition, immigration barriers, computer virus) under the single interpretive category of immunization as a protective response to risk.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-004"></span>**Baudrillard's theory of violence fails because it describes the violence of positivity—the totalitarianism of the Same—in immunological terms, treating viral violence as still obeying the scheme of an enemy that intrudes and is fought off from within.**
    - "He who lives by the Same shall die by the Same." —Jean Baudrillard
    - Baudrillard's genealogy of hostility moves through the enemy as wolf (external attacker), rat (underground), insect, and finally virus—'a ghostly enemy' welling up from all the interstices of power, with terrorism as the revolt of the singular against the global.
    - The genealogy of hostility does not coincide with the genealogy of violence: the violence of positivity requires no hostility and unfolds in a permissive, pacified society, making it more invisible than viral violence.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-005"></span>**Neuronal violence is a terror of immanence: system-immanent rather than system-foreign, it saturates and exhausts rather than deprives or excludes, and therefore remains inaccessible to unmediated perception, unlike the immunological horror embodied by Medusa as radical alterity.**
    - Medusa is the immunological Other in extreme form—radical alterity one cannot behold without perishing; neuronal violence has no negativity and escapes all immunological optics.

## Beyond Disciplinary Society

Foucault's disciplinary society of prohibition (Should/May Not) has been replaced by an achievement society of the affirmative Can, where the subject becomes an entrepreneur of itself whose freedom coincides with self-exploitation, producing depressives rather than madmen and criminals.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-006"></span>**Twenty-first-century society is not Foucault's disciplinary world of prohibition but an achievement society governed by the positive modal verb Can, whose inhabitants are achievement-subjects and entrepreneurs of themselves rather than obedience-subjects.**
    - Disciplinary society is governed by negativity (May Not, Should) and produces madmen and criminals; achievement society discards negativity through deregulation and produces depressives and losers.
    - The social unconscious switches from Should to Can because the positivity of Can is more efficient than the negativity of Should for maximizing productivity—continuity, not a break, holds across the shift, since Can does not revoke Should.
    - Yes, we can
* <span id="the-burnout-society-007"></span>**Alain Ehrenberg wrongly locates depression solely in the imperative to be oneself; in reality it is the pressure to achieve—the new commandment of late-modern labor society—together with impoverished attachment that causes exhaustive depression, so burnout expresses the exhausted, burnt-out soul rather than the exhausted self.**
    - "The depressed individual is unable to measure up; he is tired of having to become himself." —Alain Ehrenberg
    - Ehrenberg equates the present human type with Nietzsche's 'sovereign man,' but Han argues it is Nietzsche's 'last man,' who does nothing but work and lacks all sovereignty.
    - Depression erupts when the achievement-subject is 'no longer able to be able'; the complaint 'Nothing is possible' can only occur in a society that thinks 'Nothing is impossible.'
* <span id="the-burnout-society-008"></span>**The disappearance of external domination does not produce freedom but makes freedom and constraint coincide: the achievement-subject gives itself over to compulsive freedom and auto-exploitation, becoming simultaneously perpetrator and victim, master and slave, waging an internalized war on itself.**
    - Auto-exploitation is more efficient than allo-exploitation because the feeling of freedom attends it; the exploiter is simultaneously the exploited and perpetrator and victim can no longer be distinguished.

## Profound Boredom

Excess positivity produces an overload of stimuli that fragments attention into a shallow, animal-like hyperattention (multitasking), destroying the deep contemplative attention on which all cultural achievement depends and displacing the profound boredom that nurtures creativity.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-009"></span>**Multitasking represents not civilizational progress but a regression to the vigilant, scattered attention of wild animals, which cannot immerse themselves contemplatively because they must constantly monitor background threats; late-modern society is thus returning to a wilderness where concern for survival displaces the good life.**
    - A feeding animal must simultaneously guard prey from rivals, watch for predators, protect its young, and keep an eye on its mate, so it can never be contemplatively immersed in what it faces.
    - Video games, like multitasking, produce a broad but flat attention similar to animal vigilance; bullying has meanwhile achieved pandemic dimensions.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-010"></span>**Deep contemplative attention is being displaced by hyperattention, a scattered awareness with low tolerance for the profound boredom that Benjamin calls the 'dream bird that hatches the egg of experience'; without tranquillity the 'gift of listening' and the community of listeners disappear.**
    - "dream bird that hatches the egg of experience" —Walter Benjamin
    - A person with sufficient tolerance for boredom while walking is impelled toward an entirely new form of movement—dancing—which, unlike mere accelerated walking (running), escapes the achievement-principle as a luxury.
    - A purely hectic rush produces nothing new; it only reproduces and accelerates what is already available.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-011"></span>**The vita contemplativa is not nostalgic passivity but a mode of externalization or de-interiorization in which composure joins the wandering hands of nature; only profound attention can access what is floating, fleeting, long, and slow, as exemplified by Cézanne's claim to see the fragrance of things.**
    - "The landscape thinks itself in me, and I am its consciousness." —Paul Cézanne
    - "one of the most necessary corrections to the character of mankind... is a considerable strengthening of the contemplative element in it." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Modern Cartesian doubt has taken the place of wonder at the So-Sein (way things are); without contemplative composure the gaze errs restlessly and finds expression for nothing.

## Vita Activa

Against Arendt's rehabilitation of the vita activa and her diagnosis of a passive animal laborans, Han argues that the late-modern animal laborans is hyperactive and hyperneurotic, driven not by species-absorption but by the radical fleetingness of a denarrativized world that reduces life to bare survival.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-012"></span>**Hannah Arendt rehabilitates the vita activa by orienting action on natality rather than Heideggerian death, conferring a quasi-religious, heroic dimension on the new beginning humans realize by being born, and laments that modernity degrades the human into a passive animal laborans submerged in the species life process.**
    - "The miracle... is the birth of new men and the new beginning, the action they are capable of by virtue of being born." —Hannah Arendt
    - Arendt imagines that from a remote point in the universe human motorization would look like a biological mutation—the body surrounding itself with a metal housing like a snail, or bacteria mutating into antibiotic-resistant strains.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-013"></span>**Arendt's diagnosis fails for today's achievement society because the late-modern animal laborans does not surrender its individuality to the species but is individualized, ego-inflated, hyperactive, and hyperneurotic; the real cause of hectic nervousness is the radical fleetingness of a denarrativized, faithless world that reduces life to bare working.**
    - The modern loss of faith concerns reality itself; with the collapse of religions as thanatotechnics, nothing promises duration or Bestand, so nervousness and unease arise and the cult of health rises to divine status after the death of God.
    - An animal absorbed into its species could attain brute Gelassenheit; the late-modern ego stands utterly alone and is anything but animalian.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-014"></span>**Late-modern achievement society has reduced everyone without exception to a bare life that is holy (sacer as 'holy,' not 'accursed') and cannot be killed—undead life to be preserved at any cost—so that everyone carries an internal work camp being simultaneously prisoner and guard, victim and perpetrator.**
    - Agamben's homo sacer is absolutely expendable (Jews in camps, Guantanamo prisoners, asylum-seekers, patients rotting in intensive care), whereas achievement society's bare life cannot be killed absolutely—it is undead.
    - Depression, bipolar disorder, and burnout sufferers develop the symptoms of the Muselmänner—emaciated, apathetic camp prisoners who could no longer recognize cold or orders—though the late-modern version would be well fed and probably obese.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-015"></span>**Arendt's book ends by invoking thinking via Cato's dictum, but this is a stopgap that inadvertently endorses the vita contemplativa: Cicero originally quoted the same passage to praise withdrawal into contemplative solitude, and the loss of the ability to contemplate is itself responsible for modern hysteria and nervousness.**
    - "Never is he more active than when he does nothing, never is he less alone than when he is by himself." —Cato (quoted by Arendt)
    - Cicero included the same Cato passage in De re publica immediately before praising the vita contemplativa and exhorting withdrawal from the forum and the rush of the crowd.

## The Pedagogy of Seeing

Reviving the vita contemplativa requires learning to see—cultivating the negative potency of not-to (saying no, making-pause) against a positivized world where absolutized activity collapses into hyperactive reaction and thinking degenerates into calculation.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-016"></span>**Following Nietzsche's Twilight of the Idols, learning to see means accustoming the eyes to calm and patience and cultivating the inhibiting, excluding instincts—the ability to resist a stimulus and set a no in opposition—which is the first preliminary schooling for spirituality (Geistigkeit).**
    - "every characteristic absence of spirituality, every piece of common vulgarity, is due to an inability to resist a stimulus" —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Reacting immediately and yielding to every impulse is already a symptom of exhaustion; the vita contemplativa offers resistance to intrusive stimuli and steers the gaze in sovereign fashion rather than surrendering it.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-017"></span>**Hyperactive intensification abruptly switches into hyperpassivity in which one obeys every impulse without resistance; genuine action requires the negativity of interruption and making-pause (Innehalten), whereas activity following an unthinking mechanical course—like the calculating but pause-incapable computer—is stupid.**
    - "The active roll as the stone rolls, in obedience to the stupidity of the laws of mechanics." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Acceleration abolishes all intervals and between-times; a real turn to the Other presupposes an interruption, and only making-pause lets the subject measure the sphere of contingency.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-018"></span>**General acceleration also causes us to unlearn rage, which unlike mere annoyance interrupts a given state and makes a new state begin; increasing positivization absorbs every state of exception and totalizes normality, contra Agamben's claim that the state of exception is expanding.**
    - Rage negates the whole and represents a state of exception (as dread relates to Being-as-such); annoyance or 'having a beef' relates to a determinate object and cannot effect decisive change—like fear relative to dread.
    - Rising attention to concepts like 'state of exception' and 'immunitas' proves not their actuality but their vanishing.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-019"></span>**There are two forms of potency—positive potency (the power to do) and negative potency (the power not-to, to say no)—and negative potency, distinct from mere impotence, is essential to contemplation; its absence transforms thinking into calculation and turns humans and society into autistic performance-machines.**
    - With only positive potency, the senses stand at the mercy of intrusive stimuli, doing becomes fatal hyperactivity, and thinking scatters among endless objects, making reflection (Nachdenken) impossible since positivity permits only anticipation.
    - Zen meditation seeks the pure negativity of not-to—the void—by freeing oneself from intrusive Something; paradoxically an extremely active process, whereas hyperactivity is an extremely passive form of doing.
    - The computer is a Positivmaschine free of all Otherness; because negativity is absent, an idiot savant can perform what otherwise only a calculator can do.

## The Bartleby Case

Against Agamben's ontotheological reading of Bartleby as pure potentiality and de-creation, Han reads Melville's tale pathologically: it depicts a disciplinary society whose obedience-subjects degrade into apathetic animal laborans, making it a story of exhaustion rather than messianic de-creation.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-020"></span>**Melville's 'Bartleby' admits a pathological reading of an inhumane disciplinary working world whose inhabitants are neurotic animal laborans, and Bartleby's 'I would prefer not to' expresses neither negative potency nor the instinct for delay but neurasthenic lack of drive and apathy that seal his doom.**
    - The office is choked by skyscrapers, faces a 'lofty brick wall, black by age and everlasting shade,' and is 'deficient in what landscape painters call life'; the assistant Turkey runs in flurried recklessness and Nippers grinds his teeth with psychosomatic digestive troubles.
    - 'Wall' is one of the most frequently used words—the 'dead wall,' 'dead brick wall,' and the thick-walled prison called the 'Tombs'—signifying disciplinary architecture and always associated with death.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-021"></span>**Bartleby remains an obedience-subject who does not develop the depressive symptoms—self-reproach, feelings of inadequacy, I-tiredness—of late-modern achievement society, because monotonous copying leaves no space for the imperative to be oneself and does not admit initiative.**
    - What makes Bartleby sick is not excessive positivity or possibility; he is not burdened by the imperative to let his self flourish and does not know the wearing-out of the ego that leads to depressive I-tiredness.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-022"></span>**Agamben's ontotheological interpretation—Bartleby as the extreme figure of Nothing, pure absolute potentiality, an angel of annunciation heralding de-creation—founders on the narrative's facts, since Bartleby's negative being-unto-death, his 'dim eyes,' and his former work in the Dead Letter Office contradict any messianic reading.**
    - "As a scribe who has stopped writing, Bartleby is the extreme figure of the Nothing from which all creation derives." —Giorgio Agamben
    - The tale's message—'On errands of life, these letters speed to death'—and the small 'imprisoned turf' in the Tombs only underscore the negativity of the realm of the dead, refuting Agamben's reading of sky and grass as messianic signs.
    - Kafka's 'Hunger Artist' harbors fewer illusions: his unremarked death refreshes onlookers and makes room for a panther embodying desire-free joy of living, whose insubstantial freedom parallels the hunger artist's freedom of refusal.

## The Society of Tiredness

Achievement society generates a divisive, worldless I-tiredness of exhaustion, but Handke's 'Essay on Tiredness' points to a redemptive fundamental or we-tiredness of negative potency that loosens the ego, opens a between, and founds a community of singularities requiring no kinship.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-023"></span>**Achievement society as an activeness society develops into a doping society where being alive is reduced to maximizable vital functions, generating excessive tiredness and exhaustion that are not immunological reactions but caused by a too-much of positivity leading to psychic infarctions.**
    - Neuro-enhancement lets one achieve without achieving; a surgeon on enhancers makes fewer mistakes, so simple prohibition cannot prevent the body and whole human from becoming a performance-machine—doping is merely a consequence of this development.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-024"></span>**Handke distinguishes divisive, solitary I-tiredness—which strikes one mute and blind, destroys language and community, and drives one to violence in the way of seeing that distorts the other—from eloquent, reconciliatory tiredness that opens a between.**
    - "Doomed to remain speechless, that sort of tiredness drove us to violence." —Peter Handke
    - Divisive tiredness recoils each into private tiredness ('mine over here and yours over there'); the uttered word 'tired' shouted together might have set them free from their individual hells.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-025"></span>**Fundamental or we-tiredness is a tiredness of negative potency (not-to) that inspires: as 'more of less of me' it loosens the ego, shifts gravity from self to world, restores duality and the capacity to be touched, and grants access to long and slow forms invisible to fast hyperattention.**
    - "The inspiration of tiredness tells them not so much what they should, as what they need not, be." —Peter Handke
    - Deep tiredness loosens identity so things grow porous and less determinate, lending them an aura of friendliness; it is 'tiredness that trusts in the world' (weltvertrauende Müdigkeit), whereas I-tiredness is worldless and world-destroying.
    - Handke sees this community of singularities prefigured in seventeenth-century Dutch floral still lifes, where a beetle, snail, bee, and butterfly sit true to life, all together in one moment though none suspects the others.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-026"></span>**Handke conceives an immanent religion of tiredness—a 'Pentecostal company' tired to a man—modeled on the Sabbath as a day of not-to, an interval of peace and play (the use of the useless) that disarms violence and founds a society of tiredness as the possible society to come.**
    - "A certain tired man can be seen as a new Orpheus; the wildest beasts gather around him and are at last able to join in his tiredness." —Peter Handke
    - The Sabbath, whose word originally meant 'stopping' (aufhören), is the day God declared holy—the day of not-to free of all in-order-to and care, differing from Heidegger's definition of time as care and work.

## Burnout Society

The late-modern achievement-subject possesses an entirely different psyche than Freud's disciplinary obedience-subject: poor in negation and repression, it succumbs to a crisis of gratification and narcissistic self-reference that culminates in burnout, becoming its own homo sacer through voluntary self-exploitation under the injunction to achieve.

* <span id="the-burnout-society-027"></span>**Kafka's 'Prometheus' can be revised into an intrapsychic scene of the achievement-subject waging war on itself, where the relation between Prometheus and the liver-devouring eagle represents self-exploitation, and Prometheus's fatigue points toward a healing tiredness in which the wound closes wearily.**
    - "The gods grew weary, the eagles grew weary, the wound closed wearily." —Franz Kafka
    - Healthy 'tiredness that trusts in the world' (the ego abandoning itself into the world) opposes worldless I-tiredness, which annihilates reference to the Other in favor of narcissistic self-reference.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-028"></span>**The late-modern achievement-subject possesses an entirely different psyche than the Freudian and Kantian obedience-subject: poor in negation, repression, and fear of transgression, it is a subject of affirmation governed by Can rather than Should, so Freudian psychoanalysis—correlative to repressive disciplinary society—cannot approach depression, burnout, or ADHD.**
    - Freud's psychic apparatus is repressive, setting up walls, thresholds, and guards; the Kantian moral subject is internally split as defendant and judge, with God as instance of both punishment and gratification, distributing happiness in exact proportion to morality.
    - The achievement-subject does not pursue works of duty but freedom, pleasure, and inclination, working for enjoyment as a self-starting entrepreneur (Unternehmer seiner selbst) who hearkens to itself, ridding itself of the commanding Other.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-029"></span>**The absence of relation to the Other causes a crisis of gratification, since recognition presupposes a Third Party and one cannot reward or acknowledge oneself; compounded by contemporary production that makes conclusive work (Werk) impossible, this drives the subject to perform more and more.**
    - "Self-absorption does not produce gratification, it produces injury to the self... one drowns in the self." —Richard Sennett
    - Sennett wrongly claims the narcissist intentionally avoids closure to heighten the self; in fact the socially conditioned impossibility of definitive forms of closure drives narcissistic self-repetition, so the feeling of having achieved a goal simply never occurs—openness favors economic growth.
* <span class="pull-quote">The project turns out to be a projectile that the achievement-subject is aiming at itself.</span>
* <span id="the-burnout-society-030"></span>**Whereas hysteria and melancholy are phenomena of negativity (repression, loss) that admit a characteristic morphe and libidinal attachment, depression is amorphous, objectless, cut off from all attachment, and characteristic of a flexible late-modern ego without character—making the depressive a 'man without character.'**
    - Freud defines character as 'a precipitate of abandoned object-cathexes' containing the history of repression; the depressive is formless and amorphous, and the shapeless flexible human creates high economic efficiency.
    - Mourning has strong libidinal attachment to a lost object and melancholy stands in negative relation to the absent thing; depression is objectless, undirected, and lacks all gravity, since the late-modern ego devotes most libido to itself and scatters the rest among fleeting contacts.
    - Ehrenberg still clings to the conflict model, claiming depression conceals a hidden conflict, but depression eludes psychoanalysis entirely because it lacks the negativity of repression—Ehrenberg tries to rescue psychoanalysis even though its conditions have disappeared.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-031"></span>**In the transition to achievement society the repressive superego positivizes into the seductive ego ideal: the achievement-subject projects itself onto an unattainable ego ideal, and the gap between real ego and ego ideal produces auto-aggression, turning the project into a projectile aimed at the self through voluntary self-exploitation.**
    - The obedience-subject subjects itself to the superego (negative compulsion, the dictatorial 'Thou shalt'); the achievement-subject projects itself onto the ego ideal (positive compulsion interpreted as freedom).
    - Auto-exploitation is more efficient and more profitable (leistungsstärker) than allo-exploitation because a deceptive feeling of freedom attends it; exploitation now occurs without domination, and self-generated violence is more fatal because the victim considers itself free.
* <span id="the-burnout-society-032"></span>**The achievement-subject that fashions itself as homo liber, its own sovereign and entrepreneur, actually becomes homo sacer of its own self under the injunction to achieve (Bann der Leistung); Agamben's negativity-based theory of homo sacer, fixed on exclusion and a state of exception, cannot grasp the violence of positivity that operates through inclusion, exhaustion, and the violence of consensus.**
    - "only bare life is authentically political" —Giorgio Agamben
    - Agamben's homo sacer inhabits a state of exception and is absolutely expendable; achievement society's homines sacri inhabit a totalized state of normality, cannot be killed at all, and live as the undead—too alive to die, too dead to live.
    - The capitalist economy absolutizes survival rather than the good life, reducing life to bare vital functions so that health becomes the new goddess and, stripped of transcendent value, purposiveness without purpose—which is why bare life is holy.
    - "All of you who are in love with hectic work and whatever is fast, new, strange—you find it hard to bear yourselves, your diligence is escape and the will to forget yourself." —Friedrich Nietzsche (Thus Spoke Zarathustra)
