## The Smooth

The smooth surface is the signature aesthetic of the present because it embodies a society of positivity that eliminates all negativity, resistance, and otherness, reducing beauty to a consumable 'Like' devoid of meaning or the power to shock.

* <span id="saving-beauty-001"></span>**The smooth object embodies today's society of positivity because it removes all negativity: it does not injure, offers no resistance, deletes its 'Against,' and merely seeks the 'Like,' connecting phenomena as disparate as Jeff Koons's sculptures, iPhones, and Brazilian waxing.**
    - The LG G Flex smartphone has a self-healing skin that erases any scratch (trace of injury) and is flexible and adaptable, making it 'invulnerable' and perpetually smooth
    - 'Sharing' and 'Like' are communicative means of smoothening that eliminate negativities because they are obstacles to accelerated communication
* <span id="saving-beauty-002"></span>**Jeff Koons's art perfects the aesthetics of the smooth by eliminating all disaster, injury, rupture and seam, demanding no interpretation or reflection but only a disburdened 'Wow'; unlike Warhol's Death and Disaster series, which still carried the negativity of death, Koons's mirror-smooth sculptures merely reflect and confirm the observer.**
    - "an observer of his works should only emit a simple 'Wow'" —Jeff Koons
    - "The Balloon Dog... wants to confirm the observer in their existence... you are reflected in it and assured of yourself" —Jeff Koons
    - Koons's smooth sculptures cause a 'haptic compulsion' to touch, abolishing the contemplative distance that aesthetic judgement presupposes
* <span id="saving-beauty-003"></span>**Koons's art performs a sacralization of the smooth, a religion of consumption in which salvation is promised without pain, injury or guilt; the Balloon Venus gives birth not to a homo doloris crowned with thorns but to a bottle of Dom Pérignon.**
    - The Balloon Venus, shaped in birth position as Koons's 'Holy Mary,' contains a bottle of Dom Pérignon Rosé Vintage 2003 in her belly instead of a wounded saviour
* <span class="pull-quote">What is smooth does not injure. Nor does it offer any resistance. It is looking for Like.</span>
* <span id="saving-beauty-004"></span>**Against the smooth, Gadamer holds that negativity is essential to art as its 'wound' — the work's facticity offers an insurmountable resistance that shakes the observer and demands 'You must change your life,' whereas the smooth merely adapts to please and elicit a Like.**
    - "There is no place that fails to see you. You must change your life." —Rilke (via Gadamer)
    - A push comes from the work of art, pushing the observer down; the smooth has the opposite intentional nature, wanting only to please and not to knock over
* <span id="saving-beauty-005"></span>**Both the beautiful and the ugly are smoothed out today: the ugly loses its diabolical, uncanny, terrifying negativity (the petrifying Medusa gaze, Bataille's transgressive excess) and becomes a consumable formula for entertainment, while beauty exhausts itself in a Like-it so that aestheticization becomes anaestheticization, sedating perception.**
    - "the greater the anguish... the stronger the realisation of exceeding the bounds and the greater the accompanying rush of joy" —Georges Bataille
    - The 'Dschungelcamp' (Jungle Camp) presents the disgusting stripped of any negativity that might trigger an existential crisis, smoothened into a consumable form
    - A corpse is scandalous for Rosenkranz because it retains form while being 'in itself formless' — the appearance of life in what is dead is infinitely repulsive
* <span id="saving-beauty-006"></span>**Dataism introduces a 'second Enlightenment' that smoothens free acts into transparent, computable operations, because information is a pornographic form of knowledge — transparent, without inwardness or flip sides — whereas knowledge is gained against resistance and stretches between past and future.**
    - Data deliver themselves to total visibility and have no inside or ambiguity, unlike language, which does not permit things to come into perfectly clear focus
    - Robert Pfaller: things that have 'unnoticed become impossible in our culture' are experienced as objects of disgust, as dirty
    - The positivity of smoothness accelerates the circulation of information, communication and capital by eliminating the resistance of the other

## The Smooth Body

The digital close-up and selfie reduce the expressive, world-containing face to a smooth pornographic façade without gaze, while dataism dissolves the body into quantifiable data and partial objects, disenchanting it of imagination and power.

* <span id="saving-beauty-007"></span>**The cinematic close-up renders the body pornographic by muting it and dissolving the world-containing, expressive face into a smooth 'Face' or façade without gaze; whereas for Benjamin the close-up was still a hermeneutic practice reading the language of the unconscious, today it produces only self-referential emptiness.**
    - "The close-up of a face is as obscene as a sexual organ looked at from a close distance. It is a sexual organ." —Jean Baudrillard
    - For Benjamin the close-up and slow motion disclosed 'a space informed by the unconscious,' bringing to light unknown structures — a linguistic, reading practice
* <span id="saving-beauty-008"></span>**The addictive taking of selfies expresses not narcissistic self-love but a 'negative narcissism' — an inner emptiness and anxiousness about oneself, since the contemporary ego lacks stable forms of expression and impermanence de-stabilizes it, producing a self that idles and vainly tries to produce itself.**
    - Because nothing endures, impermanence destabilizes the ego and makes it insecure; this anxiousness, not vanity, drives the compulsive self-reproduction of empty forms of the self
* <span id="saving-beauty-009"></span>**Dataism and the 'Quantified Self' movement dissolve the body into exchangeable digital data and partial objects resembling sexual organs, turning the transparent body into a control-and-surveillance screen rather than a narrative scene of imagination; the self-driving, shared car likewise disenchants the body, which is no longer a phallus of power but a mere interface in the network.**
    - The digital network net-works the body; in a self-driving car I am not an actor or demiurge but merely an interface, since the sharing principle cannot apply to the phallus, a symbol of possession and power
    - The Quantified Self movement, coordinated by Gary Wolf and Kevin Kelly's California-based Quantified Self Labs, equips the body with digital sensors to register all body-related data

## The Aesthetics of the Smooth

Modern aesthetics separates the beautiful from the sublime and isolates beauty in pure positivity, beginning with Burke's equation of beauty with the smooth and continuing in Kant, where both beauty and the sublime remain autoerotic feelings of the self rather than encounters with the wholly other.

* <span id="saving-beauty-010"></span>**In antiquity the beautiful and the sublime were not separated, so beauty contained the negativity of the overwhelming: Pseudo-Longinus called beautiful women 'tortures to the eyes' (painfully beautiful), and in Plato the sight of divine Beauty does not please but shocks the onlooker into ecstasy, awe, terror and madness.**
    - In Plato the adept 'all of a sudden' catches sight of the 'wonderfully beautiful' (thaumaston kalon) and 'divine Beauty' (theion kalon), seized by awe and terror (ekplettontai)
    - Plato's metaphysics of beauty shocks the subject, in sharp contrast to modern aesthetics of pleasure, which confirms the subject in its autonomy and complacency
* <span id="saving-beauty-011"></span>**For Edmund Burke beauty begins as the smooth: the beautiful is an optimized surface without negativity, resistance, or pain — smooth skin, smooth leaves and feathers, sweetness — since roughness, sharp angles and abrupt variation diminish beauty, while the sublime alone retains an inherent negativity of pain and terror that is reversed into a purifying 'delightful horror.'**
    - "take any beautiful object, and give it a broken and rugged surface... it pleases no longer" —Edmund Burke
    - In taste, sweetness corresponds to smoothness; the smooth and the sweet share the same origin as phenomena of pure positivity exhausting themselves in pleasure
    - Burke lets the sublime's terror reverse into positivity so it stands fully at the service of the subject, thereby losing its otherness
* <span id="saving-beauty-012"></span>**Kant makes beauty an autoerotic subject-feeling rather than an object-feeling: beauty pleases because it stimulates the free harmonious play of imagination and understanding, a prelude to the work of cognition, so that faced with beauty the subject ultimately likes itself and, as Adorno notes, enjoys 'the feeling of power.'**
    - When looking at beauty the cognitive faculties are in play mode, a Vorspiel to knowledge as work; beauty keeps the machinery of cognition moving without producing knowledge itself
    - "In it subjectivity, unconscious of itself, enjoys the feeling of power" —Theodor W. Adorno
* <span id="saving-beauty-013"></span>**In Kant the sublime, though it first produces displeasure by overwhelming the imagination, is also an autoerotic self-feeling: the subject retreats into the inwardness of reason and its idea of infinity, feeling superior to nature — a 'subreption' that projects reason's own sublimity onto the object, so neither beauty nor the sublime pulls the subject beside itself toward the wholly other.**
    - "the wide ocean, enraged by storms, cannot be called sublime. Its visage is horrible" —Immanuel Kant
    - Han argues beauty can be recovered only by returning to it a de-subjectivizing sublimity beyond autoerotic subjectivity, undoing the separation of beauty and the sublime rather than opposing them in postmodern fashion

## Digital Beauty

Against Kant's self-enclosed subject, Adorno's natural beauty tears the subject out of its autoerotic inwardness through pain and shudder toward the wholly other, whereas digital beauty is perfectly smooth, removes all negativity of the other, and forms an autoerotic 'smooth space of the same' in which humans encounter only themselves.

* <span id="saving-beauty-014"></span>**Adorno's aesthetic experience is not pleasure in which the subject recognizes itself but a shudder in which it perceives its own finitude, is moved to tears, and exits its imprisonment in itself; natural beauty is accessible only to blind, unconscious perception and to pain, refusing consumption, communication, and intentional humanization.**
    - "Shudder... is a memento of the liquidation of the I, which, shaken, perceives its own limitedness and finitude" —Theodor W. Adorno
    - "The 'How beautiful!' at the sight of a landscape insults its mute language and reduces its beauty; appearing nature wants silence" —Theodor W. Adorno
    - Pain is the tear that tears the subject out of its autoerotic inwardness and announces the wholly other
* <span id="saving-beauty-015"></span>**Digital beauty is the opposite of natural beauty: perfectly smooth, containing no tear, it forms a smooth space of the same permitting no alterity, replacing alterity with consumable diversity and difference, and — through total digitalization of being — produces an absolute subjectivity, a digital inwardness in which the world is coated over with the human retina and permits no wonder.**
    - Natural beauty's temporality is the 'already of the not-yet' with auratic distance that 'veils itself at the moment of greatest proximity'; digital beauty is the immediate present without future or history
    - Natural beauty is 'the trace of the non-identical in things under the spell of universal identity,' which digital beauty banishes
    - The closer the net is woven, the more the world shields itself against the other and produces permanent self-mirroring on a screen-and-control monitor

## The Aesthetics of Veiling

Beauty is essentially a hideout requiring opacity, semblance, and veiling, so that it cannot be unveiled without being destroyed; pornography and information, by contrast, are transparent, seek final unveiling and truth, and thereby abolish the erotic tension of appearance-as-disappearance.

* <span id="saving-beauty-016"></span>**Beauty is a hideout whose essence is concealment and semblance, so that unveiling disenchants and destroys it; pornography — nakedness without drape or secret, at home in the shop window — is beauty's opposite figure, while eroticism depends on concealing, delaying, and distracting from the object.**
    - "Nothing more homogeneous than a pornographic photograph... completely constituted by the presentation of only one thing: sex" —Roland Barthes
    - Mapplethorpe shifts close-ups of genitalia from pornographic to erotic by photographing the fabric of underwear at close range, so interest shifts to the texture of the material
    - Beauty takes place beside the object, in the marginal (Nebensächliche); the beautiful object as such does not exist
* <span id="saving-beauty-017"></span>**Following Benjamin's reading of Goethe's Faust, it is the dress or veil that is divine, so beauty cannot be undressed and demands a 'hermeneutics of veiling' whose task is not to lift the veil but to know it as veil and thereby view the beautiful as a secret — the veil being more essential than the veiled object.**
    - "It is no longer the goddess – you have lost her – But is divine." —Goethe (Faust)
    - Beauty conveys itself neither to direct empathy nor to naïve contemplation, both of which try to lift or look through the veil; unveiled, beauty becomes 'infinitely inconspicuous'
* <span id="saving-beauty-018"></span>**Sacred texts deliberately eroticize themselves through veiling: Augustine holds that God obscures Scripture with metaphors to make it an object of desire, and the Kabbalah's Torah reveals herself like a beautiful damsel who unveils her face to her secret lover only for a moment, turning reading into an erotic act of love.**
    - "the Torah resembles a beautiful and stately damsel, who is hidden in a secluded chamber... She opens the door... for a moment reveals her face to her lover, but hides it again forthwith" —Gershom Scholem (on the Kabbalah)
    - Information cannot be veiled: it is transparent by nature, repels metaphor, speaks 'straight out,' and is directed toward ultimate truth — it has a pornographic nature
* <span id="saving-beauty-019"></span>**For Barthes and Baudrillard the erotic is the 'staging of an appearance-as-disappearing' located at the gaping edge of the garment; it can do without truth, being semblance and veil, whereas the progressive unveiling of striptease, the catchy novel's final truth, and pornography — which lays bare even the soul — mark the death of the seductive 'pathos of distance.'**
    - "the most erotic portion of a body is where the garment gapes" —Roland Barthes
    - Seduction rests on intuiting something in the other that remains forever secret; the movement from seduction to love, desire, sexuality and porno moves ever closer to avowal, unveiling and the lesser secret

## The Aesthetics of Injury

True seeing, poetry, thinking, and beauty require the negativity of injury and vulnerability, which today's positivity society avoids; Barthes's punctum names the wounding, unnamable element of the erotic photograph that the smooth, transparent, pornographic image and the affect-driven digital medium eliminate.

* <span id="saving-beauty-020"></span>**Seeing in the emphatic sense means seeing differently — experiencing — which is impossible without exposing oneself to injury, so sensibility is vulnerability and injury is the moment of truth in seeing; today's positivity society reduces the negativity of harm, distributing libidinal energies across many objects like capital investments to avoid total loss.**
    - "I'm learning how to see... everything enters into me more deeply and no longer stops at the point where it used to come to an end" —Rilke (Malte Laurids Brigge)
    - "No poem without accident, no poem that does not open itself like a wound, but no poem that is not also just as wounding" —Jacques Derrida
    - Without pain and injury, thinking cannot be sparked; experience is 'the suffering in which the essential otherness of beings reveals itself in opposition to the tried and usual' (Heidegger)
* <span id="saving-beauty-021"></span>**Barthes distinguishes the studium — the coded field of 'I like / I don't like,' half-desire, and inconsequential taste — from the punctum, the element that rises from the scene, pierces the observer like an arrow, and injures; only photographs with a punctum are hideouts with a 'blind field,' which distinguishes the erotic from the transparent, smooth pornographic photograph that has no punctum.**
    - "this element which rises from the scene, shoots out of it like an arrow, and pierces me" —Roland Barthes
    - The punctum fundamentally lacks transparency and evades naming: 'What I can name cannot really prick me. The incapacity to name is a good symptom of disturbance'
    - News photographs are unary: they can 'shout' and traumatize but not wound, having no punctum
* <span id="saving-beauty-022"></span>**The punctum requires an ascetic, silent vision that presupposes closing the eyes and matures only belatedly in remembering, allowing a latency; the accelerated, greedy consumption of cinematic and digital images makes it impossible to close the eyes, so Barthes's pair studium/punctum must be supplemented by the affectum — the digital medium as a medium of affect that shouts and excites, producing only immediate liking.**
    - "We photograph things in order to drive them out of our minds. My stories are a way of shutting my eyes." —Kafka (via Barthes)
    - Perception of digital images occurs as contagion — immediate contact between image and eye — which lacks aesthetic distance and forbids closing the eyes; affects are quicker than feelings and accelerate communication

## The Aesthetics of Disaster

Kant's and Hegel's aesthetics immunize the subject against any wholly-other 'outside,' but the disaster (dis-astrum, the ill-starred) names the radical heterogeneity that breaks open inwardness; genuine beauty carries the negativity of terror, brokenness, and death, so that beauty is an illness and today's kalocracy of the healthy and smooth eliminates it.

* <span id="saving-beauty-023"></span>**Kant's starry heavens and moral law lie within the inwardness of reason and Hegel's spirit is a 'thousand-eyed Argus' illuminating all — both are incantations against the disaster, the outside, the wholly other; the disaster (des-astrum, ill-starred) is the radical heterogeneity that breaks open the inwardness of spirit.**
    - "Two things fill the mind with ever new and increasing admiration and reverence... the starry heavens above me and the moral law within me" —Immanuel Kant
    - For Blanchot the disaster means a 'withdrawal outside the sidereal abode' and disorients the absolute rather than being absolute
* <span id="saving-beauty-024"></span>**The aesthetics of disaster is an aesthetics of the event opposed to the aesthetics of pleasure, in which inconspicuous 'events of emptiness' — dust swirled by a raindrop, silent snowfall, a scent off summer rock — expropriate and de-subjectify the ego, taking away its inwardness and thereby making it happy; Blanchot's empty black sky fills the child with a devastating, ravaging joy.**
    - "the sky, the same sky, suddenly open, absolutely black and absolutely empty... the ravaging joy to which he can bear witness only in tears" —Maurice Blanchot
    - A disaster means the death of the autoerotic subject that clings to itself; such events are beautiful because they expropriate the ego
* <span id="saving-beauty-025"></span>**The negativity of terror and death is constitutive of beauty: Baudelaire rhymes astres/désastres and beau/flambeau/tombeau, Rilke's beauty is 'nothing but the beginning of terror,' and beauty is not an image but a shield that at once protects us from and lets us glimpse the terrible.**
    - "[Beauty] is nothing but the beginning of terror, which we still are just able to endure" —Rainer Maria Rilke
    - In Baudelaire's Hymne à la Beauté beauty is the torch (flambeau) the butterfly burns to death in, and beauty (beau) is inscribed in both flambeau and tombeau (grave)
* <span id="saving-beauty-026"></span>**For Adorno beauty is form that inscribes itself into the formless and terrible by positing differences, but the semblance of beauty remains fragile and powerless before the terrible; forming rationality depends on a mimesis that adjusts to the formless, so beauty's coherence consists in the fact that it does not cohere — its heart is a broken heart, and beauty is a curative sickness.**
    - "Exuberant health is always, as such, sickness also... Beauty is such a curative sickness. It arrests life, and therefore its decay." —Theodor W. Adorno
    - Without the negativity of brokenness (Gebrochenheit), beauty atrophies into the smooth; the healthy radiates something morbid and lifeless, smoothened into the undead

## The Ideal of Beauty

Kant's ideal of beauty exceeds the aesthetic by expressing moral ideas and resisting consumption, but today the beautiful character has given way to sexiness, which is bound to consumer capitalism; character — as the burnt-in, enduring, telluric sign — is replaced by the connective, characterless smoothness of the digital order.

* <span id="saving-beauty-027"></span>**Kant distinguishes the species-specific 'normal idea of beauty' — a perfectly regular, smooth, characterless face expressing only the species — from the 'ideal of beauty' unique to humans as the visible expression of moral ideas; the ideal contains reason's substance and thus resists consumption, since taste unites with reason, the beautiful with the good.**
    - Hölderlin's Hymne an die Schönheit invokes Kant: nature speaks figuratively in beautiful forms and we decipher its secret writing through our moral feeling
    - The normal idea of beauty 'cannot contain anything specifically characteristic'; the ideal of beauty is an 'intellectualized judgement of taste' resting on the unification of the beautiful with the good
* <span id="saving-beauty-028"></span>**Beauty of character and moral beauty have given way entirely to sexiness, which is detached from beauty and moral character and subsumes personality under itself; the sexualization of the body is not simple emancipation but coincides with its commercialization, since consumption and sexiness condition each other and the desire-based self is a product of consumer capitalism.**
    - "Sexual appeal as such did not represent a legitimate criterion for mate selection... character and psychological makeup are ultimately subsumed under sexiness" —Eva Illouz
    - Nineteenth-century middle-class women were viewed as attractive because of their beauty as a physical and spiritual attribute, less because of sexual appeal
* <span id="saving-beauty-029"></span>**Character — from Greek charassein, to engrave or brand — has the temporality of duration, solidity and immutability, which excludes consumption; consumer culture removes duration and requires an ideal consumer without character, while the digital order, like Schmitt's characterless sea on which no fixed lines can be engraved, dissolves the telluric nomos of ownership, clans, tribes and neighbourly relations and celebrates a man without character.**
    - "The sea has no character, in the original sense of the word, which comes from the Greek charassein, meaning to engrave, to scratch, to imprint" —Carl Schmitt
    - For Schmitt the one real enemy is 'our own shape in the form of a question'; the less character one has, the smoother and slicker, the more Friends one has
    - Schmitt's solid earth allows border stones, walls and fortresses and makes families, clans, tribes and forms of ownership publicly visible; the digital order substitutes ownership with access

## Beauty as Truth

On a non-subjectivist reading, Hegel's aesthetics grounds beauty in the concept as the brilliance of truth, freedom and reconciliation: the beautiful object is a self-sufficient end that releases both subject and object into freedom, and therefore beauty and truth alike are exclusive forms that exclude consumption, contrary to today's kalocracy.

* <span id="saving-beauty-030"></span>**Hegel's 'concept' idealizes beauty and confers the brilliance of truth by gathering dispersed individualities into living, organic wholes; contrary to the postmodern critique, the Hegelian whole is not a structure of domination but a figure of mediation and reconciliation — 'the One which contains bound within itself the parts in their freedom' — so that truth is reconciliation and freedom.**
    - "The whole is... the One which contains bound within itself the parts in their freedom" —G.W.F. Hegel
    - The concept does not apply to an 'aggregate' (Haufen); no heap is beautiful, because beauty tends toward the One while the baroque heap lacks the concept
    - Han insists the whole opens the parts' space for freedom rather than subjugating them; philosophy's task is to set self-contradictory characteristics 'in the harmony and unity which is truth'
* <span id="saving-beauty-031"></span>**The beautiful object mediates between the theoretical and practical realms — where the subject is unfree because of things' independence or its own imposed desire — by being a pure end in itself that the subject neither instrumentalizes nor coerces; the subject's desire steps back into 'letting be' (Seinlassen) and 'releasement' (Gelassenheit), and in contemplating the beautiful, subject and object are reconciled and united.**
    - "the contemplation of beauty is of a liberal kind; it leaves objects alone as being inherently free and infinite" —G.W.F. Hegel
    - Desire converts its object to use by destroying it and scientific intelligence changes it into universal concept; art alone lets the object persist freely in its individual existence
* <span id="saving-beauty-032"></span>**Because neither truth nor the concept can be consumed, Hegelian beauty withdraws from all consumption: beauty rests in itself, does not promote itself, and invites lingering contemplation rather than possession, so art does not agree with capitalism; truth, like beauty, is an exclusive form opposed to the formless 'aggregate,' and thus the 'end of theory' announced by Chris Anderson via Big Data would mean the end of truth, narration and spirit.**
    - Data is purely additive and inhabits horizontal space; addition is opposed to narration, and a verticality is inherent to truth, which reduces entropy and the level of noise
    - Today's kalocracy — Botox, bulimia, cosmetic surgery — produces compulsions and terror, subjecting even the once-inalienable art to the logic of capital and the freedom of art to the freedom of capital

## The Politics of Beauty

For antiquity the beautiful (to kalon) names the free realm beyond necessity and utility, so that genuine political action is a politics of beauty and justice; today such a politics is impossible because politics merely works under systemic pressures without alternatives, and the experience of beauty has become narcissistic and consumerist rather than laterally just.

* <span id="saving-beauty-033"></span>**Beauty is a phenomenon of luxury and free play liberated from need and compulsion: Kant's 'wit' blooms like nature's flowers as intellectual luxury, and Aristotle's free man lives the three forms of life concerned with the beautiful — 'things neither necessary nor merely useful' — so the necessary that merely averts need is not beautiful.**
    - Han glosses luxury as that which 'luxates,' that is deviates; the merchant's gain-seeking life and household administration are unfree and thus not beautiful
    - Political action for Aristotle consists in creating beautiful deeds and entirely new beginnings beyond necessity and utility
* <span id="saving-beauty-034"></span>**In Plato and Aristotle the beautiful far exceeds aesthetic sensibility and grounds ethics: Aristotle's kalokagathia, the 'beautiful-good,' subordinates the good to the beautiful, and the ideal politics is a politics of beauty and freedom; today this is impossible because politics merely 'works' under the 'absence of alternatives' rather than acting, degenerating toward dictatorship, and a systemic stooge is a servant rather than a free man.**
    - Aristotle's Eudemian Ethics introduces kalokagathia, the 'beautifulgood,' in which the good is realized in the brilliance of the beautiful
    - Genuine politics would need to offer real alternatives, a real choice (Wahl); otherwise it degenerates into dictatorship
* <span id="saving-beauty-035"></span>**Beauty and justice derive from the same idea — the English 'fair' means both just and beautiful — and Scarry argues that beauty's symmetry and its retraction of the self (Weil's giving up 'our imaginary position at the center') translate aesthetic into ethical fairness by making the subject lateral rather than central; but today the experience of beauty is narcissistically central and consumerist, destroying the otherness of the other, so beauty becomes pornographic and anaesthetic, losing all transcendence that would link it to the ethical.**
    - "Beauty, according to Weil, requires us 'to give up our imaginary position at the center'... We willingly cede our ground to the thing that stands before us" —Elaine Scarry
    - Contrary to Scarry's expectation, today's experience of beauty is dominated by narcissistic centralness, not lateralness; sexiness allows for no lateralness and is incompatible with fairness

## Pornographic Theatre

Contemporary theatre has become pornographic rather than erotic, replacing indirect allusion, scenic distance, dialogue and objective play with the direct disclosure of affects and a nudism of the soul, because the narcissistic subject has lost the capacity for the other.

* <span id="saving-beauty-036"></span>**Botho Strauß's withdrawal from theatre marks the triumph of pornographic over erotic authorship: the eroticist is indirect, allusive, and loves scenic distance, while pornography is 'straight on,' getting down to business through deixis and progressive disclosure toward truth or transparency, whereas erotic secrets in principle cannot be disclosed.**
    - "I wanted to be an erotic author for the stage, but today pornographic authors... dominate the theatres" —Botho Strauß
    - Delays, slowing down and distraction are the temporal modalities of the erotic; pornographic disclosure moves progressively down to truth or transparency
* <span id="saving-beauty-037"></span>**Pornographic theatre lacks dialogue and thus loads a mass of affects directly onto the stage, because the narcissistic subject cannot open to or listen to the other; feelings are narrative and durational and alone give access to the other (hence compassion, Mitgefühl, exists while com-affects do not), whereas emotions and affects are impulsive, momentary, monological, and inscribe a negation of the other.**
    - "This fast-changing pitch of the voice... expresses a strong dialogic commitment, the wish to learn something of the other at all cost" —Botho Strauß
    - Against the pornographic nudism of the soul, Strauß calls for a 'nemological self-transparency' in which the de-psychologized, de-subjectified actress becomes a Nobody (nemo) without a soul to expose, and one exceeds oneself toward the other

## Lingering on Beauty

Beauty invites contemplative lingering in which willing and interest retreat and time comes to a standstill, saving the other from mere givenness; this festive, cultic 'high-time' that suspends passing has today been replaced by exhibition, spectacle, and the speculative logic of the stock exchange.

* <span id="saving-beauty-038"></span>**Beauty invites contemplative lingering because in its presence the willing that makes time pass retreats: for Schopenhauer aesthetic pleasure raises us above all willing so we are 'rid of ourselves,' bringing time to a standstill and stillness; this lingering aims at the other, so the task of art is the saving of the other by resisting its identification with mere givenness (Vorhandenheit).**
    - "we are raised for the moment above all willing, above all desires and cares; we are, so to speak, rid of ourselves" —Arthur Schopenhauer
    - Theunissen's 'presence-eternity' is the presence of the other; Spinoza: 'The mind is eternal to the extent that it grasps the things from the perspective of eternity'
* <span id="saving-beauty-039"></span>**Original art is the art of the festival: for Nietzsche works of art were monuments of the heydays (Hoch-Zeit) in which ordinary work-time suspends itself, and Gadamer notes that we 'enact' (begehen) a festival that is 'always there from the beginning' and does not dissolve into separate moments — the enacting suspends the passing (Vergehen) and lets us tarry, relating finite beings to eternity.**
    - "What do all our art of artworks matter if we lose that higher art, the art of festivals!" —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Festivals have religious origins: Latin feriae is time dedicated to cult, fanum a holy place; the festival begins where pro-fane ordinary time ends and presupposes a consecration
    - Today the high-time has disappeared into total work-time; even breaks are integrated into working hours as mere re-creation for the working process
* <span id="saving-beauty-040"></span>**Works of art have lost their cult value — increased by hiddenness and independent of attention — first to exhibition value in museums (spectacles, the museum as Golgotha/Schädelstätte) and now to their supreme value as objects of speculation traded on the stock exchange, so that total revenue (absoluter Erlös) takes the place of salvation (Erlösung).**
    - The totalization of attention destroys the cultic, since cult objects often remain hidden and a cult has nothing to do with attention
    - The stock exchange is today's place of worship; art's value as object of speculation subjects it to the logic of capital

## Beauty as Reminiscence

Beauty is essentially reminiscence and recollection rather than immediate brilliance, a slow, narrative, relational event of secret correspondences across time that evades the splintered temporality of consumption and information.

* <span id="saving-beauty-041"></span>**For Benjamin, recollection is the essence of human existence and of beauty, which remains inessential (wesenlos) without recollection; following Plato's Phaedrus, the experience of beauty is a re-cognition, a repetition of the past in which one recalls the form of beauty once beheld, so beauty's essence is the afterglow of a recollection rather than immediate present brilliance.**
    - "At this sight, his memory returns to that form of beauty, and he sees her once again enthroned by the side of temperance" —Plato (via Benjamin)
    - Consumption consumes only the new, not the past; recollection and duration are incompatible with consumption, which lives off splintered time
* <span id="saving-beauty-042"></span>**Proust's madeleine dipped in lime-blossom tea exemplifies beauty as the experience of duration: a tiny drop expands into a vast structure of recollection, a 'fragment of time in the pure state' that blends past and present and liberates from mortality, opposing the 'simple cinematographic vision' that disintegrates into point-like presences.**
    - "An exquisite pleasure had invaded my senses... I had ceased now to feel mediocre, contingent, mortal" —Marcel Proust
    - Beauty occurs where things turn toward each other and enter relations; truth is attained only by uniting two sensations' common essence within a metaphor, so beauty, like truth, is a narrative event
* <span id="saving-beauty-043"></span>**Beauty is a hesitating latecomer whose slow afterglow, not present brilliance, is beautiful, appearing only belatedly via detours as reminiscence and re-cognition; the 'Internet of Things' and communication as exchange of information only count without recounting, so addition and informational connection push aside the narrative metaphorical relations in which beauty consists.**
    - "The most noble kind of beauty is that which... infiltrates slowly, which we carry along with us almost unnoticed, and meet up with again in dreams" —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - The writer's task is to metaphorize and poeticize the world, discovering the hidden liaisons between things; long-lasting and slow is the pace of beauty

## Giving Birth in Beauty

In Plato's Symposium and Heidegger's ontology, beauty is generative — an Eros-driven 'giving birth in beauty' and the poetic name for the advent of the truth of being — that far exceeds aesthetic pleasure and commits us; today beauty is stripped of this consecration and Eros, reduced to a consumable object of the Like.

* <span id="saving-beauty-044"></span>**Plato's theory of beauty is active and generative rather than passive and consumptive: faced with beauty Eros awakens a procreative capacity in the soul, 'giving birth in beauty' (tokos en kalo), begetting immortal works — poetic, philosophical, and political — so that beautiful laws are the work of Eros and a politics guided by Eros is a politics of beauty.**
    - Plato praises the works of Homer and Hesiod and rulers such as Solon and Lycurgus as immortal children begotten through beauty; Socrates is initiated into the mysteries of Eros by Diotima
* <span id="saving-beauty-045"></span>**For Heidegger the Platonist, beauty is ontological not aesthetic — the 'poetic name for being' — and Eros gives wings to thought and consecrates thinking, saving it from 'pure work' that desecrates and disenchants it; beauty is the shining through of truth as the truth of being, generative and creative, and a time in which pleasure and the Like predominate is a time without Eros and without beauty.**
    - "I call it Eros... The beat of that god's wings moves me each time I take a substantial step in my thinking and venture onto untrodden paths" —Martin Heidegger
    - Beauty appears when truth sets itself into the work; the advent of truth lets beings appear differently and brings forth 'another Is,' so beauty as the shining-through of truth is inconspicuous because it hides behind the phenomena
* <span id="saving-beauty-046"></span>**The beautiful is 'that which commits us' (das Verbindliche) and founds duration, which is why Plato's beauty 'always is and neither comes to be nor passes away'; Eros as the striving for what is binding demands Badiou's unconditional 'fidelity,' the transcendence and metaphysics that increasing aestheticization and volatility destroy by reducing beauty to arbitrary, comfortable objects of fleeting pleasure — so the crisis of beauty is a crisis of commitment.**
    - "I'm going to extract something that will endure, something that will persist, a commitment, a fidelity" —Alain Badiou
    - Radical contingency and volatility (extending beyond financial markets to society as a whole) awaken a longing for something binding beyond the ordinary; the saving of beauty is the saving of that which commits us
