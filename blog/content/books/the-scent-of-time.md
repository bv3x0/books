## Non-Time

Han argues that the real problem of contemporary time is not acceleration but the loss of any capacity for meaningful conclusion or ending, so that life dissipates into an atomized sequence of point-like presents in which one perishes in 'non-time' rather than dying at the right time.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-001"></span>**The inability to reach a meaningful end or conclusion, not acceleration, is the root cause of contemporary temporal disorder; time 'tumbles on' like an avalanche precisely because it no longer contains anything to hold on to within itself.**
    - Nietzsche's 'ultimate man' extends life through a rigorous politics of health yet, unable to die at the right time, ends in non-time via drugs rather than achieving a consummating death.
    - "Die at the right time: thus Zarathustra teaches." —Nietzsche's Zarathustra
    - Temporal gravitation frames the present between past and future, creating the tension that makes 'the right time' possible; in atomized time all temporal points are alike and death becomes a mere perishing.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-002"></span>**The death of God punctuates time by depriving it of any theological, teleological, or historical tension, so that the present shrinks to a fleeting point devoid of heirs and goals; Nietzsche's 'eternal return' and Heidegger's invocation of 'heritage' and 'tradition' are attempts to restore temporal gravitation after this loss.**
    - God functioned as a stabilizer of time, ensuring a lasting, perennial present; his death removes the hold that resisted change.
    - Heidegger's Being and Time attempts to restore history as an empty form, asserting temporal formative force devoid of content, against the approaching end of history.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-003"></span>**Experience and knowledge are time-intensive, encompassing a vast temporal space linking past and future, whereas information is empty of time and can be stored and retrieved arbitrarily; where time decays into point-like presences it loses dialectical tension and the world becomes non-timely.**
    - Experience (Erfahrung) encompasses a vast temporal space and is time-intensive, as opposed to lived experience (Erlebnis), which is point-like and time-poor.
    - The storage of information is preceded by the deletion of memory, the deletion of historical time; deprived of memory, things become information or commodities in a timefree, ahistorical space.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-004"></span>**Empty duration is the negative twin of accelerated doing, sharing a common origin, so that the haste of day rules over the night as sleeplessness; against 'torn time' Proust's opening ('Longtemps, je me suis couché de bonne heure') stages an effortless continuity between sleeping, dreaming and waking that restores the 'good time' (bonne heure/bonheur).**
    - "But truly terrifying are the sleepless nights when time seems to contract and run fruitlessly through our hands." —Theodor Adorno
    - Proust's sleeper is player, wanderer and master of time, drawn up from the abyss of not-being by memory 'like a rope let down from heaven.'
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-005"></span>**The acceleration thesis mistakenly confuses a fulfilled life with mere plenitude, assuming that living faster multiplies life possibilities and thus dispels the fear of death; in reality a fulfilled life requires narrative synthesis, so that a very short life can be fulfilled while a rapid, atomized life is merely a hectic rush that never reaches completion.**
    - Han rejects the argument (attributed to Hartmut Rosa) that acceleration is an intuitive solution to a limited lifetime by realizing more worldly possibilities.
    - A long list of events does not produce narrative tension, while a very short story may possess powerful narrative tension; fulfilment cannot be explained on quantitative grounds.
    - Radical individualization and atomization strip life of the breadth that gives it duration, intensifying its mortality and producing restlessness and 'aging without growing old.'

## Time without a Scent

Han traces a historical succession from mythical time (a restful picture of eternal recurrence) through historical/linear time (a directed line toward a goal) to the present's atomized point-time, arguing that acceleration is a secondary symptom of time losing its narrative anchoring and becoming 'information,' which has no scent.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-006"></span>**Mythical time is a restful picture of an eternal, unchanging cosmic order in which time is justice (diké) that sets things right, while historical time is a directed line whose meaningfulness derives from difference, change and orientation toward a future goal rather than the recurrence of the same.**
    - Mythical events form a static plane reflecting eternal substance, so acceleration would make no sense; historical events form a progressive line whose directedness releases meaning.
    - In the mythical world, full of gods means full of meaning: narration makes world out of nothing, and the world becomes readable like a picture.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-007"></span>**The Enlightenment de-factualized and de-naturalized time, replacing thrownness with feasibility and making the free human being rather than a judging God the subject and 'master' of time; this regime change de-stabilizes time because God, as the source of a lasting present, had provided a resistance to change.**
    - The concept of 'revolution' shifted from its original astronomical sense (revolutio as stellar orbits and cyclical return) to a linear, feasible, human-made process.
    - "The progress of human reason prepared this great revolution, and you especially are charged with the duty of accelerating it." —Robespierre (1793)
    - The 1838 Brockhaus encyclopedia transfigures the railway into a 'Dampftriumphwagen' (steam-powered triumph engine) of the revolution.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-008"></span>**History now gives way to information, which possesses no narrative width, center, or direction and therefore 'has no scent'; between point-like moments yawn empty intervals of boredom and threat, driving a compulsion to accelerate sensations to hysterical levels.**
    - Against Baudrillard, information is not an ever-more-perfect simulation of an original but a new paradigm with its own atomized point-time temporality.
    - Empty intervals cause boredom (Langeweile), so sensations are made to follow each other faster to keep the intervals from 'lasting long' (lange weilen).
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-009"></span>**Acceleration is not a primary process but a symptom: time rushes off (stürzt fort) because it has lost its hold and been atomized, and this haste fails to produce any hold, only making the underlying lack of being more pungent.**
    - When time detaches from the anchoring that holds and inhibits it, it becomes devoid of support (haltlos) and, taken out of its mount, rushes off.

## The Speed of History

Han critiques Baudrillard's and Rosa's causal explanations of the end of history through speed, arguing instead that the disappearing 'gravitation of meaning' produces both acceleration and standstill as two sides of a deeper de-temporalization, which manifests in modern literature as simultaneous narrative haste and inertia.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-010"></span>**Baudrillard's image of acceleration hurling things to 'escape velocity' out of the referential sphere is misleading; the loss of meaning results not from an escape velocity but from the absence or weakness of the 'gravitation of meaning' that keeps things in the orbits that integrate them into contexts of meaning.**
    - "Every political, historical and cultural fact possesses a kinetic energy which wrenches it from its own space and propels it into a hyperspace where it loses all meaning." —Jean Baudrillard
    - It is misleading to talk of 'atoms of meaning' because meaning is not atomic; only meaningless violence can emanate from atoms.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-011"></span>**Acceleration and deceleration/standstill are both consequences of a deeper de-temporalization, not a 'dialectical inversion' of acceleration into rigidity as Rosa's 'frenetic standstill' (rasender Stillstand) thesis holds; standstill arises from the no-longer-knowing-where-to-go of directionless movements, not from everyone rushing at once.**
    - Han rejects Rosa's claim that deceleration is 'an internal element and inherent complementary principle of the acceleration process itself.'
    - The disappearance of selective narrative orbits produces a massification of events pushing into the present, which has a decelerating, blocking effect.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-012"></span>**Modern literature exhibits narrative de-temporalization as both acceleration and near-standstill; the narrator's inability to distinguish important from unimportant events destroys narrative rhythm, so that the story lists rather than narrates and can only be broken off abruptly in non-time.**
    - Michel Butor's L'emploi du temps (Passing Time) exemplifies this crisis: the narration drags from event to event, reaches no destination, and is broken off by the narrator's departure from Bleston.
    - Temporal dissipation (Zerstreuung) prevents any gathering of events into a closed wholeness; the temporal crisis becomes an identity crisis because the narrator cannot collect events around himself.

## From the Age of Marching to the Age of Whizzing

Han argues that genuine acceleration is a specifically modern phenomenon presupposing a linear, teleological process (the age of marching), while postmodern de-narrativization produces not acceleration but directionless 'whizzing,' so that the feeling of a speeding life is really an absence of the experience of duration.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-013"></span>**Bauman's figure of the modern pilgrim misdescribes modernity: the peregrinus is thrown into facticity and journeys toward a There, whereas the modern human is a free, goal-oriented marcher who creates his own path toward a better Here, closer to a soldier or labourer than a pilgrim.**
    - Modernity is a time of de-factualization and freedom, with production replacing repetition and the human being elevated to subject of history.
    - For the peregrinus there is no progress associated with a Here; modernity abolishes the difference between Here and There.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-014"></span>**Modernity remains a narrative age whose story of progress and freedom bestows meaning on time, so that acceleration toward an expected inner-worldly salvation makes sense and is desirable; technological progress inherits the teleological character of eschatological salvation history.**
    - The Brockhaus railway entry hails the 'steam-powered triumph engine' rolling toward a 'truly divine goal,' reaching it 'centuries earlier' than the slow course of history would.
    - Inner-worldly hope for happiness and freedom takes the place of religious expectations of divine salvation.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-015"></span>**Freedom is fundamentally relational, not the absence of ties: the Indo-European root fri (from which 'free,' 'peace,' and 'friend' derive) means 'to love,' so that inclusion and embeddedness, not dis-embeddedness or 'zapping,' set us free; the lack of hold, not intensified acceleration, throws postmodern life off balance.**
    - "The ultimate freedom is screen-directed, lived in the company of surfaces, and called zapping." —Zygmunt Bauman
    - Han rejects Rosa's thesis that postmodern de-narrativization and atomization are direct consequences of intensified modern acceleration reaching speed limits.
    - De-narrativization yields undirected 'whizzing' indifferent to acceleration; genuine acceleration requires a directed process.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-016"></span>**The impression that life is accelerating is really an absence of the experience of duration; whoever tries to live faster will also die faster, since fulfilment depends on duration and not on the quantitative 'rate of experience.'**
    - Han rejects the 'cultural promise of acceleration' and the 'fear of missing out' as the source of rushed feeling; a quickly lived life is itself a short life no matter how high the rate of experience.
    - When a continuous, narratively determined process is accelerated, the acceleration is absorbed by the narrative meaning and not perceived as stressful.

## The Paradox of the Present

Han argues that the elimination of intervals, thresholds, and transitions—through technology's compulsion toward total presence and instant availability—strips space and time of meaning, producing a directionless, discontinuous 'now-time' in which nothing can be completed and lingering becomes impossible.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-017"></span>**Intervals and thresholds constitute the topology of passion and simultaneously order and structure life, giving it direction and meaning; their elimination in favor of total presence and instantaneity produces a directionless space in which completion becomes senseless because one might 'miss the connection.'**
    - Acceleration is the attempt to make the temporal interval needed for bridging spatial distance disappear, leading to a semantic impoverishment of the world where space and time no longer mean much.
    - The pilgrim's path is itself a meaningful goal (penance, healing, prayer), whereas for the tourist everywhere is Here and Now and transition is an alien notion.
    - Electronic mail produces instantaneity by destroying paths as spatial intervals, dispensing with space itself.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-018"></span>**The totalization of the Here removes the There and abolishes the thresholds separating visible from invisible and familiar from alien, so the human being ceases to be a 'threshold creature'; being is thereby impoverished to being-present, leaving only two conditions—nothing and the present.**
    - Thresholds cause suffering and passion but they also delight; celebrations such as advent time give form to the meaningful in-between.
    - The nearness of the Here destroys the aura of distance; auratic distance holds appearance in reserve.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-019"></span>**The internet is an ahistorical space without direction, woven of interchangeable links whose 'now-time' has no duration and permits no lingering; the end of narrative linearity nonetheless opens new non-causal forms of perception where aesthetic tension comes from the superimposition and compression of events rather than narrative development.**
    - The form of movement is not walking or marching but surfing or browsing, unlinked to any direction or fixed path; no decision is final and everything is kept in limbo (in der Schwebe).
    - Digital products age quickly because the compulsion toward the new shortens product life cycles; there is no work or completion, only new versions and variations.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-020"></span>**The shrinkage of the present is paradoxical: rather than emptying it, everything pushes into the present at once, producing a pushing and shoving of images, events and information that makes contemplative lingering impossible, so that one zaps through the world.**
    - Truth and knowledge rest on duration and an extended time enframing the present with past and future, but they fade in the face of an increasingly shorter present.

## Fragrant Crystal of Time

Through a reading of Proust, Han argues that involuntary memory triggered by scent and taste crystallizes disparate temporal moments into a 'fragrant crystal of time' that stabilizes the disintegrating self, and that truth and beauty consist of relationships of similarity and friendship between things that only reveal themselves in slow, contemplative lingering.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-021"></span>**Proust's Recherche is an attempt to stabilize a self that threatens to disintegrate into a mere 'succession of moments'; the temporal crisis is experienced as an identity crisis, and Proust's narrative technique aims to restore duration to time against the 'age of haste' epitomized by the railway and cinematographic time.**
    - "the man that I was no longer exists, I am another person (je suis un autre)." —Marcel Proust
    - Proust's strategy of duration presupposes historical existence and a curriculum vitae; its scent is a scent of immanence, not of timeless eternity.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-022"></span>**The madeleine soaked in lime-blossom tea affords Marcel 'a little fragment of time in the pure state,' a temporal alchemy connecting sensations and memories into a fragrant crystal of time outside past and present; scent and taste, more fragile yet more enduring, survive the demise of persons and things as islands of duration.**
    - "taste and smell alone, more fragile but more enduring, more immaterial, more persistent, more faithful, remain poised a long time, like souls, remembering, waiting, hoping, amid the ruins of all the rest." —Marcel Proust
    - Time is compressed into 'sealed vessels' (vases clos), each filled with things of an absolutely different colour, scent and temperature.
    - McLuhan's report on brain-tissue stimulation reviving scent-saturated memories provides a physiological analogue; the sense of smell is 'iconic' and epic–narrative, stabilizing a self threatened with dissociation.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-023"></span>**Truth consists of relationships of similarity, friendship or affinity between things, opposed to the accident of pure contiguity; the formation of metaphor is a temporal practice that counteracts the atomization of being by rediscovering the connections a cinematographic vision suppresses.**
    - "truth will be attained by him only when he takes two different objects, states the connection between them... and encloses them in the necessary links of a well-wrought style." —Marcel Proust
    - Metaphors are the scent of things which they release when they befriend each other; truth means commitment, relationship and closeness.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-024"></span>**Beauty is owed to duration and contemplative synopsis—an afterglow or phosphorescence of things appearing only later—so the cinematographic age of haste, with its succession of point-like presences, has no access to beauty or truth, which unveil themselves only in lingering contemplation and ascetic restraint.**
    - 'Immediate enjoyment' cannot experience beauty because the beauty of a thing appears only much later, in the light of another thing or a reminiscence.
    - A scent is slow and cannot be accelerated like optical images; a society dominated by scents would develop no inclination toward change or acceleration.

## The Time of the Angel

Han examines the postmodern strategies of Lyotard (and Derrida) that respond to the end of narrative time by turning from meaning to being, but argues that Lyotard's rugged, discontinuous 'event-time' of the pure There leaves existence radically fragile—an anima minima oscillating between euphoria and the terror of death—and offers no time for living or dwelling.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-025"></span>**The end of the grand narrative is first a temporal crisis that destroys the gravitation gathering past and future into the present; postmodern thinkers (Derrida's messianism, Heidegger's turn to being) construct temporal strategies to counteract de-temporalization without falling back into old patterns of narration and identity.**
    - Narration is a mode of counting that links events into a story by selection, and is therefore blind, always having a blind spot; the end of recounting need not reduce life to mere counting.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-026"></span>**In the face of the crisis of meaning, Lyotard turns from meaning to being, treating the meaninglessness of narration as an experience of being that constitutes an ontological difference; when meaning retreats through de-narrativization, being announces itself, and events point not toward their narrative What but toward their bare That.**
    - "there is, even before that which is has any signification." —Jean-François Lyotard
    - Lyotard expects an 'intensification of being' (l'accroisement d'être) from the end of narration, bringing him into proximity with Heidegger.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-027"></span>**Lyotard mystifies time by placing 'the angel' and 'the instant' (Augenblick) close together: the moment is not a decayed particle but a vertical deepening of pure presence lacking profound meaning yet possessing profoundness of being; the 'sensation of time' precedes the synthesis of consciousness and affects rather than signifies, striking the soul as trauma rather than theme.**
    - "My paintings are concerned neither with the manipulation of space nor with the image, but with the sensation of time." —Barnett Newman
    - An event now promises nothing beyond its instantaneous presence; a discontinuous, rugged time emerges whose entire content is a naked There.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-028"></span>**Lyotard answers the decay of meaningful time not with nihilism but with a particular animism: the anima minima is a soul without mind awakened into existence only by the sensible aistheton, so that art becomes an art of pure presence bearing witness that something takes place; but Han objects that this rugged event-time on the edge of the abyss is a vegetative, not a livable, time.**
    - "The anima exists only as affected... This soul is but the awakening of an affectability, and this remains disaffected in the absence of a timbre, a color, a fragrance." —Jean-François Lyotard
    - The intervals between events are death zones; the soul falls into lethargy and joy mingles with fear of death, an ontological depression—living is more than vegetating.
    - Lyotard's mystery of pure being-There differs radically from Heidegger's, lacking the space for dwelling; there is a time for living that is neither narrative nor vegetative.

## Fragrant Clock: A Short Excursus on Ancient China

Through the Chinese incense clock (hsiang yin, 'seal of fragrance'), Han presents an alternative temporality in which time does not flow away but fills space as scent, turning time into space and duration; the good time arises for an emptied mind that rests in itself, connecting every moment with the fragrant intransience of all being.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-029"></span>**The Chinese incense clock measures time through fragrant fluidity that neither flows nor trickles away: the glow transforms incense into ash that retains the shape of the written characters (often fu, luck, or a kŏan), so the seal loses none of its meaning even as ash, and transience gives way to a feeling of duration.**
    - The clock is called 'seal of fragrance' because the incense-burning part has the shape of a seal; a mysterious kŏan reads 'How many lives before I obtain my flowers,' with 'my flowers' replaced by a flower image.
    - Silvio Bedini's documentation ('The Scent of Time,' 1963) first drew Western attention to this Far Eastern practice, which Europeans had mistaken for a mere censer; McLuhan drew on it in his chapter 'Clocks: The Scent of Time.'
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-030"></span>**Fragrant time turns time into space and creates a still life of time: the scent of incense fills the room and gives duration a semblance, while rising smoke perceived as figures (butterflies, dragons, phoenix) makes time congeal like a painting, arresting it in a 'clear image' rather than letting it trickle away.**
    - "When all the burning stops, a clear image is seen, / Of the green moss upon the epigraph's carved words." —Chinese poem quoted by Han
    - To the poet Ch'iao Chi the incense smoke appears as ancient script, and the censer's lingering warmth—while the moonlight has already died in the garden pool—gives a deep feeling of hesitant whiling and duration.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-031"></span>**These scents of time are contemplative rather than narrative: each time has its own proper scent (afternoon, evening, night) resting in itself rather than arranged in sequence, and the good time becomes accessible for a mind emptied of desire, whose depth connects every moment with all of being and its fragrant intransience.**
    - "Hundreds of flowers in spring, the moon in autumn, / A cool breeze in summer, and snow in winter; / If there is no vain cloud in your mind / For you it is a good season." —Zen verse (Mumonkan) quoted by Han
    - Desire itself makes time radically transient by causing the mind to rush ahead (fortstürzen); where it rests in itself, the good time occurs.

## The Round Dance of the World

Reading Being and Time against Heidegger's later writings, Han argues that Heidegger diagnosed the age of haste and the dispersed 'they' but mistakenly explained acceleration through Dasein's 'tendency towards nearness'; his mature response replaces historical, self-centered time with cyclical figures of repetition, the 'back and forth' and the fourfold 'round dance' that make time stand still and produce duration.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-032"></span>**Being and Time, published the same year (1927) as Proust's Time Regained, sets itself against the decay of time into point-like presences, but Heidegger wrongly explains the destruction of the everyday world through acceleration derived from Dasein's essential 'tendency towards nearness' (de-distancing), overlooking that the new media abolish space itself in a total implosion into a here and now.**
    - "With the 'radio', for example, Dasein is bringing about today a de-distancing of the 'world'... by way of expanding and destroying the everyday surrounding world." —Martin Heidegger
    - The total removal of space (new media, hyperlinks, electronic mail) is something altogether different from the de-distancing that affords Dasein a spatial existence; spaces with a scent hold their appearance in reserve (sparende Nähe).
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-033"></span>**Heidegger's 'they' (das Man) is a phenomenon of his own time, corresponding to Proust's cinematographic time of point-like presences; the emptiness of being goes hand in hand with acceleration, and Heidegger explains the general haste as an inability to perceive silence, the long-lasting and the slow.**
    - "Acceleration... not-being-able-to-bear the stillness of hidden growth... purely quantitative enhancement, blindness to what is truly momentary, which is not fleeting but opens up eternity." —Martin Heidegger
    - Heidegger speaks of a 'dispersed non-lingering' and a 'never dwelling anywhere' (Aufenthaltslosigkeit); the 'they' zaps through the world.
    - 'This not having any time is ultimately a greater being lost of the self'; inauthentic Dasein loses its time by losing itself to the world.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-034"></span>**Heidegger's early strategy re-anchors time by making the self provide direction, transforming 'I have no time' into 'I always have time' through the constancy of the authentic self, whose pre-narrative framing encloses 'birth and death and their between' as a fatefully whole stretching that does not need a narrative connection.**
    - "it is the distinction of the temporality of authentic existence that in resoluteness it never loses time and 'always has time'." —Martin Heidegger
    - Narrative identity establishes a connection, whereas Heidegger aims to extract the primordial stretching of the whole of existence that is not lost and needs no connection—a notion of identity viable in an age of de-narrativization.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-035"></span>**The later Heidegger abandons the historical model of time for cyclical figures of repetition—the seasons, the 'back and forth' (Hin und Her), the pendulum—that make time stand still and produce duration; the country path becomes a via contemplativa of eternal repetition and gathering in which nothing is lost and everything rests in an eternal presence.**
    - "Only when it stops, the clock, / with its pendulum swinging back and forth, / only then do you hear: it goes and is gone and goes no more." —Martin Heidegger (poem 'Time')
    - The 'back and forth' produces duration within cyclical change: all Forth is caught by a Back as if by an echo, silencing the world into the 'Same.'
    - The world becomes a 'round dance' of the fourfold (earth, sky, divinities, mortals), invoked in a poem of two symmetrical four-line stanzas ('Regen rinnt / Segen sinnt') whose metre creates the feeling of an eternally valid order.

## The Scent of Oak Wood

Han argues that the acceleration of life derives from the groundlessness and de-facticization of the world—the loss of belief that things exist of their own accord—and that Heidegger's philosophy of autochthony, the enduring 'thing,' and the contemplative gaze that goes easy on things offers a temporal strategy of conservatio, hold, and lingering against the tearing away of time.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-036"></span>**Acceleration derives ultimately from groundlessness (Haltlosigkeit)—the absence of abodes and holds—following the de-facticization of the world, in which the belief is lost that things exist of their own accord and remain in their suchness (So-Sein), so that facticity gives way to production and being is de-facticized into an accelerable, goal-directed process.**
    - A processor knowing only calculation can be accelerated to any degree because it lacks a meaningful rhythm; a computer does not hesitate, registering delay only as disturbance.
    - "Precipitance and surprise... The former arises in calculation. The latter comes out of the unexpected... The former follows a plan. The latter visits a lingering." —Martin Heidegger
    - Techniques of deceleration or relaxation cannot stop the tearing away of time (Fortriß der Zeit) because they do not remove the cause, which is the missing hold.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-037"></span>**Heidegger's 'thing' (exemplified by the jug, a container that gives a hold to its content so nothing trickles away) is a place of contemplative lingering outside usage and consumption; dwelling is a 'staying with things' that requires a hold, and only a hold comports (verhält) time and brings out what is durable.**
    - "Being is the protective heed that holds the human being in his ek-sistent essence to the truth of such protective heed." —Martin Heidegger
    - Han notes it is no coincidence Heidegger chose a jug (Krug), whose very word holds its breath through its closedness, better suited than a pitcher (Kanne) to illustrate contemplative rest.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-038"></span>**Heidegger uses conservatio as a temporal strategy for creating duration: humans are 'hearers of the Origin' whose distant origin gives a home, old age is wisdom, and God—the un-producable, unconditioned, stabilizer of eternal validity—reveals himself as a God of slowness and home only when the world is left to rest in its own time; every de-facticization of the world by human power leads to de-temporalization.**
    - God represents the un-producable that evades human intervention; the attempt to eliminate death would be sacrilege, a human 'machination' equal to eliminating God.
    - 'Meagre time' is a time without God and without scent; the accelerating noise of media (Apparate) is almost taken for the voice of God, drowning out the stillness in which God speaks.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-039"></span>**The contemplative gaze is a practice of friendliness that goes easy on things, preserving a distance without losing nearness ('a distanced nearness'), whereas work aims at domination and assimilation, destroying distance; Heidegger's temporal figures (hesitation, waiting, patience) express a positive relationship of a 'More of the Less' toward what withdraws and evades any violent capture.**
    - "in the long, contemplative look... the urge towards the object is always deflected, reflected. Contemplation without violence... presupposes that he who contemplates does not absorb the object into himself." —Theodor Adorno
    - The 'scent of oak wood' represents what endures and is slow; 'Abandonment does not take. Abandonment gives. It gives the inexhaustible power of the Simple.'

## Profound Boredom

Han argues that profound boredom arises not from uneventful time but from the emptiness of time in an age of resolute action, and that the later Heidegger's shift from the 'moment of vision' (resolute action) to 'releasement' recognizes that boredom is the flip side of compulsive activity, curable only when the vita activa reincorporates the vita contemplativa.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-040"></span>**Profound boredom arises in event-rich but duration-poor time, not in uneventful time: Büchner's revolutionary Danton is struck by profound boredom in the midst of dramatic action because the free subject's determination to act produces no temporal gravitation, so that 'time is losing us' and time itself becomes vacuous.**
    - "But time is losing us. How boring life is: day after day we put on our shirts and pull up our trousers, crawl into bed in the evening and out again in the morning." —Büchner's Danton
    - Boredom is not the antithesis of resolute action but involves it; the resolve to act deepens the boredom, and once duration decays the tiniest repetition is perceived as tormenting monotony.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-041"></span>**Heidegger diagnoses profound boredom as the fundamental attunement of the age, deriving it from beings' telling refusal of themselves as a whole, which leaves an emptiness in which Dasein can establish no temporal relationship (retrospection, inspection, prospection) with beings and thus no meaning.**
    - "Do things ultimately stand in such a way with us that a profound boredom draws back and forth like a silent fog in the abysses of Dasein?" —Martin Heidegger
    - Where no temporal perspective on beings is possible, time becomes amorphous; profound boredom is experienced as the total absence of meaning and derives from the emptiness of time.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-042"></span>**In 1929/30 Heidegger held that the redeeming 'moment of vision' (Augenblick)—Dasein's resolute self-disclosure for action—could break the entrancement of boredom, but he did not yet see that it is precisely the emphasis on resolute action and the freedom of the initium that empties time; thirty years later he recognizes profound boredom as hidden homesickness (Heimweh), a longing for home masked as addiction to distraction.**
    - "Presumably, this profound boredom – in the form of the addiction to distractions – is the hidden, unacknowledged, pushed aside and yet inescapable longing for home: hidden homesickness." —Martin Heidegger
    - The 'look of resolute disclosedness' is too short-sighted to capture what is long and slow; not more of the self but more of the world, not more activity but more lingering, breaks the spell of boredom.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-043"></span>**Profound boredom is the flip side of excessive activity, a vita activa lacking any contemplation; in the later Heidegger the emphasis on acting yields to 'releasement' (Gelassenheit), a counter-rest that grants dwelling in the world in a wholly different way and breaks the spell of compulsive activism.**
    - Concepts such as hesitancy, timidity (Scheu) and restraint are turns against the emphasis on acting; the subjectivity of an action-determined Dasein cannot found a home but means its end.

## Vita Contemplativa

In this concluding four-part chapter Han traces the historical revaluation of work from Aristotle's primacy of leisure through the Reformation's totalization of labour to the modern degradation of the human being into an animal laborans, and argues—against Hannah Arendt—that the loss of the contemplative faculty, not the primacy of contemplation, is responsible for modern restlessness, so that only a revitalization of the vita contemplativa can restore duration, freedom, and being.

* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-044"></span>**For Aristotle the nature of human existence is not care but leisure (schole): work is need-ful un-freedom subordinate to contemplative rest, and the highest happiness lies in the bios theoretikos, the contemplative lingering on the imperishable that brings man close to the gods; the modern concept of work as the fundamental trait of Dasein would have been alien to him.**
    - Aristotle divides life into non-leisure (a-scholia) and leisure (schole), non-rest and rest, subordinating work to leisure; St Augustine and Aquinas affirm the priority of contemplation ('vita contemplativa simpliciter melior est quam activa').
    - Leisure is not relaxation or switching off but a special ability requiring education, the opposite of lazy inactivity; the medieval calendar's festive days were narrative resting points structuring time, not days off work.
    - "What Heidegger actually should have said is: 'Aristotle was born, did not work, and died.'" —Byung-Chul Han
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-045"></span>**The Reformation, especially Calvinism, invests work with a theological meaning tied to the economy of salvation: uncertain of election, the Calvinist assures himself of grace through resolute ascetic action, so that anxiety over salvation turns the individual into a worker and prefigures the spirit of capitalism, whose compulsion to accumulate rests on a striving for salvation and power.**
    - "One does not only work in order to live, but one lives for the sake of one's work, and if there is no more work to do one suffers or goes to sleep." —Zinzendorf (quoted by Weber)
    - Protestant asceticism restricted consumption while freeing acquisition from traditionalist inhibitions, treating profit as willed by God; the economy of time and economy of salvation become intermingled (Baxter: esteem time more than gold and silver).
    - For Marx money effects a de-facticization suspending thrownness in favour of projectedness, even suspending ugliness: with money the ugly buyer can buy the most beautiful woman.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-046"></span>**Reinterpreting Hegel's master–slave dialectic as a dialectic of power and work, Han argues that the working slave's dispositif of labour becomes the predominant social dispositif, so history culminates not in freedom but in a society of work in which everyone becomes a slave to work; work does not set free but creates a new servitude, and consciousness will be fully free only if it liberates itself from the imperative of work.**
    - "This creative education of Man by work (Bildung) creates History – i.e., human Time. Work is Time." —Alexandre Kojève
    - The master, freed from work, could realize a contemplative (bios theoretikos) relationship to things not determined by domination or work—an aspect ignored by Hegel's power-and-subjectivity perspective in which every relation to things is negation.
    - Because Marx's subject remains a subject of labour, it is questionable whether it can transform into a 'different subject' capable of free time; outside work it is at best a consumer, and labourer and consumer both merely use up time.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-047"></span>**Against Hannah Arendt's rehabilitation of the vita activa, Han argues that human action is reduced to mere labour precisely by losing its contemplative aspects, not through the primacy of contemplation; Arendt wrongly treats contemplation as motionless passivity, whereas the vita contemplativa rests in itself yet is active (Aristotle's theoria as energeia, God as actus purus), and thinking surpasses all other activities because it is contemplative.**
    - "Active men are usually lacking in higher activity – I mean individual activity... Active people roll like a stone, conforming to the stupidity of mechanics." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - The passivity of the animal laborans is not the opposite of active life but its reverse side; being merely active agrees with the collective life process of the species and impoverishes experience, which requires a particular kind of passivity (letting oneself be concerned).
    - Arendt ends her book quoting Cato ('Never is he more active than when he does nothing'), a remark that actually praises the vita contemplativa, which she involuntarily advocates.
* <span id="the-scent-of-time-a-philosophical-essay-on-the-art-048"></span>**Thinking in the emphatic sense (pensive thinking, sinnendes Denken, originally meaning 'journeying') cannot be accelerated at will and progresses discontinuously via detours, unlike linear calculating, which is labour; the general time pressure of the absolutized vita activa destroys all that is indirect, ambivalent, or melodic, so that only a revitalization of the vita contemplativa can liberate humans from the compulsion to labour and give time and space, duration and vastness.**
    - "From lack of rest, our civilization is ending in a new barbarism... one of the necessary correctives that must be applied to the character of humanity is a massive strengthening of the contemplative element." —Friedrich Nietzsche
    - Contemplative lingering interrupts the time that is labour and gives time; reflection (Besinnung) begins where thinking-that-labours stops, opening access to what is slow and evades quick capture—only in the hesitating hand's pause does an immeasurable space open.
    - Gregory (via Meister Eckhart) mediates the two lives: contemplation lit in the heart passes its perfection to activity and calls back to activity; Heidegger's 'Pathway' is a via contemplativa, and 'schonen' (to spare/go gentle) derives from Middle High German meaning 'friendly.'
    - The democratization of work must be followed by a democratization of otium, lest work turn into the bondage of everyone; whoever runs out of breath (pneuma as breath and spirit) is without spirit.
