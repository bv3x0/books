## Kristjan Kullamägi: From Living to Trade to Trading to Live

Kullamägi transformed a $5,000 account into over $100 million by mastering three repeatable trade setups—episodic pivots, momentum breakouts, and parabolic shorts—learned entirely from others, demonstrating that disciplined application of well-researched patterns with strict risk control matters far more than original insight. His catastrophic 2022 losses stemmed directly from abandoning his own rule of avoiding long trades in downtrending markets, illustrating that violating a proven methodology is the primary cause of even skilled traders' worst periods.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-001"></span>**Kullamägi's initial profitable methodology centered on buying intraday breakouts in high-volume, news-driven stocks, with the protective stop always placed at the low of the day—an approach that worked only after he added disciplined risk control, having blown up three accounts by going all-in without stops.**
    - He blew up accounts at ages 13–17 by combining maximum leverage with no position-sizing rules, requiring him to return repeatedly to his security guard job to save fresh capital.
    - The turning point was switching to a US-based broker that enabled shorting and slashing commissions by ~90%, combined with establishing a maximum loss per trade and per day.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-002"></span>**The episodic pivot—a stock gapping sharply higher on surging volume in response to transformative news—is Kullamägi's primary trade setup, requiring all three conditions (major catalyst, large gap, massive volume expansion) before entry, with the low of the day as the protective stop.**
    - Tempus AI in January 2025 and PTON in August 2024 exemplified the setup: neglected or beaten-down stocks that suddenly experienced ten-times average volume after genuine business-changing announcements.
    - Kullamägi credits the concept to Pradeep Bonde of Stockbee, who in turn traces the idea to Paul Tudor Jones and other Market Wizards.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-003"></span>**Kullamägi's parabolic short strategy—waiting for a small-cap stock to rise 300%-plus over three to five consecutive days before shorting—requires waiting for an A+ setup on day three or later, because going short on day one of a rally risks unlimited losses before the eventual collapse.**
    - LAES in December 2024 showed both sides: he bought the episodic pivot correctly but exited prematurely when the stock had not hit his stop, missing an 800% subsequent move—a lesson that rules must override subjective mental state.
    - The same LAES stock later provided a classic parabolic short on day five of its run, which he executed profitably after being stopped out on an earlier attempt.
* <span class="pull-quote">I used to live to trade, and now I trade to live.</span>
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-004"></span>**A simple 10-day/20-day moving average crossover on broad market indexes serves as Kullamägi's market-condition filter: he takes breakout and episodic pivot longs only when both averages are rising, and sits out of those strategies entirely when the 10-day crosses below the 20-day.**
    - Following this rule could have prevented roughly 90% of his 2022 losses; he ignored the bearish crossover signal and kept buying breakouts in a downtrending market, driven by overconfidence after his $36M-to-$105M run.
    - He estimates his winning percentage averages 25–30%, meaning the strategy works because average wins are far larger than average losses, not because most trades are profitable.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-005"></span>**Kullamägi's worst period—losing approximately $60 million from his November 2021 peak—resulted from a combination of ego detachment from reality after extreme success, abandoning his market-environment filter, and maintaining leveraged long exposure of $150 million in a turning market.**
    - "The move from $3.5M to $36M in 2020 and then to $105M created a psychological detachment: 'I started seeing trading as a video game, which I kept winning.'" —Kristjan Kullamägi
    - After the peak, distraction from traveling on a yacht and reduced chart-review discipline contributed to mediocre performance in 2023 and 2024, including missing an obvious SMCI episodic pivot in January 2024.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-006"></span>**Kullamägi risks no more than 0.5–1% of account equity per trade, using the day's low as the hard stop on buys and the day's high on shorts, refusing trades where the distance to the stop exceeds the average daily true range—a framework that prevents any single trade from causing lasting damage.**
    - He credits this position-sizing discipline, absent in his early blow-up years, as the primary structural change enabling his long-term survival and compounding.
    - Winning percentage is irrelevant; expected gain per trade—the product of win probability times average win minus loss probability times average loss—is what matters.

## Lance Breitstein: Beyond Trading Success

Breitstein built a 10-year day trading career at Trillium generating $46 million in net profits—and then doubled that in five subsequent years of personal account trading by shifting to longer holding periods—proving that the same chart-pattern and news-reaction skills that create an edge in fast trading can generate far more money at a slightly extended time frame with much lower intensity. His chapter concludes with his deliberate effort to leave trading behind for a more balanced life, arguing that relentlessly optimizing for monetary gain is neither a sustainable nor the most fulfilling path.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-007"></span>**Breitstein chose a $26,000 salary at Trillium over a $100,000 Bank of Montreal offer because empirical conversations with Trillium traders confirmed that consistent six- and seven-figure annual profits were achievable—treating the salary differential as a two-year investment in an asymmetric opportunity.**
    - He deliberately sought placement at Trillium's Princeton satellite office to sit next to the firm's top trader, sacrificing the social life of New York City for mentorship, living on a $400/month room share.
    - His video game background—particularly strategy games that reward delayed gratification—prepared him mentally for the discipline required in trading.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-008"></span>**Breitstein recorded key trading moments each day and replayed them at varying speeds on Sundays for 10 years, practicing at 1.5–2× speed so that real-time trading felt slowed down—a training technique that compressed years of pattern recognition into a much shorter period.**
    - He lost money for 11 consecutive months after joining Trillium, with the turnaround coming not from one discovery but from the cumulative effect of marginal improvements captured through this review process.
    - A standout early trade—shorting Tesla in 2013 via an exhaustion gap pattern—showed him his mentor made 25× as much on the same trade through position size alone, revealing that methodology was transferable and that sizing was the variable.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-009"></span>**Breitstein's core trade types—exhaustion gaps, capitulation panics, bouncy-ball shorts, and inverse bouncy-ball breakouts—are all predicated on identifying extreme sentiment and price velocity, then fading or following through, with entry timed on two-minute bars and stops placed just below (or above) the prior two-minute bar's low (or high).**
    - The Nikkei futures panic on August 5, 2024 was his largest single trade: he bought the capitulation—one of the largest three-day selloffs ever in the Nikkei, surpassing even peak COVID panic—risking approximately $2 million pre-tax (about 5% of his account) and making roughly $10 million.
    - Breaking news trades, such as the SMCI auditor resignation short and the Oracle Stargate announcement long, exploit the gap between algo speed on one-line headlines and human nuance in interpreting complex context.
* <span class="pull-quote">Bad trades don't kill people; bad sizing kills people.</span>
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-010"></span>**Breitstein's worst career loss—over $2 million on Avis (CAR) in November 2021—stemmed not from a flawed setup but from anchoring to a $20 million annual profit goal, causing him to size the trade far larger than he would have otherwise, illustrating that external targets corrupt objective position sizing.**
    - CAR eventually ran from his short entry near $300 to $550 after he correctly stopped out; had he not exited, the position could have ended his career.
    - "'The market demands you size your trades objectively based upon expected value'—a lesson he says he internalized only after this experience." —Lance Breitstein
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-011"></span>**After leaving Trillium, Breitstein shifted to multi-day trades in his personal account, becoming approximately 1% as active as before yet generating $71 million in five years on net deposits of $5 million—demonstrating that scalability, not frequency, determines long-run profitability for skilled traders.**
    - The same chart analysis and news-reaction skills applied to daily rather than two-minute bars, with stops placed below the prior day's low instead of the prior two-minute bar's low, while allowing profits to run over days rather than hours.
    - He also sells out-of-the-money puts during extreme panic selloffs at strike prices where he would genuinely want to own the stock, achieving an 80–90% win rate while using short calls to hedge long volatility exposure.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-012"></span>**Breitstein is actively attempting to exit trading entirely for health and life-balance reasons—a sleep study confirmed he has among the worst sleep patterns outside active military—arguing that beyond a certain level of wealth, continued trading represents a net negative tradeoff against presence, peace of mind, and purpose.**
    - He spent approximately $700,000 and 15 months creating a trading course (theonelanceb.com), donating all net profits to charity, as a way to pass on his knowledge as a trading legacy before stepping back.
    - He cites Bill Gates post-Microsoft, Jeff Bezos leaving D.E. Shaw to found Amazon, and Bryan Johnson's health movement as models of redirecting wealth and attention toward greater impact.

## Simon Russo: The Fifth Time Is the Charm

Under a pseudonym, Russo describes turning a $40,000 stake into over $500 million by developing a highly selective short-selling methodology targeting small-cap stocks whose SEC filings reveal hidden selling pressure, then expanding to long trades in 'broken shorts' and pyramiding winning positions—a strategy that worked brilliantly when followed but produced two catastrophic $50-million losses whenever he deviated from his rules. His story is ultimately about the iron logic that discipline is more valuable than insight, and that even extraordinary skill cannot prevent ruin when trading outside one's methodology.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-013"></span>**Russo blew up his account five times in the years 2013–2015, each time for the same root cause: taking positions without predefined stops that allowed a small scalp trade to balloon into an account-destroying loss, with brokers ultimately covering him on margin calls.**
    - A biotech short in August 2015 wiped out a $550,000 account that had been built from $50,000 in four months; the trade started as a $2,000 scalp target but became catastrophic when he added to the loser and held overnight without a stop.
    - After his fifth blow-up left his account negative $10,000, a fellow trader in an online community lent him $50,000 interest-free; he doubled it in one month, repaid the loan plus a $10,000 bonus, and used the remaining $40,000 as his permanent stake.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-014"></span>**Russo's core short-selling methodology requires three layers of analysis before entry: the news catalyst must be identifiably meaningless upon scrutiny (not genuine business improvement), the company's financials must show bearish conditions such as low cash or high burn rate, and the capital structure must reveal identifiable sellers such as convertible debt holders or warrants.**
    - He catalogued every SEC filing type to learn which sections signal dilutive events; a stock with $20M in convertible debt at $3 gapping to $5 on fluff news was his canonical setup because the converts would immediately sell.
    - He avoided shorting stocks with genuinely positive news (a biotech reporting real phase-three results, a company moving to profitability) regardless of how much the stock had run.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-015"></span>**A 'broken short' strategy—going long a stock that displays the hallmarks of a short candidate but refuses to break down—became Russo's most profitable long-side trade type, because the trapped shorts create explosive buying pressure when the stock moves to new highs rather than collapsing.**
    - The tell for a broken short is that the upmove is gradual and trending rather than parabolic, that any pullback is immediately bought, and that price forms tight intraday consolidations before breaking out to new daily highs.
    - Virpax Pharmaceuticals (VRPX) in August 2021 illustrated the pattern: after an initial failed long attempt stopped out, the subsequent move to new highs while shorts were trapped confirmed the broken-short signal.
* <span class="pull-quote">Relying on willpower in trading is like a recovering alcoholic keeping a bottle of whiskey on the kitchen counter to test his discipline.</span>
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-016"></span>**Russo's pyramiding strategy—adding to winning short positions only when market action allows him to lower his stop, keeping total dollar risk constant while increasing potential profit—dramatically raised his average win-to-loss ratio and accounts for why his hit rate of 71% is paired with an unusually high average win size.**
    - He waited for intraday chart signals (such as a lower high forming) before adding, then sized the addition so that the full position still risked the same dollar amount as originally defined, preventing pyramiding from increasing exposure.
    - His best trades in 2021—GameStop ($10M and $7M), AMC ($10M), and DWAC ($5M)—all used this pyramiding approach on multi-day parabolic moves.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-017"></span>**Russo's two worst career losses—each approximately $50 million—occurred in late 2023/early 2024 during a period of reduced trading involvement and overconfidence, and both shared the same structural flaw: he entered or maintained positions that violated his own selection criteria and held without stops.**
    - The SPY short was taken purely because of recency bias after a successful long mean reversion; the market's push to new highs was an obvious 'broken short' signal he ignored due to P&L anchoring.
    - The Carvana short escalated from a 0.5% risk trade to catastrophic exposure when he added to a loser and held through a surprise bullish earnings gap—he eventually exited at ~$68 for a $50M loss; Carvana subsequently reached $292.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-018"></span>**After a near-career-ending postscript drawdown of $120 million (from which he recovered), Russo replaced mental stops with automated mechanical risk controls: a tiered system that automatically liquidates 50% of any position exceeding 1.2× its risk tier and the remainder at 1.5×, with a panic override at 3× tier risk.**
    - "'Systems over willpower, mechanical over manual, and hard stops over hope'—his conclusion that designing systems to eliminate temptation is superior to relying on discipline in the moment." —Simon Russo
    - Despite his $500M+ cumulative profits, Russo attributes significant portions to luck and luck-adjacent timing, and warns that in parallel universes the same reckless loss behavior would have ended his career.

## Lukas Fröhlich: Metamorphosis: Short-Term Short to Long-Term Long

Fröhlich achieved a verified 1,132,045% return in 2020 by exploiting small-cap pump-and-dump patterns through rigorous SEC filing analysis and quantitative pattern research, then successfully pivoted to mid-cap momentum longs during the 2020–21 bull market, and subsequently evolved into a long-term value-plus-catalyst investment approach—three radically different strategies each executed profitably, driven by his relentless goal of growing his account to $1 billion. His evolution illustrates that matching strategy to market environment and scaling methodology to account size are as important as the strategies themselves.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-019"></span>**Fröhlich began trading at 13 using his father's account with money saved from gifts, repeatedly blowing up small accounts of $500–$1,000 by combining maximum CFD leverage (up to 100:1) with volatile European small-cap stocks and no defined methodology—losing his bicycle to cover a margin call at one point.**
    - "His initial motivation was escaping school and gaining freedom, not financial sophistication: 'suffering can be a great motivator.'" —Lukas Fröhlich
    - He eventually stopped blowing up by establishing a maximum loss per trade and a maximum daily portfolio loss—simple rules that forced him to survive long enough to develop an edge.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-020"></span>**Fröhlich's small-cap shorting edge came from combining fundamental analysis (SEC filings showing impending capital needs, identifying unscrupulous underwriters, detecting bogus press releases) with quantitative pattern research of 25 years of historical data on how stocks with similar gap-up characteristics behaved intraday.**
    - A textbook example was SONN on April 14, 2020: a stock with near-zero working capital, warrants and convertibles signaling imminent dilutive selling, and a fabricated gap-up news release—all identified in about 20 minutes.
    - He pyramided into winning short positions by entering with half the intended size and adding only when the trade showed weakness, which he attributes as a key driver of his outsized returns.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-021"></span>**Fröhlich's mid-cap breakout strategy—buying $1–5B market cap stocks with small floats after a 40–100% catalyst-driven surge, then adding on consolidation breakouts and pullbacks to the 20-day EMA—was designed explicitly for the euphoric phase of bull markets and required willingness to accept many small losses for rare exponential wins.**
    - GrowGeneration Corp. (GRWG) in late 2020–early 2021 exemplified the trade: a 100%+ earnings-driven catalyst, months of consolidation, two pyramid additions on a second catalyst and an EMA pullback, and exit at signs of hysteria.
    - "'Ninety-nine percent of traders who use this type of strategy will fail, not because of mistakes in applying it, but because they continue to use it when the market environment is no longer appropriate.'" —Lukas Fröhlich
* <span class="pull-quote">As soon as something becomes too easy or a consensus trade, the game changes.</span>
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-022"></span>**Fröhlich's current strategy combines Soros/Druckenmiller-style macro conviction with value investing, targeting out-of-favor stocks with strong fundamentals, low valuations, high short interest, and an anticipated catalyst that the market is not pricing in—entered technically when the stock reclaims its 200-day moving average.**
    - Alibaba (BABA) in 2024 embodied the approach: trading at a P/E near 10 despite double-digit earnings growth and cloud optionality, with Fröhlich anticipating a Chinese government stimulus package that the consensus missed—after six failed starter positions costing 2% of equity, the eventual position made more than 20× those losses.
    - Centene (CNC) in 2025 was a second example: healthcare stocks had sold off catastrophically on regulatory fears, but Fröhlich expected political protection of ACA subsidies as a high-probability surprise catalyst.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-023"></span>**Fröhlich's worst mistake was buying beaten-down growth stocks too aggressively in the 2022 bear market—taking positions that were too large, adding to losers, and ignoring the broader market cycle—resulting in a 40% drawdown, from which he learned to wait for stocks to reclaim their 200-day moving average before building substantial positions.**
    - "'Underestimating how long bear markets can last'—he viewed cheap valuations as sufficient reason to buy but failed to account for the market continuing to discount Fed rate hikes far longer than expected." —Lukas Fröhlich
    - He ultimately recovered when the market discounted Fed hikes and the AI boom began, lifting the growth stocks he had stubbornly held.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-024"></span>**Fröhlich argues that experienced traders systematically undersize their best positions relative to the Kelly criterion, and that risking 5–10% on a single high-conviction trade—while aggressive-seeming—is likely still well below the theoretically optimal fraction, provided the edge can be reasonably estimated.**
    - He follows Soros and Druckenmiller in pushing size aggressively during strong performance periods, viewing position sizing as the mechanism for transforming a sound process into outsized returns.
    - He would start trading crypto if beginning today, viewing it as analogous to small-cap stocks 15 years ago: highly inefficient, volatile, and before institutional saturation.

## Phil Goedeker: The Impatient Trader

Goedeker turned $5,000 into $53 million over 20 years using two strategies—shorting parabolic small-cap rallies and selling deep out-of-the-money options—both widely considered prone to account-destroying losses, yet he avoided catastrophe through an almost innate reflexive instinct to exit positions immediately when they fail to move in his favor. His story demonstrates that matching strategy to personality (his impatience made him a natural mean-reversion short seller) and applying radically different position sizes to A+ versus ordinary setups are the two most consequential trading decisions a trader can make.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-025"></span>**Goedeker discovered short selling in college after two years of losing $3,000 accounts by blindly buying uptrending stocks; the concept of betting on stocks going down was a revelation because most stocks he bought went down anyway, and he quickly found that parabolic small-cap rallies on fabricated news reliably collapsed.**
    - His first short—Host America, which rallied from $3 to $16 on a false Walmart partnership claim—was halted for two weeks before reopening near $5, nearly tripling his account and defining his career direction.
    - He spent years studying tens of thousands of charts, keeping annotated binders of the biggest winning (and losing) stocks each year, building the pattern library that underpinned his edge.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-026"></span>**Goedeker's pivotal performance improvement came from analyzing 500 prior year tickers and discovering that only 15 produced large gains while most losses came from non-A+ trades sized identically to his best setups—leading him to implement radically different position sizing for A+ versus ordinary trades.**
    - An A+ trade is defined simply: a trashy, small-cap stock after a parabolic rally. On these trades he sizes up aggressively; on all others, he sizes down by ~90% to make them financially meaningless.
    - This change—combining moderately larger A+ sizing with drastically smaller other-trade sizing—pushed his annual profits above $1 million for the first time and every subsequent year.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-027"></span>**Goedeker's risk management philosophy is nearly reflexive: either a trade moves in his direction almost immediately after entry, or he exits—taking what he calls 'paper-cut losses'—because every moment a position is not working, his edge is decaying toward zero.**
    - "In a group short where other traders added at $12 and $13 as the stock rose, Goedeker covered at $10.50 because 'if I'm losing money, it means my entry was wrong, and I'll get out. I can always get in again later.'" —Phil Goedeker
    - This same instinct applied to his Liberation Day hedge on April 3, 2025: he bought 800–900 SPY puts to fully neutralize his option portfolio when down only $100,000, locking in that loss while the market fell another 13% in the following days.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-028"></span>**Goedeker's option-selling strategy—predominantly deep out-of-the-money puts on mega-cap stocks (Apple, Amazon, Microsoft), with strikes 10–50% below market depending on expiration—generates steady income in normal markets but requires an instinctive willingness to immediately hedge or exit during sharp selloffs, a skill he explicitly warns most traders do not possess.**
    - He learned not to sell calls in volatile small-caps after a $1 million loss on GameStop calls, and now sells calls only on mega-caps where buyout risk is near zero.
    - He keeps only about 10% of his total assets in his trading account so that even a catastrophic one-day crash scenario would not endanger his overall wealth.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-029"></span>**Goedeker deliberately sets low annual income goals (e.g., $2 million after a $6 million year) because he recognizes that trading performance depends on what the market provides for his specific strategy, and that ambitious targets lead to overtrading in low-opportunity environments—his worst behavior pattern.**
    - 2020 was his best year ($10M+) because the post-stimulus euphoric market produced multiple small-cap stocks gapping 1,000%+ daily, almost all of which failed in the final hour—a structural tailwind for his strategy despite being a bull market.
    - He has traded every day for 20 years including vacations, but only for 30 minutes to an hour daily, reflecting his love of the game rather than compulsion or financial necessity.

## Kelvin Chiu: Trading Both Sides of Asymmetric Trades

Chiu achieved a 108.7% compound annual growth rate over seven-plus years as an independent trader—primarily in agricultural futures spreads—by evolving from a conservative positive-asymmetry-only approach to incorporating carefully timed negative-asymmetry trades, and by reframing his identity and risk management through cognitive behavioral therapy after a 25% drawdown revealed that he had been conflating trading outcomes with personal self-worth. His journey illustrates that intellectual mastery of markets is insufficient without the psychological infrastructure to endure drawdowns and the courage to size up when conviction is highest.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-030"></span>**Chiu's first two years at Goldman Sachs's agricultural options desk were consistently losing—down nearly $1 million each year—because he had no defined method, was trading on emotion and other traders' ideas, and was engaging in damaging negative self-talk that put him in a 'strange place antithetical to achievement.'**
    - High academic achievement (straight As at Cambridge, top 50 in Hong Kong citywide exams) created unrealistic expectations and low resilience: having never been wrong frequently before, the reality of trading—where even great traders lose more than half the time—conflicted with his ego.
    - "Having his trading book taken away and being made junior to an experienced trader was 'one of the best things that ever happened to me' because it lifted the emotional weight and allowed curiosity to replace fear." —Kelvin Chiu
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-031"></span>**Chiu's core methodology at Goldman and Vitol was calendar spread and relative value trading in agricultural commodities—going long the nearer contract and short the forward when the spread approached full carry (the maximum theoretically justified discount), capturing the bounded downside with theoretically unlimited upside.**
    - The 2016 Kansas City/Chicago wheat spread trade exemplified the approach: the discount of hard red winter wheat to soft red wheat reached the arbitrage boundary where commercial firms like Cargill could profitably deliver cheaper wheat to Chicago, creating essentially unlimited buying support.
    - A $10 million loss on an old-crop/new-crop soybean meal spread in 2016—where he shorted old crop without fundamental justification, riding the spread from 14 to 45 before exiting around 38—taught him to avoid 'concave' trades (limited upside, theoretically unlimited loss).
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-032"></span>**After going independent in 2018, Chiu initially restricted himself to only positive-asymmetric ('convex') trades, achieving excellent return/risk statistics but missing highly attractive negative-asymmetric opportunities—such as a corn spread trade in 2019—until he recalibrated toward maximizing absolute profits while keeping blow-up risk at zero.**
    - The key innovation for safely incorporating negative-asymmetric trades was timing: waiting for the spread to form a clear top and for expiration to be close enough that a meaningful renewed rally became exponentially less likely, then adding progressively as the position moved in his favor.
    - The May/July Chicago wheat spread trade following Russia's Ukraine invasion—sold from 75 cents premium down to a short covering at −12—demonstrated the approach: he waited until early sellers had been squeezed out and price action confirmed the top before building his maximum 11,000-spread position.
* <span class="pull-quote">You have to be aggressive when you see an exceptional trading opportunity.</span>
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-033"></span>**A 25% drawdown in 2021 led Chiu to cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), through which he identified his limiting belief—measuring self-worth by trading P&L—and reframed his identity around three core values (growth, resilience, courage) so that drawdowns became part of the journey rather than crises of self.**
    - "'When I put on a trade, I either make money or learn'—the reframing that allowed him to evaluate trades by process quality rather than outcome, eliminating the emotional damage of losses." —Kelvin Chiu
    - CBT also motivated his expansion from agricultural futures into equities, copper, and EU carbon allowances by challenging his limiting belief that he was 'only good at grains'—a belief he overcame by recognizing his analytical tools were market-agnostic.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-034"></span>**Chiu's analytical framework distinguishes regime changes (sudden reversals in dominant market narrative that catch participants off guard, creating explosive moves) from persistent themes (gradually-priced dominant factors that generate trends), with regime changes offering the highest conviction and most aggressive sizing opportunities.**
    - European carbon allowances in early 2022 illustrated intuition-driven regime recognition: the price failed to rally on the Ukraine invasion despite the bullish thesis about coal substitution, signaling to every 'bone in my body' to exit—the market then collapsed from €85 to €55 in four days.
    - He uses conviction stops rather than price stops for spread trades because spreads must expire at fundamentally determined fair value—the closer to expiration, the lower the risk of extreme deviation, making conviction the appropriate risk metric.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-035"></span>**Chiu is building a multi-strategy firm by hiring traders with deeply ingrained risk management, a generalist technical approach, and prior independent trading experience, while personally focusing on Japanese small- and mid-cap equities trading below net cash as a generational opportunity driven by corporate governance reforms.**
    - One holding: a Japanese digital advertising company with a $160M market cap sitting on $140M in net cash plus $80M in long-term investments—market cap below asset value—where Chiu owns 9% and is engaging management on buybacks and dividends.
    - He also has conviction on uranium due to AI-driven power demand, Chinese and Indian nuclear expansion, and Russian control of global supply chains creating a Western supply premium.

## Jason Berry: Mr. Consistency

Berry achieved one of the smoothest equity curves in Market Wizards history—only five losing months over 14 years, none consequential—by combining an almost reflexive instinct to cut losing positions immediately with a relentless career-length search for new trading edges, because he recognized early that all strategies eventually erode and that a 'trainee mindset' of continuous innovation is the only reliable path to trading longevity. His proprietary firm Positive Equity, which has trained over 200 traders in Dublin and Croatia, embeds this philosophy into a multi-year training program built around the three Cs: control, consistency, and confidence.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-036"></span>**Berry's trading is predominantly spread-based—buying one market while simultaneously selling a closely related market—which he credits as a larger cumulative profit source than directional trades, because the inherent hedge reduces noise while the mean-reversion of price ratios between correlated markets provides a quantifiable edge.**
    - His most profitable years came in extreme volatility environments like 2008 (making £160,000 in one day when four central banks simultaneously cut rates), because volatile markets provide larger spread dislocations and more pronounced mean-reversion opportunities.
    - Earlier strategies such as Nokia/FESX relative value trades and S&P mini/SPY arbitrage worked until faster players arbitraged them away; Berry simply moved on to new strategies rather than fighting the erosion.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-037"></span>**Berry identifies specific time windows within each market's trading day when momentum patterns are most reliable, and trades only during those windows—refusing to trade during choppy periods—a time-of-day consideration not mentioned in any prior Market Wizards book.**
    - His ability to recognize when a market is 'behaving normally' versus unusually is a form of experiential market feel developed over decades, allowing him to distinguish meaningful signals from noise in real time.
    - He uses 1-, 2-, and 5-minute bars and targets 'fantastic fill feeling' (FFF) trades—positions where the market moves instantly in his direction, with a partial fill being a particularly bullish signal.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-038"></span>**Berry pre-plans his response to potential news scenarios—mapping out how different tariff levels, geopolitical events, or policy shifts would affect each market he trades—so that when the news breaks, he can react instantaneously rather than analyzing in real time during chaotic market conditions.**
    - This scenario-planning habit also enables him to spot mispricings in initial market reactions: if news should be more bullish for the S&P than the NASDAQ but NASDAQ spikes more due to volatility, Berry can immediately buy S&P and sell NASDAQ.
    - He maintains a personal historical database of annotated charts showing how markets responded to past major news events, giving him a reference framework that most traders lack.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-039"></span>**Berry's training program at Positive Equity uses a three-C progression (control → consistency → confidence) and a two-to-three year timeline, including nine-to-18 months of simulation trading before live trading, with the firm teaching 20–30 structural strategy frameworks but requiring trainees to self-discover and own their own edges.**
    - He identifies problem traders early by observable signs: extremely low trading volume (fear of pulling the trigger) and inability to cut losers—one trader who couldn't cut losses went elsewhere, had a €10,000 up day, then a €245,000 loss.
    - Success rate improved dramatically when hiring standards tightened to favor 'A candidates'—people who have competed and succeeded in demanding settings—over 'maybes' who tick only some boxes.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-040"></span>**Berry insists that all edges eventually erode—his VIX strategy evaporated overnight when the Ukraine war began in 2022, without any clear causal connection—and that maintaining a 'trainee mindset' of constant innovation is the defining secret of his 27-year career longevity.**
    - Nothing he traded at the beginning of his career is relevant today; the advent of algorithmic trading eliminated the simple human-executable edges while creating new opportunities in spotting where algos themselves err.
    - He writes a daily trading summary, rereads it the next morning, and conducts monthly reviews—not P&L accounting but qualitative assessments of psychology, sizing decisions, and whether he let trades run as far as warranted.

## Kenny Sharkness: The Only Constant Is Change

Sharkness built a 62.9% compound annual growth rate over a decade at SMB/Kershner by constantly adapting a growing repertoire of strategies across multiple timeframes, but with a crucial insight: despite trading dozens of strategies, roughly 80% of his annual profits come from only five or six days where he sizes up aggressively into high-conviction opportunities. His story demonstrates that a multitasking personality enabling diverse strategy deployment is valuable primarily because it keeps a trader engaged and prepared for the rare moments of extreme opportunity—which are the true source of elite performance.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-041"></span>**Sharkness began as a momentum day trader focused on high-volume breakouts from tight consolidations, learning from SMB mentor Gilberto Mendez the concept of 'energy'—stocks expend energy in directional moves and need time or price corrections to recharge before the next move—a framework he still uses across all timeframes.**
    - He became profitable after about 10 months, starting with 100-share lots, and reached consistent profitability only in his second year—then had no losing month for an extended period.
    - He always looked at multiple timeframes before entry (tick, 5-minute, hourly, daily) to ensure shorter-term signals were not invalidated by the broader context, a habit his mentor instilled from the beginning.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-042"></span>**The merger of SMB with Kershner was the single most important career inflection point for Sharkness: seeing Kershner traders making $25,000–$50,000 per day shattered his belief that $2,000/day was an aspirational ceiling, and access to their trade executions allowed him to absorb 'gimmick trades' (index rebalances, IPO opens, end-of-day imbalances) that had far greater scale potential.**
    - The Roger Bannister parallel: until he saw others achieving it, Sharkness did not believe the scale was possible—once the limiting belief was broken, his own performance scaled accordingly.
    - He adopted parabolic short trading after learning it from Peter To (churningandburning.com), confirming the pattern through reviewing ~1,000 daily charts per day and noting the consistent tendency for post-parabolic collapses.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-043"></span>**Sharkness's rigorous daily journaling—recording his best and worst trade each day in a 'playbook' and 'anti-playbook,' conducting weekly and monthly reviews, and tracking how emotional states affect subsequent trading—is the primary mechanism through which he identifies and corrects recurring behavioral flaws, particularly his self-diagnosed impatience.**
    - Working with Dr. Brett Steenbarger, who reviewed his daily trading summaries and provided process-improvement feedback, was instrumental in developing larger position sizing on high-conviction trades and reducing over-eagerness.
    - He sets process goals (e.g., 'pause before exiting a profitable trade') rather than monetary goals, reasoning that monetary targets cause overtrading in low-opportunity environments—his worst year (2022) partially resulted from chasing prior years' performance in unfavorable conditions.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-044"></span>**Sharkness combines directional shorts and longs with a sophisticated options overlay—buying out-of-the-money calls when volatility is low to express a bullish thesis on a parabolic stock, rolling up strikes as the stock rises, then selling puts when volatility spikes to lock in gains, and ultimately going short the stock when conditions align—creating a dynamic position that profits at multiple stages of the move.**
    - CoreWeave (CRWV) in mid-2025 illustrated the strategy: bought OTM calls at low volatility, rolled to higher strikes as the stock rose from $40 IPO to $160, planned to go short around $200 where short squeeze exhaustion was expected.
    - SMCI in 2024 was his best trade: initiated long calls, went short the stock on day two or three of the rally, made most of the money on the short side as the stock eventually collapsed.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-045"></span>**Sharkness's 2022 drawdown—his worst by far—resulted from attempting to replicate 2020–21 performance in a fundamentally different market environment: stocks showed one-day moves suggesting momentum but lacked follow-through, and he took suboptimal trades to maintain activity rather than accepting that the market was not providing opportunities for his strategies.**
    - He recovered by drastically reducing size until regaining consistency, then rebuilding slowly when a great trade restored his confidence—a pattern he recognizes as necessary whenever P&L control is lost.
    - His KBIO loss (~$150,000 when Martin Shkreli's reverse merger caused a stock he was short to go from $2 to $15) was the more psychologically significant career event because recovering it gave him the confidence that defined his subsequent trading.

## Rick Bandazian Jr.: Beating the Bots

Bandazian achieved over 11 consecutive years of all-winning months with an average annual return of 316% by finding a niche the algorithms cannot efficiently exploit: event-driven trading around merger and corporate-action SEC filings requiring nuanced multi-factor analysis, where his 20 years of deal expertise and pre-built scenario analyses allow him to act correctly within minutes of a filing while machines misread or miss the embedded complexities. His extraordinary consistency comes not from a complex system but from extreme risk aversion—refusing to hold positions more than five minutes on average unless they behave exactly as expected—combined with the willingness to work effectively a 16-hour day to maintain this preparedness.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-046"></span>**Bandazian's trading career evolved from investment banking (J.P. Morgan syndicated finance) to event-driven equity analysis to merger arbitrage at First New York Securities, where accidentally trading a merger headline and doubling a month's salary in minutes revealed his true edge and prompted him to abandon all other strategies.**
    - His decade-long career before discovering this niche—spanning J.P. Morgan, First New York, a hedge fund spinoff, National Bank of Canada, and back to First New York—gave him the deal-analysis expertise that underlies his trading advantage.
    - He went solo in 2015 after concluding that the firm's capital was no longer necessary given his minimal capital requirements, and that trading desk noise was impairing his laser focus.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-047"></span>**Bandazian's edge over algorithmic traders rests on the distinction between simple one-line headlines (which bots handle faster) and multi-contingent SEC filings requiring contextual interpretation—his 20 years of reading merger agreements and tracking the same deal players mean he knows what matters before bots are even trained on the deal's specifics.**
    - Whitestone REIT (WSR) in early 2024 exemplified this: he shorted the rally on a $14/share bid knowing that Maryland incorporation law allows companies to ignore activist bids, and that WSR had a documented history of doing so—information that took other traders 20–30 minutes to discover.
    - Foot Locker (FL) in May 2025: buried in the Dick's Sporting Goods acquisition press release was a clause that allowed FL to trade above the $24 bid price; he bought aggressively before other arbitrageurs recognized this detail.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-048"></span>**Bandazian's primary risk control is a time stop: he exits positions after three to four minutes if they do not behave as expected, regardless of whether his price stop has been hit, because each passing minute reduces his probability edge from ~98% (when he is the first to act) toward 50% (when the information has been widely disseminated).**
    - He thinks of his trades in 'hourglass terms'—the sand (his edge) flows from the top when he is early to the bottom as others catch up, and he must exit before most of the sand is gone.
    - His 85% win rate (profitable or breakeven) reflects this discipline: he accepts many small losses when positions don't immediately respond, preserving capital for the trades that do behave perfectly.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-049"></span>**Bandazian maintains a database of historical bids categorized by deal type (strategic vs. activist, merger vs. tender, regulatory jurisdiction, domicile), which he uses to estimate fair value for any announced transaction—allowing him to immediately assess whether a stock is trading above or below where it should be and to position accordingly.**
    - The Twitter/Musk 13D in 2022 showcased the approach: having pre-analyzed the situation (concluding Musk intended to buy, estimating a 10% discount to his eventual bid as fair value), he could buy within seconds of the 6 a.m. 13D filing and offer it at the day's high within minutes.
    - He operates primarily outside regular market hours—about two-thirds of his trading—because algorithms are less active during pre- and after-market sessions, preserving more of the edge that speed alone would eliminate during market hours.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-050"></span>**Bandazian works approximately 16 hours per day (3:45 a.m. to 8 p.m.) five days per week and serves as a volunteer firefighter, acknowledging that this intensity is likely unsustainable long-term and is actively developing a semi-systematic macro 'offsides positioning' strategy to eventually replace his screen-dependent current approach.**
    - He explicitly refutes the efficient market hypothesis (EMH): 11+ years of all-winning months with substantially above-market returns is statistically incompatible with the EMH's prediction that sustained outperformance is impossible without commensurate risk.
    - He limits his trading account to approximately $100,000 (far below what the firm offered) not due to capital constraints but because his edge dissipates quickly and most stocks he trades cannot absorb larger positions without moving against him.

## Epilogue

The epilogue uses a parable about a speculator who creates a heaven-emptying rumor and then follows the crowd he created—illustrating that even Market Wizards cannot fully escape the emotional impulses (FOMO, greed, overconfidence) that derail most traders—and concludes that the path to market success requires not merely superior strategy but the passion, dedication, and innate emotional makeup that only a small minority of people truly possess.

* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-051"></span>**Even the most skilled traders in the book made their worst trades when emotion overrode methodology—exemplified by Russo's Carvana short, which violated every one of his selection criteria and risk rules, resulting in a $50 million loss that could have been catastrophically worse had he not eventually reverted to discipline.**
    - The epilogue's speculator parable crystallizes the human condition: even after creating a false rumor, the speculator follows the crowd he generated, because FOMO operates below rational awareness.
    - Awareness of natural human emotional biases is necessary but not sufficient—traders also need methodologies and discipline to counteract those biases in real time.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-052"></span>**Two factors fundamentally limit who can become a Market Wizard: innate talent and emotional composition that most people do not possess, and the total commitment—passion, sacrifice of other interests, years of intensive work—that most people are unwilling or unable to make.**
    - Schwager candidly acknowledges he personally strikes out on both counts: he never committed to trading full-time and does not believe he possesses the emotional makeup for it, redirecting his market passion into writing the Market Wizards series instead.
    - The traders in the book uniformly convey a 'this-is-what-I-was-born-to-do' attitude—for them, the long hours of market research are not sacrifice but genuine enjoyment.
* <span id="market-wizards-the-next-generation-053"></span>**A love of trading is a necessary but not sufficient condition for Market Wizard performance: passion motivates the required hours of research and endurance through setbacks, but without the appropriate innate temperament, passion alone will not overcome the structural difficulties of beating well-resourced professional competition.**
    - "'Win or lose, everyone gets what they want out of the markets'—Ed Seykota's insight, which Schwager now interprets as meaning that his own wanting to be a writer rather than a trader was revealed by his actions, not his stated desires." —Ed Seykota
    - The book's lesson is not that anyone can become a Market Wizard but that the lessons from those who did—on methodology, risk management, psychology, and self-knowledge—can help any aspiring trader progress toward their own individually defined goals.
